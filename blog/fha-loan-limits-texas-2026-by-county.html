FHA Loan Limits in Texas by County (2026)

Loan ProgramsBy Bond Peter NjokuJuly 30, 20268 min read

I'm Bond Peter Njoku, a licensed Mortgage Loan Officer (NMLS #2670329), and one question I get from nearly every FHA buyer is some version of "how much house can I actually get with an FHA loan?" The answer depends partly on your income and debt, but it's also capped by a hard ceiling — the FHA loan limit for your specific county. Understanding that number before you start house hunting saves a lot of wasted showings on homes that would need a different loan program entirely.

How FHA Loan Limits Work

FHA loan limits are set annually by HUD based on median home prices, and they're calculated by metropolitan statistical area (MSA) rather than by individual county in most cases. Since Dallas, Tarrant, Collin, Denton, and Rockwall counties all fall within the Dallas-Fort Worth-Arlington MSA, they typically share the same FHA loan limit for 2026 — which is one reason the limit doesn't shift dramatically as you move between Garland, Plano, Fort Worth, or McKinney.

What the Limit Means for Your Purchase Price

The FHA loan limit is the maximum loan amount FHA will insure — it's not your maximum home price. With FHA's minimum 3.5% down payment, your maximum purchase price is actually somewhat higher than the loan limit itself, since a portion of the purchase is covered by your down payment. I run this calculation for every FHA buyer so you know your real ceiling, not just the raw loan limit number.

When You'd Need an Alternative to FHA

If the home you want exceeds what FHA financing supports even after factoring in your down payment, you have two main paths: increase your down payment enough to bring the loan amount under the FHA cap, or move to a conventional loan, which has its own separate and typically higher limit set by Fannie Mae and Freddie Mac. For homes well above both limits, a jumbo loan becomes the relevant option — call or text me at 469-545-7180 and I'll walk you through how jumbo financing works for higher-priced DFW purchases.

Comparing FHA to Conventional Limits

Conventional conforming loan limits are generally set higher than FHA limits in most markets, which is one reason move-up buyers in higher-priced areas like Collin County and southern Denton County often end up using conventional financing instead of FHA once their target price climbs. I compare both side by side for every client — read my broader FHA vs. conventional guide for the full breakdown of when each program makes more sense.
