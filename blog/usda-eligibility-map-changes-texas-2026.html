What Happens If My Area Loses USDA Eligibility Before I Close in Texas?

USDA LoansBy Bond Peter NjokuAugust 21, 20267 min read

I’m Bond Peter Njoku (NMLS #2670329), and this is the USDA question I get most often from buyers in fast-growing DFW suburbs: “What if this area stops qualifying for USDA before we close?” It’s a real concern — USDA maps update, DFW is growing, and buyers in transitional areas need to understand exactly when they’re protected and when they’re not.

For the full list of DFW cities that still qualify for USDA loans in 2026, see my USDA eligible cities guide for DFW — I’ve mapped every eligible zone by county so you can check your target area before making an offer.

Why do USDA eligibility maps change?

USDA’s Rural Development program is restricted to areas the Census Bureau classifies as “rural” — generally places with populations under 35,000 that are not contiguous with a large urban area. As DFW grows outward and formerly rural suburbs reach population thresholds, USDA reclassifies them as ineligible. Map updates typically happen on a roughly annual schedule, often in the fall, though USDA can revise eligibility outside the standard cycle as well.

Communities most at risk in the DFW area are places like Forney, Royse City, and outer Kaufman County — each has grown substantially over the last five years. They’re still eligible as of the most recent map update, but any of them could be reclassified in a future cycle.

When is your loan protected if the map changes mid-transaction?

USDA has a specific grandfather rule: if your loan has received a Conditional Commitment (CC) from USDA before the date the map change goes into effect, your loan can proceed to closing even if the area becomes ineligible on the new map. A Conditional Commitment is not the same as a pre-approval or a Loan Estimate — it’s the formal underwriting approval issued by USDA’s Guaranteed Underwriting System (GUS) after full underwriting review.

If you are still in early contract stages — pre-approval letter only, no CC yet — and the map changes before your loan reaches underwriting, you are not protected. The loan would need to switch to a different program (FHA or Conventional) or the purchase would need to be restructured.

What is the timeline to get a Conditional Commitment?

A typical USDA loan timeline from contract to CC runs three to five weeks, though it can be faster with a strong file and a lender who processes USDA regularly. The critical path is: gather documentation (pay stubs, W-2s, tax returns, bank statements) → underwriting submission → GUS approval → USDA issues CC. If a map update is expected — USDA generally announces upcoming changes on its website — we push hard to get documentation in hand before the effective date.

What to do if you’re buying in a borderline USDA zone

Four things that reduce your risk:

  • Move quickly to underwriting. A pre-approval isn’t protection — a Conditional Commitment is. The faster you clear underwriting, the more protected you are from a map change.
  • Have a backup program identified. I always run FHA and Conventional numbers alongside USDA for borderline-area buyers. If USDA closes off, you want to know the FHA payment and down payment before you’re in crisis mode at week four of escrow.
  • Check USDA’s scheduled update announcements. USDA publishes advance notice of map changes. Your lender should be monitoring these — I do.
  • Understand the risk before you lock a rate. In a borderline zone, locking a USDA rate on day one and assuming you’ll get to CC before any map change is a planning failure. Know the odds.

What happens to my earnest money if USDA eligibility changes before I close?

It depends on your contract language. Most Texas residential contracts (the TREC One to Four Family Residential Contract) include a financing contingency — if the buyer can’t obtain the specified financing, the earnest money is returnable. However, if you’ve already waived the financing contingency or are past the option period, an unexpected USDA disqualification can create risk. This is exactly why I tell borderline-area buyers to keep the option period open until we have at minimum a GUS approval in hand.

What happens to my USDA loan if the area loses eligibility while I’m in contract?

If USDA has already issued a Conditional Commitment on your loan before the eligibility change takes effect, your loan is protected and can close normally. If you only have a pre-approval and the map changes before underwriting approval, the loan cannot proceed as USDA and would need to switch programs. What is a USDA Conditional Commitment and why does it matter?

A Conditional Commitment is the formal underwriting approval issued by USDA after your complete file has been reviewed in the Guaranteed Underwriting System (GUS). It’s the document that protects your loan from a map change — a pre-approval letter or Loan Estimate alone does not provide that protection.
