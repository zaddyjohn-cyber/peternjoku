Texas Property Tax Deduction 2026: What the $40,400 SALT Cap and PMI Deduction Mean for DFW Homeowners

Tax PlanningBy Bond Peter NjokuOctober 14, 20268 min read

Texas has no state income tax, so for most DFW homeowners the federal SALT (state and local tax) deduction comes down to property taxes plus sales tax. In 2026 the cap on that deduction is $40,400, four times the old $10,000 limit. Mortgage insurance premiums are deductible again starting in 2026. Together those changes make itemizing worthwhile for more Texas homeowners than any year since 2017. I'm Bond Peter Njoku (NMLS #2670329), a mortgage loan officer in Garland. I'm not a CPA, but I run these numbers with buyers every week because they change the true cost of a home. Here is how the 2026 rules apply in Dallas, Collin, Denton, Tarrant and Rockwall counties.

Can I deduct property taxes in Texas in 2026?

Yes, if you itemize. Property taxes on your home go on Schedule A as part of the SALT deduction. Under the 2025 tax law (the One Big Beautiful Bill Act), the SALT rules for 2026 are:

  • Cap: $40,400 per return ($20,200 married filing separately), up from $40,000 for 2025. It rises 1% a year: $40,804 in 2027.
  • Income phase-down: the cap shrinks by 30 cents for every dollar of modified AGI over $505,000, but never below $10,000.
  • Sunset: the cap is scheduled to drop back to $10,000 starting with the 2030 tax year.
  • Texas detail: you choose between deducting state income tax or state and local sales tax. Texans have no income tax, so you deduct sales tax (the IRS table amount, or actual receipts if you bought a car or boat). That's on top of property taxes, all under the one SALT cap.

For nearly every DFW homeowner the $40,400 cap isn't the limit you'll hit. A Texas home generally needs more than $35,000 a year in property taxes, about a $1.75 million home at 2%, before the new cap matters. What it changed is the old $10,000 cap, which used to stop deductions on homes as low as about $550,000 in Collin County once sales tax was added.

Should I itemize or take the standard deduction in 2026?

This is the question that decides whether any of it matters to you. The 2026 standard deduction is $32,200 for married filing jointly and $16,100 for single filers. Itemizing only helps by the amount your itemized total exceeds those figures. Is PMI or FHA mortgage insurance tax deductible in 2026?

Yes, again. The 2025 law permanently treats mortgage insurance premiums as deductible mortgage interest starting with the 2026 tax year. That covers conventional PMI, FHA MIP, the VA funding fee and the USDA guarantee fee. It isn't retroactive to 2022–2025, and rental properties don't qualify. The old version of this deduction phased out between $100,000 and $109,000 of AGI. Confirm the current income limits in IRS Publication 936 or with your tax preparer, because that phase-out decides whether a moderate-income FHA buyer gets any benefit.

For the Garland FHA buyer above, about $1,700 of annual MIP could push the itemized total from $609 below the standard deduction to about $1,100 above it. That's a small saving, but it's real if the household income is under the phase-out.

What is the mortgage interest deduction limit in 2026?

The 2025 law made the $750,000 acquisition-debt limit permanent for loans taken out after December 15, 2017. Mortgages from before that date keep the $1 million limit. Interest on a home equity loan or HELOC is deductible only if you used the money to buy, build or substantially improve the home. A cash-out used to pay off credit cards doesn't qualify. That affects how I structure a Texas 50(a)(6) cash-out refinance for clients who care about the deduction.

The $750,000 limit sits close to the 2026 conforming loan limit of $832,750. A buyer in Prosper or Southlake with a $800,000 loan can deduct interest on only about 94% of it.

Can I pay my 2026 Texas property taxes before December 31 to deduct them this year?

Often, yes. Property taxes are deductible in the year they're paid, and the 2026 Texas tax bills issued this fall aren't due until January 31, 2027. That gives you a choice:

  • No escrow: pay by December 31 to deduct on your 2026 return, or in January to deduct in 2027. If you itemize in 2026 but expect to take the standard deduction in 2027, paying in December is usually better.
  • With escrow: your servicer decides when to pay, and many Texas servicers pay in December, some in January. Your Form 1098 shows what was actually paid in the year. If the timing matters to you, call your servicer in early November and ask when it plans to pay.
  • Only billed (assessed) taxes count. You can't prepay taxes that haven't been assessed yet. In Dallas County, 2026 bills are delayed until after the November 3 Prop A election, so homeowners there will have a shorter window to pay before December 31.

Do I lose the deduction in the year I buy?

The first year is complicated, and it's where I see buyers get confused. At a Texas closing, the seller credits you for their share of the current year's taxes, and you (or your escrow account) pay the whole bill later. The IRS splits the deduction by the days each party owned the home, not by who wrote the check. Your mortgage interest for the first year is usually only a few months' worth, too. Most buyers who close in the fall end up taking the standard deduction for that year and itemizing from the first full year. Give your tax preparer the final Closing Disclosure. It shows the proration and any points you paid, and points on a purchase are generally deductible in the year you close.

Here's a composite case. A Plano couple I worked with this spring bought a $520,000 home with 15% down. They expected to itemize in 2026 but closed on April 30, so they had only eight months of interest and a prorated share of taxes. That put them about $2,000 under the standard deduction for 2026. Their first full year, 2027, will clear it by about $6,000. We picked a loan that suited their budget, not one built around the deduction, and I'd recommend the same to anyone.

How should taxes factor into how much house I buy?

When I pre-approve you, I qualify you on the full monthly payment, not on an after-tax estimate. A deduction lowers your tax bill once a year (or your withholding, if you adjust your W-4). It doesn't lower the PITI the lender uses for your debt-to-income ratio. Texas property taxes also cost more each month than any deduction gives back.
