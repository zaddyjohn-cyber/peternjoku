Defaulted Student Loans Blocking Your FHA Loan? How to Clear CAIVRS and Buy a Home in DFW (2026)

Student LoansBy Bond Peter NjokuOctober 14, 20268 min read

The Education Department restarted collections on defaulted federal student loans in 2025 and began wage garnishment in 2026, so I'm seeing more DFW buyers whose FHA pre-approval stops at a check most of them had never heard of: CAIVRS. A defaulted federal student loan doesn't only lower your credit score. It can make you ineligible for FHA, VA and USDA financing until the default is resolved. I'm Bond Peter Njoku (NMLS #2670329), a Garland-based loan officer, and this post covers how CAIVRS works, the three ways to clear it, how long each takes, and how to buy a home in Mesquite, Garland or Dallas as soon as possible afterward.

What is CAIVRS and why did it block my FHA loan?

CAIVRS (Credit Alert Verification Reporting System) is a HUD-run federal database of people who are delinquent or in default on federal debt: defaulted federal student loans, SBA loans, FHA or VA loans that went to claim, and similar debts. FHA, VA and USDA lenders are required to check it on every borrower. A "hit" means you can't get a new federally backed mortgage until the agency that reported you clears it.

What surprises people:

  • CAIVRS isn't on your credit report. Only lenders and agencies can search it, so most people find out from their loan officer.
  • A good credit score doesn't override it. A 700 score with a CAIVRS hit still can't get an FHA loan.
  • FHA can't remove you. Only the reporting agency can, which for student loans is the Department of Education's Default Resolution Group (1-800-621-3115).
  • The pandemic-era relief has ended. The Fresh Start program, which temporarily cleared many student loan defaults from CAIVRS, closed on October 2, 2024. If you didn't resolve the default then, it's back on the list.

Rehabilitation can generally be used only once per loan, so don't start it and then stop. If garnishment has already started, it continues until you've made five rehabilitation payments, which is another reason not to wait.

After CAIVRS clears, how is my student loan counted in DTI?

Clearing CAIVRS gets you eligible, but the loan still counts against your debt-to-income ratio. On FHA, the lender uses the payment on your credit report. If that shows $0, which is common right after consolidation into an income-driven plan, FHA uses 0.5% of the outstanding balance. On a $34,000 balance that's $170 a month, whatever your actual payment is. The new Repayment Assistance Plan (RAP), which started July 1, 2026, changes the reported payment for many borrowers. I explain how in RAP and your mortgage DTI.

What does this look like for a real DFW buyer?

Here is a composite of a Mesquite buyer I worked with this year. A 31-year-old teacher's aide earning $58,000, with a 628 credit score that had been recovering. She'd been renting for six years and had $9,000 saved, and was looking at $260,000 homes. Her FHA file stopped at a CAIVRS hit from a $34,000 federal student loan that had defaulted in 2022. She hadn't known about it, because her score had partly recovered.

  • Day 1: She called the Default Resolution Group and applied for Direct Consolidation into an income-driven plan.
  • About week 7: The consolidation loan disbursed and the old loans showed "paid by consolidation."
  • About week 11: I reran CAIVRS and she was clear. The new loan showed a $0 IDR payment, so FHA counted 0.5% × $34,000 = $170/mo.
  • The numbers: $260,000 purchase, 3.5% down ($9,100). We used down payment assistance so she kept her savings for closing costs. Base loan $250,900 plus UFMIP comes to about $255,291, which is about $1,768 P&I at 7.40%. Add about $433 Dallas County taxes, $250 insurance and $115 monthly MIP for a PITI of about $2,566.
  • DTI: $2,566 + $170 student loan + $95 card minimum = $2,831 on $4,833 monthly income = 58.6%, too high. She switched to a $230,000 townhome (PITI about $2,280) and closed at about a 52.6% DTI with an approve/eligible finding. That's at the top of what FHA's automated underwriting will accept.

The whole process, from the CAIVRS hit to the keys, took about four months. If she had chosen rehabilitation, it would have taken nine to ten months, but she'd have a cleaner credit history. Neither choice is wrong. It depends on how soon you need to move and whether you're about to sign another lease.

Can I buy a house while my wages are being garnished?

Not with FHA, VA or USDA. Garnishment means you're in default, and that means a CAIVRS hit. For a conventional loan, the garnishment shows up on your pay stubs, the lender has to count it, and your score is probably well below 620. The quickest route is to stop the garnishment by resolving the default, then rebuild from there. If you're in Garland or Mesquite and not sure where you stand, call me before you call a builder or a realtor. I can run a CAIVRS check as part of a pre-approval so there are no surprises after you're under contract.

Step-by-step: from CAIVRS hit to closing

  1. Log into StudentAid.gov and confirm which loans are in default and who holds them.
  2. Call the Default Resolution Group (1-800-621-3115) and choose consolidation, rehabilitation or payoff.
  3. Keep every confirmation letter. Underwriters may ask for proof the default is resolved.
  4. Watch your credit report until the accounts update ("paid by consolidation" or rehabilitated).
  5. Ask your loan officer to rerun CAIVRS before you shop. Don't make an offer on a hit.
  6. Get fully pre-approved, with the student loan payment counted the way your loan program requires.

FHA is still the most forgiving program for buyers rebuilding credit. Once CAIVRS is clear, see my FHA loan page and the Mesquite buyer guide for local numbers.
