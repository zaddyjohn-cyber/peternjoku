Can I Buy a House with an ITIN Number in Texas?

ITIN LoansBy Bond Peter NjokuSeptember 12, 20269 min read

I'm Bond Peter Njoku (NMLS #2670329), and I get this question often from buyers across Garland, Dallas, and the wider DFW metro: "I don't have a Social Security number — can I still buy a house?" The answer is yes. ITIN mortgage loans exist specifically for this situation, and I've helped clients close on homes across Texas using nothing but their Individual Taxpayer Identification Number. Here's exactly how it works, what it costs, and what to expect.

What Loan Options Are Available for ITIN Borrowers in Texas?

ITIN loans are a specialty non-QM (non-qualified mortgage) product, which means they don't meet the standard Fannie Mae/Freddie Mac or FHA guidelines that require a Social Security number. They're offered by a smaller pool of lenders than conventional or FHA loans, but they're a real, established program — not an obscure workaround.

What Documents Do I Need for an ITIN Mortgage?

  • ITIN assignment letter from the IRS. This is the core requirement that replaces a Social Security number.
  • Valid photo ID. A passport from your country of origin or a consular ID card (matrícula consular) is standard.
  • Two years of income and residence history. Lenders want to see stability, whether you're a W-2 employee, self-employed, or paid in cash with tax returns filed under your ITIN.
  • Tax returns filed under your ITIN, or 12-24 months of bank statements if your lender offers a bank-statement ITIN program.
  • Proof of down payment funds. Since ITIN loans typically require 10-20% down, you'll need to document where that money is coming from.

How Much Down Payment Do I Need?

Most ITIN programs in Texas require 10% to 20% down, meaningfully more than the 3-3.5% minimum on conventional and FHA loans. Where you land in that range depends on your income documentation strength, credit history (or alternative credit history), and the specific lender's program. Borrowers with strong, well-documented income and a longer U.S. residence history tend to land closer to 10%; thinner files often need closer to 20%.

What If I Don't Have a Credit Score?

This is one of the most common concerns I hear, and it's not a dealbreaker. Many ITIN borrowers haven't built a traditional FICO score, so lenders build what's called an alternative credit history instead — using 12 to 24 months of on-time rent payments, utility bills, phone bills, insurance payments, and other recurring obligations to demonstrate a track record of paying on time. I've had clients qualify for ITIN loans with strong alternative credit files even though a credit bureau had never issued them a FICO score at all.

How Much Will My Payment Be on an ITIN Loan?

Let's run real numbers. Say you're buying a $280,000 home in Garland with 15% down ($42,000), leaving a loan amount of $238,000. ITIN loan rates typically run 0.5-1.5% above conventional — let's use 7.75% over 30 years. Using M = P[r(1+r)^n] / [(1+r)^n − 1], your principal and interest payment comes to approximately $1,706/month. Adding an estimated $467/month for Dallas County property taxes (roughly 2.0% effective rate) and $150/month for homeowners insurance brings your total PITI to about $2,323/month.

Here's a composite scenario built from clients I've worked with. A family in Dallas had been in the U.S. for six years, filing taxes under an ITIN the entire time, but had never had an SSN or a traditional credit score. They closed in just over 45 days, comparable to a typical conventional timeline.
