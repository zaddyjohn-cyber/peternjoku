Yes, You Can Stack Down Payment Assistance With a Conventional Loan in Texas

ConventionalBy Bond Peter NjokuSeptember 17, 20268 min read

Almost every Texas buyer I talk to assumes down payment assistance is an FHA-only tool. It isn't. I'm Bond Peter Njoku (NMLS #2670329), and I regularly stack TSAHC and TDHCA assistance onto conventional loans for DFW buyers who have the credit score to qualify conventional but not quite enough cash for a down payment.

Which down payment assistance programs work with a conventional loan in Texas?

ProgramAmountTypeWorks with conventional?
TSAHC Home Sweet Texas3–5% of loan amountGrant — never repaidYes
TDHCA My First Texas HomeUp to 5%Deferred second lien, forgiven over timeYes
Dallas DHAPUp to $60,000Forgivable over 5 yearsFHA-focused — confirm with program before assuming conventional eligibility
Fort Worth HAPUp to $25,000Forgivable over 5 yearsFHA-focused — confirm with program before assuming conventional eligibility

The two statewide programs — TSAHC and TDHCA — are the ones I pair with conventional financing most often, since they're built to be loan-type flexible rather than FHA-specific. See the full conventional loan requirements in Texas for how these interact with the 620 credit score floor and DTI guidelines.

Do I need to be a first-time buyer to use DPA on a conventional loan?

It depends on the program. TSAHC's Home Sweet Texas does not require first-time-buyer status, which is exactly why it's the more flexible pairing for conventional buyers who already own a home. TDHCA's My First Texas Home does require first-time-buyer status, with an exception for eligible veterans regardless of prior ownership.

How much does DPA actually add to my buying power?

On a $320,000 conventional purchase with 5% down, that down payment is $16,000. A TSAHC grant at 4% of the loan amount adds roughly $12,160 toward that — meaning a buyer who might have needed to save for another year can often close with a fraction of the cash they expected to need, sometimes covering most of the down payment plus a meaningful chunk of closing costs.

Does stacking DPA change my conventional loan's rate or terms?

Usually the rate is set slightly differently than a standalone conventional loan without assistance, since DPA-paired loans run through their own investor pricing. It's still frequently the stronger net outcome once you weigh the assistance dollars against that adjustment — but I always run the comparison both ways so you can see exactly what you're trading.

Named example: a Garland buyer who stacked TSAHC with conventional

I worked with a Garland buyer with a 665 credit score and steady W-2 income who qualified conventional but only had about $6,000 saved — not enough for 5% down plus closing costs on her $275,000 target home. We paired her conventional loan with a TSAHC Home Sweet Texas grant at 4%, adding roughly $10,600 toward her down payment and closing costs. She closed with under $2,000 out of pocket, on a conventional loan she wouldn't have otherwise been able to afford to close on for another 8-10 months of saving.
