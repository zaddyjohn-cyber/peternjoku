How to Check if Your Texas Address Qualifies for a USDA Loan in 2026

USDA LoansBy Bond Peter NjokuAugust 21, 20267 min read

I’m Bond Peter Njoku (NMLS #2670329), and the single most common USDA mistake I see is buyers assuming an entire city is eligible or ineligible. USDA eligibility is decided address by address — two properties a block apart can get opposite answers from the same map. Before you fall in love with any home that depends on USDA financing, here’s exactly how to confirm the property qualifies.

For the full list of DFW cities that still qualify for USDA loans in 2026, see my USDA eligible cities guide for DFW — I’ve mapped every eligible zone by county so you can check your target area before making an offer.

Step 1: Go to the official USDA eligibility website

The only authoritative source is eligibility.sc.egov.usda.gov. It’s the USDA’s own system and it’s what underwriting uses. Third-party tools and agent opinions don’t substitute for this — I’ve seen Zillow listings labeled “USDA eligible” for properties that clearly aren’t, and vice versa.

Step 2: Click “Property Eligibility” and enter the full address

From the site’s home screen, select Property Eligibility from the menu. Type the complete address — including the street number, street name, city, and ZIP code. Hit Enter and the map will zoom to the parcel. If the map highlights the area in green or white (unshaded), the property is in an eligible zone. If it highlights in orange or shows “ineligible”, USDA financing cannot be used at that address, period.

Step 3: Screenshot the result before you make an offer

Take a screenshot of the map result with the address confirmation visible. This is useful in two ways: it confirms you’re looking at the right parcel (not an adjacent ineligible lot), and it gives your loan officer a starting reference before pulling the formal check in the loan origination system. I always verify independently in my system — but a buyer who has already done this step comes into the conversation knowing their situation.

What does “borderline” mean and how do I handle it?

Some properties sit right on the edge of an eligible zone — the parcel straddles the boundary, or the map resolution makes it unclear which side a home is on. When this happens:

  • Enter the exact parcel number or APN from the county appraisal district website instead of the street address — it’s more precise than the address lookup.
  • Call your USDA-approved lender (that’s me — 469-545-7180) to run a formal eligibility check in the GUS system, which uses a higher-resolution parcel dataset than the public map.
  • Do not enter contract on a borderline property assuming it’s eligible until the formal check is done. Backing out of a contract because of USDA eligibility is avoidable.

Which DFW areas are currently USDA-eligible in 2026?

The short list of confirmed USDA-eligible communities in the DFW area includes Forney, Terrell, Royse City, Lavon, Nevada, Josephine, Caddo Mills, Fate, Quinlan, and parts of outlying Rockwall County. Communities on the boundary — where USDA eligibility exists for some but not all addresses — include portions of Wylie, Sachse, and Kaufman County’s outer edges. Established urban areas including Dallas, Garland, Plano, Frisco, McKinney, and Allen are not eligible, though buyers in those cities can often use FHA or Conventional with down payment assistance instead.

Do I need to check both address eligibility AND income eligibility?

Yes — they’re separate hurdles, and both must clear. The property address must sit inside a USDA-eligible zone, AND your household income must fall below your county’s 2026 limit (approximately $112,450 for 1–4 person households, $148,450 for 5–8 person households in DFW counties). I run both checks at the same time so you have a complete yes or no before you’re emotionally invested in a specific property.

Frequently Asked Questions

How do I check if a specific address is USDA eligible in Texas?

Go to eligibility.sc.egov.usda.gov, click “Property Eligibility,” enter the full address, and look at whether the map highlights the parcel in green/white (eligible) or orange (ineligible). Is an entire city either USDA eligible or ineligible?

No — eligibility is decided by property address, not by city. Two homes on the same street can get different answers if the boundary runs between them. Never assume eligibility from a city name alone. I’m Bond Peter Njoku (NMLS #2670329) — call or text 469-545-7180 and I’ll check the specific address for you.

What if the USDA map shows my property is borderline?

Enter the county APN (parcel number) instead of the street address for a more precise result. Then contact a USDA-approved lender to run the formal check in the GUS system, which uses higher-resolution parcel data. Don’t enter contract on a borderline property without that confirmation. Does USDA eligibility change, and if so, how often?

Yes — USDA updates its eligibility maps periodically, typically annually, as the Census Bureau reclassifies areas based on population growth. Growing DFW suburbs are at higher risk of losing eligibility than stable rural areas. Areas in transition are the most time-sensitive for buyers who want to use USDA financing.
