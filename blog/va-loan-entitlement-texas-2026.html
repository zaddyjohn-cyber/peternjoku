VA Loan Entitlement Texas 2026 — Full vs. Reduced, Explained

VeteransBy Bond Peter NjokuAugust 4, 20268 min read

I'm Bond Peter Njoku, and "entitlement" is the single most misunderstood word in the VA loan process. It's the term for how much the VA guarantees a lender on your behalf, and it's what allows Texas veterans to buy with $0 down and no monthly mortgage insurance. But most veterans I talk to don't know how much entitlement they actually have, whether it's "full" or "reduced," or that it can be restored and reused. Here's the complete picture for 2026.

Full Entitlement vs. Reduced Entitlement

Entitlement TypeLoan LimitDown Payment
Full EntitlementNone — borrow what you qualify for$0
Reduced EntitlementVaries by remaining guaranty and countyMay require down payment above limit

Figures as of August 2026. Actual entitlement amounts are calculated individually via your Certificate of Eligibility.

What "Full Entitlement" Actually Means

Most Texas veterans who've never used a VA loan — or who have paid one off entirely and restored their entitlement — have full entitlement. In plain terms, this means there is no VA loan limit at all. You can borrow whatever amount you qualify for based on income and credit, and still put $0 down, whether you're buying a $250,000 home in Mesquite or a $650,000 home in Frisco.

When Entitlement Becomes "Reduced"

Reduced entitlement typically happens in two scenarios: you still have an active VA loan on another property, or a prior VA loan went to foreclosure and the VA had to pay out on the guaranty. In these cases, your remaining entitlement may not cover 25% of a new loan amount with $0 down, which means you could need a down payment on the portion above your entitlement. This is one of the most common surprises I walk Texas veterans through when they're buying a second property.

Restoring Your Entitlement After Selling

If you sell a home that was financed with a VA loan and pay off the mortgage in full, your entitlement is restored and you can use it again as if it were your first time. I've helped Texas veterans go through this cycle two and three times over the course of a career — buying, selling, restoring, and buying again, each time with $0 down.

Can You Have Two VA Loans at the Same Time?

Yes, in many cases. If you have remaining entitlement after your current VA loan, you can use it toward a second property without selling the first — this comes up often with Texas veterans who get PCS orders or relocate for work and want to keep their existing home as a rental. The math on how much you can borrow with $0 down on the second property depends entirely on your remaining entitlement, which is why I pull your COE and run the numbers before you make an offer.
