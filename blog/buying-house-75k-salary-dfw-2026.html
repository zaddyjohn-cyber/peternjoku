Can You Buy a House on $75,000 a Year in Dallas-Fort Worth in 2026?

First-Time BuyersBy Bond Peter NjokuOctober 22, 20266 min read

I'm Bond Peter Njoku, a mortgage loan officer in Garland, and this week a statistic caught my attention: only about 10.2% of Dallas-Fort Worth listings are affordable to a household earning around $75,000 a year, according to a 2026 housing-mismatch report from Realtor.com and the National Association of Realtors (as reported by Hoodline on October 21). Nationally, first-time buyers are down to a record-low 21% of purchases.

Those numbers are discouraging, but they don't mean a $75K buyer can't buy here. They mean the buyer needs to know their real number and where to shop. Most national "$75K salary" articles assume low property taxes and lower rates than we have today. Here's the DFW version, at today's 7.40% rate (Freddie Mac, mid-October 2026) with Texas tax rates.

How much house can I afford on $75,000 in DFW?

A $75,000 salary is about $6,250 a month before taxes. Depending on which rule you use, you get three different answers. All three below assume an FHA loan with 3.5% down, Dallas County's ~2.0% tax rate, ~$170 a month for insurance and FHA mortgage insurance:

Budget ruleMax total housing paymentApprox. max priceWho this fits
Conservative: 28% of gross for housing$1,750~$177,000Buyers who want a lot of breathing room
Standard: 43% total DTI with $300 of other debt$2,387~$249,000Most of the approvals I write
Stretch: ~50% DTI on FHA with strong credit and reserves$2,825~$298,000Only with an automated approval and a tight budget

Your realistic range on $75,000 is about $230,000 to $260,000, with more room if you have no car payment or student loans. Every $100 of monthly debt you pay off adds roughly $10,000 in buying power at today's rates.

At $250,000 with 3.5% down, you'd need $8,750 for the down payment plus closing costs of about 2–3% ($5,000–$7,500). Sellers in today's DFW market are often willing to cover part of that.

Does the county I buy in change what I can afford?

It does. Texas has no state income tax but high property taxes, and they vary by county. With the same $2,387 monthly budget:

Collin (Plano, McKinney, Allen)~1.5%~$261,000, though few homes are listed there

A lower tax rate doesn't help much if homes there cost much more. Collin County's rate is lower, but the typical home in Plano or McKinney is well over $400,000. For a $75,000 income, the best fits in DFW are usually Mesquite, Terrell, parts of Garland, east and south Fort Worth, parts of Arlington, and Forney for USDA-eligible buyers. To understand how taxes affect your approval, see my property tax and DTI post.

Can down payment assistance help on a $75,000 income?

Yes, and at this income most buyers qualify. The 2026 limits for TSAHC and TDHCA programs are about $97,200 in Dallas and Tarrant counties and $119,700 in Collin, Denton and Rockwall counties, so a $75,000 household is well under both.

  • TSAHC Home Sweet Texas: generally 3–5% of the loan as a grant or forgivable second lien. On a $245,472 FHA loan, 5% is about $12,270, enough for the full 3.5% down payment plus a few thousand toward closing costs.
  • TDHCA My First Texas Home: up to 5% as a deferred second lien for first-time buyers and veterans.
  • City of Dallas DHAP (inside Dallas city limits) and Fort Worth HAP have their own income limits and can be larger.

DPA loans usually come with a slightly higher rate, so I compare the payment with and without it. For most $75K buyers, though, DPA is the difference between buying in 2026 and saving for another three years. My DPA page lists all the programs.

A Mesquite example: $74,000 and a car payment

This year I worked with a single buyer in her late twenties, a dental office manager earning about $74,000, with a 640 credit score and a $410 car payment. With the car payment, her approval topped out around $238,000. She found a three-bedroom 1980s home in Mesquite for $246,000. We had two choices: pay down the car loan or find a cheaper house.

The car loan had about $4,800 left, so she used part of her savings to pay it off entirely, which removed the $410 payment from her DTI, and used TSAHC assistance for the down payment instead of her own cash. Her FHA approval rose to about $255,000. The seller covered $5,500 in closing costs, and she closed with about $3,000 of her own money and a payment around $2,350 a month, roughly $250 more than her rent.

How do I get started on $75,000?

  1. Get pre-approved with your real debts so you know your ceiling. It's free and won't commit you to anything.
  2. Check DPA eligibility at the same time.
  3. Shop in the $220K–$260K range in the cities above, and ask your agent to filter for homes sellers have already reduced.
