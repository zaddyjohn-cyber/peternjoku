Moving to Fort Worth TX — Mortgage Guide for Relocating Buyers (2026)

RelocationBy Bond Peter NjokuJuly 26, 20269 min read

I'm Bond Peter Njoku (NMLS #2670329), and Fort Worth is one of the fastest-growing cities I work in across North Texas. It draws relocating buyers who want big-city culture — world-class museums, a historic Stockyards district, and a walkable downtown — at a price point that's noticeably friendlier than Dallas or Collin County. If you're moving to Fort Worth, this guide covers price ranges, neighborhoods, loan programs, and how to get pre-approved before you ever set foot in Texas.

Why People Are Moving to Fort Worth

  • Affordability — Fort Worth remains one of the most affordable major cities in the Dallas-Fort Worth metro, with a lower median home price than Dallas, Plano, or Frisco
  • Major employers — Lockheed Martin, American Airlines, BNSF Railway, Bell Textron, and Cook Children's Health Care System all have a major presence here
  • Naval Air Station JRB Fort Worth — a major military installation that draws active-duty and veteran families relocating on PCS orders
  • Cultural District — home to the Kimbell Art Museum, Modern Art Museum of Fort Worth, and Amon Carter Museum, all within a few blocks
  • Historic Stockyards and Sundance Square — genuine Texas character with restaurants, live music, and walkable entertainment
  • No state income tax — a major draw for buyers relocating from California, Illinois, or the Northeast

Loan Programs for Fort Worth Buyers

Conventional (Most Common in Fort Worth)

The 2026 conforming loan limit for Tarrant County is $806,500. With 620+ credit and 3%–20% down, conventional works well across Fort Worth's full price range, from starter homes to the newer construction in Alliance. PMI is required with less than 20% down but cancels automatically at 80% loan-to-value. Conventional Loan Fort Worth details →

FHA Loans

The 2026 FHA loan limit for Tarrant County is $498,257. FHA requires just 3.5% down with a 580+ credit score, or 10% down with a 500–579 score, and covers most of Fort Worth's entry and mid-range resale market. FHA Loan Fort Worth details →

VA Loans — Zero Down for Veterans and Active Duty

With Naval Air Station JRB Fort Worth right in the city, I work with a steady stream of veterans and active-duty families relocating here on PCS orders. Veterans with full entitlement can purchase with zero down payment and no loan cap. I'm experienced with VA loans throughout Tarrant County and can walk you through the COE process and funding fee structure. VA Loan details →

Fort Worth Neighborhoods at a Glance

Alliance / North Fort Worth

Fort Worth's fastest-growing corridor, anchored by the Alliance master-planned development and major logistics employers. New construction dominates, typically $350,000–$550,000.

Near Cultural District / TCU Area

Established, walkable neighborhoods close to the museums and Texas Christian University. A mix of older character homes and updated resale, generally $350,000–$550,000.

Southwest / Chisholm Trail Ranch

Newer master-planned communities along the Chisholm Trail Parkway, popular with families wanting new construction and shorter commutes to south Fort Worth employers. Typically $300,000–$450,000.

Getting Pre-Approved When You're Out of State

Many Fort Worth buyers I work with are relocating for jobs at Lockheed Martin, American Airlines, or on PCS orders to NAS JRB Fort Worth, and buying remotely is completely normal in today's market. The process:

  1. Initial call — 15–20 minutes to discuss your income, credit range, down payment, and timeline
  2. Document upload — 2 years W-2s, 30 days paystubs, 2 months bank statements, and ID via secure link
  3. Credit pull — with your authorization, I pull a tri-merge credit report and confirm your qualifying score
  4. Pre-approval letter issued — usually within 24–48 business hours, usable with any Texas Realtor
  5. Remote offers — your Realtor handles tours and offer submission, often signed entirely via DocuSign

Out-of-state income — W-2, self-employed, military BAH, or remote work — is fully acceptable. Call or text me at 469-545-7180 to start.

Property Taxes in Fort Worth

Fort Worth (Tarrant County) property tax rates run approximately 2.1%–2.6% of assessed value annually, depending on the specific school district and municipal utility district. On a $380,000 home, that's roughly $7,980–$9,880/year, or about $665–$823/month added through escrow. File for the Texas Homestead Exemption at tad.org (Tarrant Appraisal District) immediately after closing.
