Fannie Mae HomePath in DFW Texas 2026: Buy a Foreclosure with 3% Down and Closing Cost Help

Conventional LoansBy Bond Peter NjokuOctober 15, 20266 min read

Most buyers in DFW have never heard of Fannie Mae HomePath — but if you're a first-time buyer with limited cash for closing costs, it could be the strongest path to ownership you haven't considered. HomePath is Fannie Mae's program for selling foreclosed homes it owns (called REO or Real Estate Owned properties), and it comes with a benefit that's genuinely hard to find elsewhere: up to 3% of the purchase price in closing cost assistance, available to first-time buyers who complete a short online course before making an offer.

I'm Bond Peter Njoku, a licensed Mortgage Loan Officer (NMLS #2670329) based in Garland and serving buyers across Dallas-Fort Worth. I help buyers who want to use every legitimate dollar of assistance available to them — and HomePath is one of those programs that most loan officers don't bring up unless you ask. Here's what you need to know.

What is Fannie Mae HomePath?

When a homeowner defaults on a Fannie Mae-backed mortgage and the property goes through foreclosure, Fannie Mae takes ownership of the home and lists it for sale on homepath.com. These properties are sold directly through local real estate agents just like any other listing, but with some structural advantages for owner-occupant buyers:

  • 30-Day First Look period: For the first 30 days a property is listed on HomePath, only owner-occupants, public entities, and nonprofits can submit offers. Investors are locked out. This prevents the all-cash investor bidding wars that have priced first-time buyers out of many entry-level DFW neighborhoods.
  • 3% closing cost assistance: First-time buyers who complete the HomePath Ready Buyer online course (available at homepath.com, costs $75 which is reimbursed at closing) are eligible for up to 3% of the purchase price in closing cost assistance from Fannie Mae. On a $275,000 home, that's up to $8,250 in closing cost help.
  • HomeReady financing option: HomePath properties can be financed with Fannie Mae's HomeReady loan program — only 3% down, 620 minimum FICO, and household income can count toward qualification even if those household members aren't on the loan.

HomePath + HomeReady: The combination worth understanding

When you pair HomePath closing cost assistance with HomeReady financing, here's what the cash-to-close math can look like on a $275,000 DFW property:

  • Down payment (3% HomeReady): $8,250
  • Estimated closing costs: $6,000–$7,500
  • HomePath 3% closing cost credit: −$8,250
  • Estimated out-of-pocket at closing: $6,000–$8,250 (varies based on prepaid escrow and negotiation)

That is significantly lower than what most conventional purchase transactions require. For context, a standard conventional loan with 5% down on $275,000 would require $13,750 in down payment plus closing costs — you'd likely need $19,000–$22,000 out of pocket. HomePath with HomeReady cuts that by more than half for qualified first-time buyers.

What "as-is" means and how to protect yourself

HomePath properties are sold as-is — Fannie Mae will not negotiate repairs. This is the single biggest risk management consideration in a HomePath purchase. Here's what I tell every buyer I work with on HomePath properties:

  1. Always get a home inspection. Budget $400–$600 for a professional inspection even though the purchase is as-is. You need to know what you're buying before you sign. If the inspection reveals major issues (roof, HVAC, foundation), you can decide not to proceed and get your earnest money back during the inspection period.
  2. Get a sewer scope on older properties. Many REO homes have sat vacant for months. Sewer lines can deteriorate in that time, especially in DFW where clay soils expand and contract. A sewer scope costs $150–$200 and can save you from a $5,000–$15,000 surprise post-closing.
  3. The as-is price reflects condition. Fannie Mae prices HomePath properties below market to account for the condition. If a HomePath home in Garland is listed at $240,000 when comparable renovated homes are selling for $290,000, that $50,000 gap reflects the repairs needed and the risk you're taking on.

A first-time buyer in Dallas County using HomePath: how it works in practice

I recently worked with a buyer in Mesquite — a single income household earning $68,000/year, 645 credit score, and $12,000 saved. They'd been looking at FHA-financed purchases but kept losing to all-cash investors on the homes that fit their budget. When a HomePath property came up in their price range, they had a 30-day window where investors couldn't compete.

They completed the HomePath Ready Buyer course ($75 online, completed over a weekend), which made them eligible for the 3% closing cost credit. We financed with HomeReady at 3% down. Their $12,000 in savings covered the down payment and left enough for the remaining closing costs after the HomePath credit. They closed without asking for a gift from family or scrambling to borrow from a retirement account.

How to find HomePath listings in DFW right now

The only official source for HomePath properties is homepath.com. You can search by city or zip code — search Dallas, Garland, Mesquite, Fort Worth, Arlington, Irving, and other DFW cities to see current listings. Inventory changes frequently as Fannie Mae acquires new REO properties and sells existing ones.

Your real estate agent can also flag HomePath listings on the MLS — they're marked in listing data. If you don't have an agent, I can refer you to buyer's agents in DFW who specialize in REO and HomePath transactions.
