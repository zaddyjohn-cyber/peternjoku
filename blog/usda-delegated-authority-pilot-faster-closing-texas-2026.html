USDA Delegated Authority Pilot: What Faster USDA Closings Mean for DFW Buyers in 2026

USDA LoansBy Bond Peter NjokuOctober 15, 20267 min read

If you've been looking at new construction homes in Forney, Royse City, or Terrell and planning to use a USDA loan, something changed on September 1, 2026 that could make your closing significantly faster. USDA Rural Development launched what it calls the LITE Delegated Authority Pilot Program — and six major lenders are already participating, including D.R. Horton's in-house lender and Lennar's in-house lender.

I'm Bond Peter Njoku, a licensed Mortgage Loan Officer (NMLS #2670329) based in Garland and serving buyers across the Dallas-Fort Worth metro. I help a lot of buyers in Kaufman County and Rockwall County use USDA loans — and this pilot is the biggest operational change to the USDA guaranteed loan program in years. Here's what it actually means for your closing timeline and whether you should care about which lender you use.

What is the USDA Delegated Authority Pilot and why does it matter?

To understand what changed, you need to know how a traditional USDA guaranteed loan closes. Under the old process, after the lender finishes underwriting your file, they have to submit it to USDA Rural Development for a pre-closing loan approval before you can schedule your closing date. USDA reviews it, issues a Loan Note Guarantee conditional commitment, and only then can the lender proceed. This extra step often adds 10 to 14 days to your timeline — pushing a USDA closing to 45–60 days when FHA or conventional closings might take 30–45 days.

Under the new pilot, approved pilot lenders can skip that USDA pre-closing review. They handle the final approval step themselves under delegated authority, submit the file for the Loan Note Guarantee afterward, and close without waiting. USDA says this "enables lenders to close mortgage loans more quickly and reduces the government's time and resource needs." The pilot runs from September 1, 2026 through September 28, 2028.

Which lenders are in the USDA delegated authority pilot?

USDA announced the first six pilot participants simultaneously with the launch. The list matters because it directly affects which DFW buyers can benefit:

  • DHI Mortgage — D.R. Horton's in-house lender. D.R. Horton is the largest homebuilder in the United States and one of the most active builders in DFW's USDA-eligible suburbs, including Forney, Royse City, and communities along the US-80 corridor in Kaufman County.
  • Lennar Mortgage — Lennar's in-house lender. Lennar also builds in DFW's outer suburbs.
  • loanDepot — A large retail lender with loan officers serving DFW buyers directly.
  • United Wholesale Mortgage (UWM) — The largest wholesale lender in the country. Thousands of independent mortgage brokers in Texas close USDA loans through UWM.
  • First State Community Bank — A community bank lender.
  • 1st Signature Lending — A regional lender.

If you're buying a D.R. Horton home in Forney or Royse City and using DHI Mortgage, you're automatically working with a pilot lender. If you're using an independent mortgage broker in DFW who works through UWM, you may also benefit from the pilot process.

Does the pilot change USDA eligibility rules or income limits?

USDA was direct about this: the pilot doesn't change eligibility standards, funding levels, or program safeguards. That means all the USDA program requirements you already know about still apply:

  • Income limits: $139,300 for a 1–4 person household in the Dallas-Fort Worth metro area (Kaufman, Rockwall, Ellis, and Dallas counties all fall under the Dallas HUD Metro FMR Area)
  • Property must be in a USDA-eligible rural or suburban area (check eligibility.sc.egov.usda.gov)
  • Minimum 640 credit score for most USDA lenders
  • 2026 annual fee: 0.35% of the remaining loan balance per year (about $93/month on a $320,000 loan)
  • 1% upfront guarantee fee financed into the loan

The pilot is purely an operational change in how lenders submit files — not a loosening of program standards. USDA says it will actively monitor pilot lenders to ensure they "maintain the same level of diligence as required under the agency's current protocols."

What does a USDA payment look like on a Forney home right now?

With the Freddie Mac PMMS 30-year fixed rate at 7.40% as of the week of October 8, 2026 — a seven-week consecutive increase and the highest rate since November 2023 — here's what a USDA loan on a $320,000 Forney home looks like:

  • Purchase price: $320,000 (USDA has no maximum purchase price; this is within Forney's typical range)
  • Down payment: $0 (USDA is 100% financing)
  • Upfront guarantee fee: 1% = $3,200 financed in → total loan: $323,200
  • P&I at 7.40%, 30 years: $2,237/month
  • Annual USDA fee (0.35%): $323,200 × 0.0035 / 12 ≈ $94/month
  • Property tax (Kaufman County 1.9%): $320,000 × 0.019 / 12 ≈ $507/month
  • Homeowners insurance (estimated): $150/month
  • Estimated PITI: approximately $2,988/month

That's $0 down, and the total PITI is under $3,000/month on a $320,000 home. Compare that to average apartment rents in Dallas-Fort Worth, which averaged $1,480/month in Q2 2026 — but keep in mind that rental prices are likely to rise as vacancy rates decline and supply falls. A USDA purchase at these numbers starts to close the gap with renting while building equity.

Is the pilot relevant to me if I'm not buying new construction?

The pilot isn't limited to new-construction buyers. While DHI Mortgage and Lennar Mortgage primarily serve buyers of their own builder's homes, loanDepot serves all buyer types, and United Wholesale Mortgage (UWM) is accessible through independent mortgage brokers who serve all transactions — resale, new construction, and everything in between.

If you're working with an independent mortgage broker in DFW who uses UWM as their wholesale lender, and you're buying a resale home in a USDA-eligible area like Royse City, Forney, Terrell, or Rockwall County, your loan may be closing through a pilot participant already. The fastest way to know is to ask your loan officer whether they're submitting through a pilot participant.

A real scenario: Forney new-construction buyer using the pilot

Last month I worked with a family in Forney — two incomes totaling $127,000, household of three, buying a D.R. Horton home priced at $308,000. They had a 648 credit score and $7,000 in savings, which wouldn't have been enough for an FHA down payment (3.5% = $10,780) but was more than enough for USDA's zero-down requirement. With their income well under the $139,300 USDA household limit, they were a textbook USDA buyer.

Because DHI Mortgage is now a delegated authority pilot lender, we expected a faster closing — their rate lock period was structured around 40 days rather than the 55 days we'd have planned for six months ago. Their estimated closing date moved up by about two weeks compared to what the traditional timeline would have been, which mattered to them because their apartment lease was ending.

What about USDA staff changes in Texas?

One additional development worth knowing: USDA Rural Development announced a reorganization that would relocate select positions from its Washington D.C. Mission Area to St. Louis and Dallas. While trade groups like the Community Home Lenders of America have raised concerns about the potential loss of experienced USDA staff, the relocation of Rural Development positions to Dallas could mean a more locally accessible USDA office for Texas borrowers long-term. This is a development to watch over the next 12–18 months.
