Best Day of the Month to Close on a House in Texas: Prepaid Interest Math at 7.40%

ClosingBy Bond Peter NjokuOctober 24, 20266 min read

I'm Bond Peter Njoku, a mortgage loan officer in Garland, and buyers ask me this all the time: is it cheaper to close at the end of the month? The short answer is that closing late in the month means less cash at the closing table, but not a cheaper loan. On a $300,000 loan at today's 7.40% (Freddie Mac PMMS, mid-October 2026), the difference between closing on the 3rd and closing on the 28th is about $1,500 in cash to close. That money doesn't disappear. It moves.

The national articles on this topic stop there. In Texas, two more things matter: property tax bills that come out in the fall and are due January 31, and the way refinances fund. Here's the full picture.

How does prepaid interest work when I close?

Mortgage interest is paid in arrears, meaning your payment on December 1 covers November's interest. At closing, you prepay the interest from the closing (or funding) date through the end of that month. Then your first regular payment is due on the 1st of the second month after closing.

The daily ("per diem") interest formula is: loan amount × rate ÷ 365. On a $300,000 loan at 7.40%, that's $60.82 a day.

Do I really "skip" a mortgage payment?

No. This is the biggest myth about closing dates. You don't make a payment in the month after closing because you either prepaid that interest at closing or you're paying it in arrears. As one loan officer puts it in a widely shared explainer, there are no interest-free days. Your total interest is the same whatever day you close. The only difference is when you pay it:

If you're renting, the overlap matters too. Closing on the 28th and moving on the 30th can save you a month of double housing costs, and that is often worth more than the interest timing.

How do Texas property tax bills change the math for fall and winter closings?

Texas tax bills generally go out in October and are due by January 31. If you close between the time bills are issued and January 31, the current year's bill is often handled at closing: the seller's share is credited through proration, and either the title company pays the bill or your lender collects a larger initial escrow deposit so it can pay it. Either way, a November, December, or January closing usually needs more cash than a spring closing, whatever day of the month you pick.

This year Dallas County is holding 2026 bills until after the November 3 Prop A election, so the timing is different there. Read Dallas County's delayed tax bills and my Texas property tax proration guide before you set a late-fall date.

Is it different for a refinance in Texas?

Yes. On a refinance of your primary home, federal law gives you a three-business-day right of rescission after signing. The loan funds after that, and prepaid interest starts on the funding date, not the signing date. If you sign on the 27th, rescission can push funding into the next month, which changes both the prepaid interest and your first payment date. Texas cash-out refinances under Section 50(a)(6) also have a 12-day waiting period before closing. Plan your dates with both in mind. See my 50(a)(6) cash-out guide.

Which days should I avoid?

  • Fridays: if anything is late (a wire, a document, a funding approval), you can lose the whole weekend. Recording and funding desks are slower too.
  • The last business day of the month: every title company in DFW is crowded with month-end closings. A small delay can roll you into the next month, and that resets your prepaid interest to almost a full month.
  • The day before a holiday: banks close and wires stop. Thanksgiving week and Christmas week are especially tight.
  • Your rate-lock expiration day: give yourself a few days of cushion. My rate-lock extension guide shows what an extension costs.

My usual recommendation: close around the 20th–27th, Monday through Thursday. You keep prepaid interest low without the month-end crunch.

How did a Plano buyer pick their date?

A couple I worked with this fall was buying a $400,000 home in Plano with 20% down, a $320,000 conventional loan at about 7.40%, for a per diem of $64.88. Their lease ended November 30. The seller offered October 30 or November 2.

Closing October 30 meant 2 days of prepaid interest (about $130) and a first payment on December 1. Closing November 2 meant 29 days (about $1,882) and a first payment on January 1. They had the cash either way. What decided it was that an October 30 closing gave them a full month to move before their lease ended, and they weren't comfortable closing on a Monday right after a month-end weekend. They chose October 30 (a Friday), and we had every condition cleared and documents signed off by Wednesday to reduce the Friday risk.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is it better to close on a house at the beginning or end of the month?

I'm Bond Peter Njoku (NMLS #2670329), a Garland loan officer. Closing at the end of the month means less prepaid interest and less cash at closing. Closing at the beginning means more cash at closing but a longer gap before your first payment. Your total interest is the same either way. On a $300,000 loan at 7.40%, closing on the 3rd costs about $1,764 in prepaid interest, compared with $243 on the 28th. Call or text me at 469-545-7180 and I'll run your numbers.

When is my first mortgage payment due after closing in Texas?

I'm Bond Peter Njoku (NMLS #2670329). Your first payment is usually due on the 1st of the second month after closing. If you close anytime in October, your first payment is December 1, because interest is paid in arrears and you prepaid October's interest at closing. On a refinance, count from the funding date, which comes after the three-day rescission period. Call or text me at 469-545-7180 to confirm the date for your loan.

Why do I need more cash to close in November or December in Texas?

I'm Bond Peter Njoku (NMLS #2670329). Texas property tax bills generally go out in October and are due by January 31. When you close in that window, the current year's taxes are often paid at closing or your lender collects a larger escrow deposit to pay them, with the seller crediting their share through proration. Should I avoid closing on a Friday in Texas?

I'm Bond Peter Njoku (NMLS #2670329). I recommend Monday through Thursday when possible. If a wire, document, or funding approval runs late on a Friday, you can lose the weekend and your movers. The last business day of the month and the day before a holiday carry the same risk. If you do close on a Friday, clear every condition and sign off documents early in the week.
