USDA vs FHA Loan Texas 2026 — Which Is Better for You?

Loan ProgramsBy Bond Peter NjokuAugust 4, 20269 min read

I'm Bond Peter Njoku, and this is one of the most common questions I get from buyers looking at the outer edges of DFW — Forney, Royse City, Terrell, and parts of Rockwall. Both USDA and FHA loans are built for buyers who don't have a large down payment saved, but they work very differently. Here's how I explain the tradeoffs so you can pick the right one for your situation.

Where You're Buying Decides the Loan

The biggest factor is location. USDA loans are restricted to properties in USDA-designated eligible areas, which in the DFW area typically covers Forney, Royse City, parts of Rockwall, and Terrell — not Dallas, Garland, or Mesquite. FHA loans, on the other hand, work anywhere in Texas. If your target home is in an ineligible USDA area, the decision is already made for you.

Down Payment and Upfront Costs Compared

USDA offers true 100% financing with $0 down, while FHA requires a minimum 3.5% down payment. On a $320,000 home, that's the difference between $0 and $11,200 out of pocket before closing costs. USDA's upfront guarantee fee runs around 1% of the loan, versus FHA's 1.75% upfront mortgage insurance premium — both of these are typically rolled into the loan rather than paid in cash.

Ongoing Mortgage Insurance Costs

This is where USDA usually wins long-term: its annual fee runs around 0.35% of the loan balance, compared to FHA's 0.55% annual mortgage insurance premium (MIP), which unlike conventional PMI typically doesn't cancel for the life of the loan unless you refinance. On a $300,000 loan, that difference adds up to roughly $600 a year in USDA's favor.

Credit Score and Income Requirements

FHA is more forgiving on credit — a 580 score gets you 3.5% down, and some lenders go lower with a larger down payment. USDA lenders generally want to see 640 or above. USDA also has household income limits by county (around $119,700 for Collin/Denton/Rockwall counties in 2026), while FHA has no income cap at all. If your income is above USDA's limit or your credit is under 640, FHA is usually the practical choice, especially paired with a down payment assistance grant.

Which One Should You Choose?

If your property is USDA-eligible and your credit is 640+, USDA usually saves you the most money over time thanks to lower ongoing fees and zero down payment. If you're buying in Dallas, Garland, Mesquite, or anywhere else outside USDA-eligible zones — or your credit is under 640 — FHA paired with down payment assistance is the stronger path. I run both scenarios side by side for every client before we commit to an application.
