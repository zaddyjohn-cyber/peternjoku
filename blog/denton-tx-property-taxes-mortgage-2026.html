Denton TX Property Taxes and Your Mortgage Payment — 2026

Property TaxesBy Bond Peter NjokuAugust 27, 20269 min read

I'm Bond Peter Njoku (NMLS #2670329), and Denton is a market with a personality of its own — a college town anchored by UNT and Texas Woman's University, a genuine downtown square, and some of the fastest population growth in North Texas. Denton County's combined effective property tax rate runs around 1.8% of assessed value in 2026. That puts it between low-tax Collin County and the 2.0–2.2% you'd pay in Dallas or Tarrant. For buyers weighing Denton against Lewisville, Flower Mound, or a longer commute from Collin County, the tax rate belongs in that comparison from the start.

What is the property tax rate in Denton TX for 2026?

Your Denton tax bill combines Denton County, the City of Denton, Denton ISD, and the county's hospital and college districts. Newer master-planned areas on the south and west sides of town may also sit inside a municipal utility district (MUD) that adds its own levy for the first years while infrastructure debt is repaid. Combined, the effective rate lands near 1.8% for 2026, but a MUD can push a specific address noticeably higher. I check every property through the Denton Central Appraisal District (DCAD) and confirm whether a MUD applies before I quote a payment, because that single factor can move the escrow by $100 a month or more.

How much does a mortgage cost in Denton TX, taxes included?

Full math on a $335,000 Denton home — near the 2026 median — with 10% down ($33,500) on a 30-year loan at 7.0%:

  • Principal & interest: $301,500 loan → about $2,006/month
  • Property taxes: $335,000 × 1.8% = $6,030/year → about $503/month
  • Homeowners insurance: roughly $150/month in Denton County
  • Full PITI payment: approximately $2,659/month before homestead exemption

That $503 tax figure is escrowed — your servicer collects one-twelfth monthly and pays Denton County when due. The same $335,000 home in Dallas County at roughly 2.0% would escrow about $558/month, and in Tarrant County at 2.2% about $614/month, so Denton's rate saves $55–$111 a month versus those counties on an identical price.

How much does the homestead exemption save me in Denton?

Once the home is your primary residence, you file a general homestead exemption with DCAD. The state exemption removes $100,000 of value from school-district taxation, and the City of Denton and Denton County each apply an additional percentage-based homestead exemption. On a $335,000 home taxed near 1.8%, the school-district portion of the state exemption saves close to $1,000 a year, and combined with the local percentage exemptions total annual savings generally land between $1,100 and $1,700. File the form after closing — I tell every Denton client to do it within 90 days so no tax year is missed.

Do Denton property taxes affect what mortgage I qualify for?

Yes. Your debt-to-income ratio is calculated on the full PITI payment, so Denton County's 1.8% rate — and any MUD levy on top — lowers your qualifying loan amount. On a $335,000 Denton home, the $503/month tax escrow weighs on your DTI about like an extra $76,000 of loan principal, and if the property sits in a MUD district the effect is larger. I run every Denton pre-approval with the true rate for the exact address, MUD included. If your ratios are close to the line, an FHA loan in Denton gives you more DTI room than conventional financing.

What other exemptions can lower my Denton tax bill?

Owners 65 and older get an additional homestead exemption plus a Denton ISD school-tax ceiling that freezes the district portion of the bill. Disabled homeowners qualify for a similar exemption, and disabled veterans receive a partial-to-total exemption based on VA disability rating. Every Denton owner can also protest the appraised value with DCAD each spring — the informal hearing with recent comparable sales is the most effective path, and a successful protest lowers your escrow for as long as you own the home. In a fast-appreciating market like Denton's, protesting most years is worth the hour it takes.

How do I keep my Denton mortgage payment manageable despite the tax rate?

Start by knowing whether the home sits in a MUD before you write the offer — that one check can save you from a $100-a-month surprise, and if it is in a MUD, factor the higher escrow into your target price. File the homestead exemption the week you close for the year-two adjustment. On financing, weigh a permanent buydown with discount points against a seller-funded 2-1 temporary buydown; Denton has enough newer inventory that builder and reseller concessions are often available. And protest the DCAD value every spring — Denton County has been one of the fastest-appreciating parts of North Texas, so appraisals tend to run ahead of what a documented comp set supports. These moves don't lower the 1.8% rate, but they keep the total payment where your budget needs it.

A recent Denton example

Last fall I worked with a UNT staff member buying her first home — a $328,000 property on the south side of Denton, credit in the low 700s, 5% down on a conventional loan. The listing was in a newer subdivision, and when I pulled the DCAD record it sat inside a MUD that added roughly $95/month to the tax escrow beyond the base county rate. Because we caught it before the offer, she adjusted her target price down about $15,000 to keep the total payment where she wanted it, and she filed her homestead exemption the week she moved in. Had she found that MUD levy at closing instead, the payment would have been a real problem.
