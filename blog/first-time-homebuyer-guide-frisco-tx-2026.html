First-Time Homebuyer Programs Frisco TX 2026: FHA, TSAHC Grants, and How to Afford One of DFW's Priciest Suburbs

First-Time BuyersBy Bond Peter NjokuAugust 15, 20269 min read

I'm Bond Peter Njoku (NMLS #2670329) and I help first-time buyers purchase homes in Frisco — one of the fastest-growing cities in the United States. Frisco ISD is consistently ranked among the top school districts in Texas, Toyota Stadium anchors a thriving entertainment district, and Collin County continues to attract major employers. Median home prices run $500,000–$650,000, which intimidates a lot of first-time buyers who assume they can't compete here. But the 2026 FHA loan limit covers purchases up to $563,500 in Frisco, and Collin County's DPA income limit of $119,700 keeps many two-income households eligible for up to $25,000+ in grant assistance. Here's exactly how it works.

What first-time homebuyer programs are available in Frisco TX?

Frisco is in Collin County, which gives buyers access to the two largest state-funded DPA programs in Texas:

TSAHC Home Sweet Texas — a grant of 3–5% of the loan amount, never repaid. On a $520,000 Frisco purchase with 5% down FHA ($26,000 down), a 5% TSAHC grant of $26,000 covers the full down payment. You'd still owe closing costs, but sellers in Frisco can credit up to 6% under FHA. Requires a 620 FICO and income at or below $119,700 for a Collin County household of 1–4 people. No first-time buyer requirement — TSAHC is available even if you've owned before.

TDHCA My First Texas Home — up to 5% as a deferred second loan, forgiven at year 3. Same income limit and credit floor as TSAHC. Requires the buyer to be purchasing their first home (or a veteran re-entering the market after 3+ years without ownership). Pairs with FHA, conventional, or VA loans.

These programs matter more in Frisco than in lower-cost DFW markets because the dollar amounts are larger — 5% of $520,000 is $26,000, compared to 5% of $320,000 being $16,000. In a market like Frisco, that grant can be the difference between buying now and waiting two more years to save.

Frisco's higher home prices make the FHA vs conventional decision more nuanced than in lower-cost cities. If the home you're targeting is under $563,500, FHA with TSAHC is often the most efficient path — especially for buyers at 580–679 FICO. If you're at 680+ and the home is priced $500K–$800K, conventional gives you a clear path to PMI cancellation and often a better total cost over time despite the higher down payment.

What will my mortgage payment be on a Frisco TX home?

At a $530,000 purchase price with 5% down FHA at 7.0% on a 30-year fixed:

  • Purchase price: $530,000
  • Down payment (5%): $26,500
  • Loan amount: $503,500
  • Principal & interest: approximately $3,351/month
  • FHA annual MIP (0.55%): approximately $231/month
  • Collin County property taxes (~1.5%): approximately $663/month
  • Homeowners insurance: approximately $175/month
  • Total PITI: approximately $4,420/month

With a TSAHC 5% grant ($26,500), the down payment is fully offset. Closing costs on a $530,000 Frisco purchase typically run $9,000–$12,000. FHA allows the seller to contribute up to 6% ($31,800) toward closing — which means you could, in theory, close with $0 out of pocket if the seller cooperates and your grant covers the down payment.

Can I actually afford to buy in Frisco as a first-time buyer?

The income needed to comfortably carry that $4,420/month PITI (at a 43% DTI) is roughly $123,000 combined gross income. That's above the $119,700 DPA income limit — so buyers right at that threshold face a timing question: qualify for the grant now at a slightly smaller home, or save for conventional and buy a pricier home later without DPA.

Last year I helped a Frisco couple — one software engineer and one nurse, combined income of $117,000 — buy a $495,000 townhome. Their 658 FICO qualified for FHA, and we layered TSAHC's 5% grant ($24,750) to zero out the down payment. The seller covered $9,000 in closing costs, and they brought $5,200 to closing: home inspection, appraisal, and first-year homeowners insurance paid upfront. Their PITI came to $4,180/month — $400 less than their rental in Frisco's rental market had been.
