Buying a House With Foundation Problems in DFW: What Your Mortgage Lender Will Require

Buying ProcessBy Bond Peter NjokuOctober 22, 20267 min read

I'm Bond Peter Njoku, a mortgage loan officer in Garland, and foundation issues are probably the most common inspection problem I see in Dallas-Fort Worth deals. The reason is the ground. Much of DFW sits on expansive clay soil that swells in wet springs and shrinks in summer droughts, and most homes here are built on slabs. The result is cracks in brick, doors that stick, and an inspection report that says "evidence of foundation movement."

Most articles on this topic are written by real estate agents or foundation repair companies. They cover what to inspect and how to negotiate. This one covers what happens inside your loan once foundation problems show up, because that decides which fixes actually work.

Can I get a mortgage on a house with foundation problems in Texas?

Usually, yes. Settlement doesn't automatically make a home unfinanceable. What matters is whether the movement is minor, stable or already repaired, or active and affecting the structure. Each loan program asks the same basic question in a different way:

Conventional (Fannie/Freddie)Selling Guide property rulesCondition rating C1–C6; abnormal settlementC5/C6 conditions or structural concerns; a C6 home isn't eligible until correctedYes, with HomeStyle Renovation or a similar loan

A common contractor claim is that FHA and VA "always require engineer-certified repairs." That's not a blanket rule. An engineer's report and repair proof are required when the appraiser or underwriter sees a problem. Disclosed but repaired, stable movement with good paperwork often goes through without trouble.

What's the difference between the inspector, the appraiser and the engineer?

  • TREC home inspector (you hire them during the option period): reports visible signs of movement. Inspectors don't diagnose structural causes, and the report often says "recommend evaluation by a structural engineer."
  • Structural engineer (you or the seller hire them): measures elevations, decides whether movement is active, and writes a repair plan if needed. Underwriters trust this document.
  • Appraiser (the lender orders them): values the home and notes readily apparent conditions. If they see a structural concern, they may complete the appraisal "subject to" an inspection or repair, and the loan can't close until that condition is cleared.

Your inspector's report does not automatically go to the lender. The appraiser's observations, the Seller's Disclosure Notice and any engineer report you share do become part of the loan file. Once a problem is in the file, it has to be resolved.

Why can't the seller just give me a credit for the repair?

This is where many DFW deals go wrong. The buyer gets a $12,000 foundation bid and asks the seller for "$12,000 at closing to fix it." But seller concessions can only pay closing costs, prepaids and rate buydowns. Lenders can't give you cash back for repairs. Any part of the credit beyond your actual costs gets cut or forces a price reduction anyway. See my seller concessions guide for the limits (FHA 6%, VA 4%, conventional 3–9% depending on down payment).

How much does a price reduction change my payment?

Take a $320,000 Mesquite home with an FHA loan (3.5% down, financed upfront MIP, 7.40% rate) and a $12,000 pier repair bid:

Original priceAfter $12,000 price cut
Purchase price$320,000$308,000
Down payment (3.5%)$11,200$10,780
Loan with upfront MIP$314,204$302,421
Principal & interest$2,175/mo$2,094/mo
Property tax (~2.0% Dallas County)$533/mo$513/mo

The price cut saves about $100 a month on principal, interest and tax, plus $420 at closing. Most of the benefit, though, is the $12,000 you'll spend on the repair. Only take the price cut if you have that cash and the appraisal isn't conditioned on the repair. If it is, the repair has to be done before closing, and the seller is the one who can do it.

What paperwork should I get if the house was already repaired?

A previously repaired foundation can be a positive if the paperwork is complete. Ask for:

  • The engineer's report before the repair and the post-repair letter confirming the work matched the plan
  • The city permit, if the city required one
  • The warranty and its transfer terms. Many "lifetime" warranties transfer only if a fee is paid and a form is filed within a set number of days after closing
  • Any plumbing leak test results, since slab leaks under DFW homes often cause movement

One more point: homeowners insurance in Texas generally excludes settling and foundation movement, so the warranty is your only protection after closing. Read it before your option period ends.

A Mesquite example: a crack that became an appraisal condition

This summer I worked with a first-time buyer, a warehouse supervisor with credit in the low 600s, using FHA and Texas down payment assistance on a 1979 home in Mesquite. Her inspector noted diagonal cracks over two interior doors. Then the FHA appraiser completed the appraisal "subject to" a structural inspection, so it was now a loan condition, not just an item on her own report.

The seller's first offer was a $7,500 credit. We explained why that couldn't work on FHA. Instead, the seller hired an engineer, who recommended eight exterior piers. A contractor installed them before closing, and the file got the engineer's completion letter and a transferable warranty. The appraiser did a final inspection, the condition cleared, and she closed six days later than planned with the foundation repaired and no repair bill of her own.

How do I protect myself when I make an offer in DFW?

  1. Negotiate enough option days to fit a general inspection and, if needed, an engineer visit. I recommend 10 days for homes built before 2000.
  2. Read the Seller's Disclosure Notice for previous repairs or claims before you write the offer.
  3. Call me as soon as the inspection mentions movement, so we can pick the repair approach that fits your loan program.
  4. Get every repair agreement in writing on the TREC amendment, including who hires the engineer and contractor.

If you're shopping in older parts of Mesquite or Garland, where most homes date from the 1960s to 1980s, plan on this from day one. My Mesquite FHA page and 203(k) renovation guide are good next reads.

Frequently Asked Questions

Will FHA finance a house with foundation problems?

I'm Bond Peter Njoku (NMLS #2670329), and FHA can finance a home with past or minor foundation movement as long as the home meets HUD's safety, soundness and structural integrity standards. If the appraiser sees significant structural signs, the appraisal may be completed subject to a structural inspection or repair, and that has to be cleared before closing. Active problems usually need to be repaired with engineer documentation first, or financed through an FHA 203(k). Call or text me at 469-545-7180 and I'll review your inspection report with you.

Can the seller give me a credit for foundation repairs at closing in Texas?

I'm Bond Peter Njoku (NMLS #2670329). Seller concessions can pay your closing costs, prepaids and rate buydowns, but lenders can't give you cash back for repairs, so a repair credit beyond your actual costs doesn't work. The better options are a seller-completed repair before closing with an engineer's letter, a price reduction if the appraisal isn't conditioned on the repair, or a 203(k) that finances the work. Which one fits depends on your loan type and the appraiser's notes. Call or text me at 469-545-7180 before you sign the repair amendment.

Do I need a structural engineer to buy a house in DFW?

I'm Bond Peter Njoku (NMLS #2670329), and you don't need one for every purchase, but I recommend one whenever your TREC inspector reports foundation movement or recommends further evaluation. An engineer's report tells you whether the movement is active, and underwriters rely on it if the appraiser flags the foundation. Schedule it inside your option period so you can still walk away. Call or text me at 469-545-7180 to plan your option-period timeline.

Does homeowners insurance cover foundation repair in Texas?

I'm Bond Peter Njoku (NMLS #2670329), and in most Texas homeowners policies, settling, cracking and foundation movement from soil are excluded. Some damage from a sudden covered plumbing leak may be treated differently, so read your policy. That makes the transferable repair warranty and the engineer's post-repair letter very important on a previously repaired DFW home.
