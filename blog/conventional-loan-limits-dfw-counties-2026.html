2026 Conventional Loan Limits Across DFW Counties

ConventionalBy Bond Peter NjokuSeptember 17, 20267 min read

Every county in the Dallas-Fort Worth-Arlington metro shares the exact same 2026 conforming loan limit — a fact that surprises buyers moving between DFW suburbs who assume Collin County or Tarrant County must have its own number. I'm Bond Peter Njoku (NMLS #2670329), and here's the full 2026 conventional loan limit picture across the metro, broken out by property unit count.

What is the 2026 conventional loan limit across DFW?

Units2026 conforming limit
1-unit (single-family)$832,750
2-unit (duplex)$1,066,250
3-unit (triplex)$1,288,800
4-unit (fourplex)$1,601,750

These figures apply identically whether you're buying in Dallas, Collin, Denton, Tarrant, Rockwall, or Kaufman county — the whole metro is treated as one area by Fannie Mae and Freddie Mac for 2026, with no high-cost county adjustment inside DFW. Full qualification details are in my conventional loan requirements in Texas guide.

Does the loan limit vary by DFW city or county?

No — this is the most common misconception I hear from buyers relocating within the metro. Someone moving from a $400,000 home in Garland to a $750,000 move-up purchase in Southlake or Prosper is working against the exact same $832,750 single-family ceiling, not a higher one just because the destination city has pricier homes. The limit is set at the metro level, not the city or even the county level.

What happens if my purchase price crosses the limit?

Once your loan amount exceeds $832,750 for a single-family home, you move from conforming conventional financing into jumbo territory. Jumbo loans are not sold to Fannie Mae or Freddie Mac, so they typically carry a higher minimum credit score, larger cash reserve requirements, and sometimes a different rate structure than a conforming loan just under the line. If your target home is priced close to that threshold, the size of your down payment can be the deciding factor in whether you stay conforming or cross into jumbo.

Can I buy a duplex or fourplex under these same limits?

Yes — and this is where the DFW loan limit table becomes especially useful for house-hacking or small multi-family buyers. A duplex purchase up to $1,066,250 still qualifies as a conforming conventional loan, as does a triplex up to $1,288,800 and a fourplex up to $1,601,750. Owner-occupied multi-unit purchases can also often use rental income from the other units to help qualify, which is a separate conversation worth having before you shop.

Named example: a Frisco move-up buyer near the limit

I recently worked with a move-up buyer relocating from Garland to a $790,000 home in Frisco. At first glance, that price looked close enough to the $832,750 limit to worry about jumbo financing — but once we structured the loan with her planned 15% down payment, her loan amount came in comfortably under the conforming ceiling, keeping her in standard conventional pricing with none of the added jumbo reserve requirements. Running that math before writing the offer meant she knew exactly which financing lane she'd be in before she ever competed for the house.
