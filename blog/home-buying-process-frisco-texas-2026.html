Home Buying Process Frisco Texas — Step by Step for 2026

Local GuideBy Bond Peter NjokuAugust 22, 202610 min read

I'm Bond Peter Njoku (NMLS #2670329), and I walk Frisco buyers through this exact process regularly. Frisco's median home price sits near $520,000 in 2026 — one of the higher price points in DFW — driven by Frisco ISD's reputation, the PGA Frisco headquarters, and the Ford Center and Comerica Center sports and entertainment district. Most conventional purchases here close in 30–45 days from an accepted offer, and FHA purchases typically run 35–50 days. Here's exactly what happens at each stage, with realistic day ranges, so you know what to expect before you start touring.

How does the timeline differ between FHA and conventional in Frisco?

FHA takes longer at nearly every stage because of the additional appraisal requirements (FHA appraisers must also flag health and safety issues, which conventional appraisers don't) and an extra layer of underwriting review. Here's how the two compare stage by stage:

StageConventionalFHA
Appraisal turnaround7–14 days10–18 days
Underwriting10–15 days14–20 days
Repairs required before closingRare, negotiatedMore common — health/safety items
Total closing window30–45 days35–50 days

Because Frisco's median price of $520,000 sits comfortably under the 2026 conforming loan limit of $806,500 but occasionally approaches or exceeds the FHA limit of $563,500 depending on the home, most Frisco buyers I work with end up using conventional financing, with FHA reserved for entry-level condos and townhomes closer to city center.

Before you start touring Frisco listings, get pre-approved. Frisco's newer developments near PGA Frisco and the sports and entertainment district move quickly, and agents frequently won't schedule showings for unapproved buyers, while sellers rarely accept an offer without a pre-approval letter attached. Call or text me at 469-545-7180 and I'll run a soft credit pull and give you a real number in about 10 minutes, checking FHA, conventional, and VA eligibility together rather than one at a time.

Which appraisal district covers my Frisco property?

This is one of the more common questions I get from Frisco buyers, because the city technically spans two counties. Most of Frisco falls under the Collin Central Appraisal District (CCAD), but the northern and western portions of the city extend into Denton County and are covered by the Denton Central Appraisal District (DCAD). Both districts show the current assessed value, tax payment history, and homestead exemption status — which matters because exemptions don't automatically transfer to you, so your own tax bill after closing could be higher than the seller's. I confirm which district applies before every Frisco client writes an offer.

What happens during the Frisco option period?

Texas contracts include an option period — typically 7–10 days in Frisco's market — during which you pay a small fee (often $200–$500) for the right to terminate the contract for any reason. This is when your inspector goes through the property. At Frisco's median price point of roughly $520,000, a full inspection typically runs $450–$700 and takes 2–4 hours. Frisco's inventory skews newer than many DFW suburbs, so inspections here more often turn up minor builder punch-list items than major structural concerns — though I still recommend a full third-party inspection on new construction, not just a builder walkthrough.

What title company should I use in Frisco?

I work with a handful of title companies that handle high purchase volume across both Collin and Denton County closings and communicate proactively when something threatens the closing date. That matters more than people expect, since a delayed closing can mean a delayed move-in or a rate lock expiration. I don't take referral fees for these recommendations — I suggest them because they consistently close on time for my Frisco clients. Call or text 469-545-7180 and I'll make the introduction.

A recent Frisco timeline in practice

Last spring I worked with a family relocating to Frisco for a corporate transfer — 705 credit score, conventional loan, 10% down on a $530,000 new-construction home near the sports and entertainment district. Pre-approval took one call, done before the family had even visited in person. Their builder had them under contract within a week of touring model homes. The third-party inspection (separate from the builder's own walkthrough) turned up a handful of minor punch-list items the builder corrected before closing. Appraisal came in at value on day 15. We had clear-to-close by day 33 and closed on day 36, in time for the kids to start the new school year at their zoned campus.
