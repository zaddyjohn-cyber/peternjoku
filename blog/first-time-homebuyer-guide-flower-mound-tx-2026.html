First-Time Homebuyer Programs Flower Mound TX 2026: TSAHC, TDHCA, Conventional, and FHA

First-Time BuyersBy Bond Peter NjokuAugust 16, 20269 min read

I'm Bond Peter Njoku (NMLS #2670329) and I help first-time buyers close on homes in Flower Mound. Flower Mound is one of Denton County's most desirable family markets, zoned almost entirely to top-rated Lewisville ISD schools, with median home prices running $460,000–$580,000 — the highest in Denton County. Because Flower Mound sits at the higher end of the price spectrum, program selection matters more here than in most DFW cities. Here's what first-time buyers need to know for 2026.

What first-time homebuyer programs are available in Flower Mound TX in 2026?

Flower Mound buyers have access to the same DPA programs as the rest of Denton County, but the higher price point shifts which loan type makes the most sense:

TSAHC Home Sweet Texas — a grant of 3–5% of the loan amount, never repaid. Available at Denton County's $119,700 income limit for a 1–4 person household. Requires 620 FICO minimum. No first-time buyer requirement.

TDHCA My First Texas Home — up to 5% deferred, forgiven at year 3. Same $119,700 Denton County income cap. Requires first-time buyer status or veteran re-entry.

Conventional loans — the most common choice in Flower Mound given the price range, with as little as 3% down for qualified first-time buyers with 620+ FICO, and PMI that drops off once you hit 80% loan-to-value.

FHA loans — still viable up to the 2026 limit of $563,500, which covers most of Flower Mound's price range, but move-up or Lakeside DFW-area properties above that figure require conventional or jumbo financing instead.

Given Flower Mound's price range, most of my first-time buyer clients here use conventional financing rather than FHA, since a meaningful share of listings run above the $563,500 FHA cap. TSAHC's grant still applies to either loan type as long as household income stays under $119,700.

What will my mortgage payment be on a Flower Mound TX home?

At Flower Mound's median of roughly $520,000, here's a 5% down conventional purchase at 7.0% on a 30-year fixed:

  • Purchase price: $520,000
  • Down payment (5%): $26,000
  • Loan amount: $494,000
  • Principal & interest: approximately $3,286/month
  • PMI (until 80% LTV, approx.): approximately $210/month
  • Denton County property taxes (~1.8%): approximately $780/month
  • Homeowners insurance: approximately $175/month
  • Total PITI: approximately $4,451/month

With a TSAHC 5% grant ($26,000), the down payment is fully covered for income-qualifying buyers. Closing costs on a $520,000 Flower Mound purchase typically run $9,000–$13,000. Given the higher price point, I encourage Flower Mound buyers to negotiate seller-paid closing costs whenever possible — conventional loans allow up to 3% in seller concessions ($15,600) with 5% down.

What if my household is over the $119,700 DPA income limit?

A significant number of Flower Mound buyers, especially dual-income professional households, exceed the $119,700 TSAHC/TDHCA income cap given the area's price point and buyer profile. In that case, I focus purely on conventional financing with strong rate and PMI-removal strategy rather than DPA — most of my Flower Mound clients in this position still close successfully with 5–10% down and standard underwriting.

Last year I helped a family relocating to Flower Mound specifically for the LISD school zones — combined income of $142,000 (over the DPA limit), 705 FICO, purchasing a $535,000 home. Without DPA eligibility, we structured a conventional loan with 10% down ($53,500) and negotiated a 2% seller credit toward closing costs, bringing their total cash to close to roughly $58,000 including reserves — well-planned given their savings and income profile.

How do I get started with a mortgage in Flower Mound?

  1. Pre-approval call (15 minutes): I check your income against the $119,700 DPA limit and, if you're over it, build a conventional strategy around your savings and target price.
  2. Pre-approval letter (1–2 days): Flower Mound listing agents expect a strong letter before scheduling showings, especially in competitive LISD-zoned neighborhoods.
  3. Home search (3–6 weeks): Flower Mound inventory in top school zones moves quickly — I stay in close contact with your agent throughout.
  4. Under contract to close (30–45 days): Conventional typically runs 30–40 days; FHA on qualifying entry-level inventory runs 35–45 days.
