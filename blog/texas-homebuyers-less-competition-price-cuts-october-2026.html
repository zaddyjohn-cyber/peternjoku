Texas Buyers Face Less Competition and Record Price Cuts: What It Means for Your DFW Mortgage (October 2026)

Market UpdateBy Bond Peter NjokuOctober 19, 20266 min read

I'm Bond Peter Njoku, a mortgage loan officer in Garland, and two reports out this month confirm what I've been seeing on DFW contracts since summer: buyers have room to negotiate again. On October 6, 2026, Texas REALTORS released its Texas Homebuying Experience Report, covering agents' experiences with buyer clients from January through August 2026. Nine days later, Redfin reported that U.S. asking prices fell year over year for the first time since March, and a record 22% of sellers cut their price.

The news coverage stops at "it's a better time to buy." I want to go one step further — how to turn less competition into better loan terms: which protections to keep, when a seller credit beats a price cut, and what that's worth in dollars at today's 7.40% rate.

Two caveats. The Texas REALTORS report reflects agents' observations, not a count of every sale. And Redfin's numbers are national. But both match what I see in Garland, Mesquite, Rockwall and Collin County files: fewer bidding wars, more listings with price reductions, more sellers willing to pay closing costs.

Why does affordability still feel tight if competition is down?

Because the problem moved from price to payment. Only 6% of agents said buyers paused because there were too few homes; 34% said the homes were too expensive. Rates are the biggest part of that — Freddie Mac's 30-year average was 7.40% for the week of October 15, versus 6.27% a year earlier. In Texas, property taxes and insurance add hundreds more. On a $350,000 Garland home with FHA:

  • Down payment at 3.5%: $12,250. Loan with upfront MIP: about $343,665.
  • Principal and interest at 7.40%: about $2,380
  • Dallas County property tax at ~2.0%: $583
  • Insurance: about $175. FHA monthly MIP: about $155
  • Estimated PITI: about $3,290 a month

That's why the negotiation I care about isn't only price — it's anything that lowers your payment or your cash to close.

Which contract protections should I keep when there's less competition?

All of them. In 2021, buyers waived protections to win. In 2026 there's no reason to:

  • Third Party Financing Addendum — protects your earnest money if your loan isn't approved by the deadline. See my TPFA buyer guide.
  • A full option period — 7 to 10 days to inspect and renegotiate repairs.
  • Appraisal protection — don't sign away the right to renegotiate if the appraisal comes in low.
  • Reasonable earnest money — about 1% is normal in most of DFW.

How do I make one offer that gets accepted?

Nearly a third of recent buyers bought after one offer. What I see winning that first offer isn't overbidding — it's certainty. A fully underwritten pre-approval, an earnest money deposit that shows you're serious, a closing date that fits the seller, and a clear request (credit or price, not both plus a long repair list). Ask for the seller credit up front, not after the inspection, when you have less leverage.

A Garland first-time buyer who bought on her first offer

In September I worked with a first-time buyer in Garland — 655 credit score, $78,000 salary, FHA, looking around $300,000. The home she liked had been listed 41 days with one price reduction already. We went in at $296,000 with a $9,000 seller credit (about 3% — well within FHA's 6% limit), the TPFA kept at a 21-day deadline, and a full 10-day option period. The seller accepted with no counter. The credit covered most of her closing costs, so she kept about $7,000 in savings instead of emptying her account at closing. A year ago that same request would have been laughed off.
