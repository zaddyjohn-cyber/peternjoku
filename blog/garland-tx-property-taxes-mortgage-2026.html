Garland TX Property Taxes and Your Mortgage Payment — 2026

Property TaxesBy Bond Peter NjokuAugust 27, 20269 min read

I'm Bond Peter Njoku (NMLS #2670329), and Garland is home — my office is on South Shiloh Road, and Garland first-time buyers are the clients I work with most. Dallas County's combined effective property tax rate runs around 2.0% of assessed value in 2026. That's higher than the Collin County suburbs to the north, but Garland's home prices are lower, which keeps the city one of the most accessible entry points into DFW homeownership. The key is running the real tax number into your payment from day one so your budget and your pre-approval actually match.

What is the property tax rate in Garland TX for 2026?

Your Garland tax bill stacks several taxing units: Dallas County, the City of Garland, your school district, the Parkland Hospital District, and Dallas College. Garland ISD covers most of the city, but Richardson ISD reaches into the west side and Rockwall ISD and Sachse-area districts touch the northeast, and each taxes at a slightly different rate. Combined, the effective rate lands near 2.0% for 2026. I confirm the exact taxing-unit mix through the Dallas Central Appraisal District (DCAD) for each specific address before I quote a payment — in a city that borders four school districts, that check matters.

How much does a mortgage cost in Garland TX, taxes included?

Full math on a $325,000 Garland home — near the 2026 median — with 10% down ($32,500) on a 30-year loan at 7.0%:

  • Principal & interest: $292,500 loan → about $1,946/month
  • Property taxes: $325,000 × 2.0% = $6,500/year → about $542/month
  • Homeowners insurance: roughly $140/month in Dallas County
  • Full PITI payment: approximately $2,628/month before homestead exemption

Your servicer escrows that $542, collecting one-twelfth each month and paying Dallas County when the bill is due. The same $325,000 home in Collin County at roughly 1.5% would escrow about $406/month for taxes — a $136/month gap on an identical price. For a first-time buyer, that difference can be the room for a car payment or student loan in the DTI math.

How much does the homestead exemption save me in Garland?

After you close and occupy the home as your primary residence, you file a general homestead exemption with DCAD. The state exemption removes $100,000 of value from school-district taxation, and the City of Garland and Dallas County each add a percentage-based homestead exemption — Dallas County's is 20% of value. On a $325,000 home taxed near 2.0%, the school-district portion of the state exemption saves roughly $1,000 a year, and combined with the local percentage exemptions total annual savings usually land between $1,100 and $1,800. It's not automatic. I walk every Garland client through the DCAD homestead form and tell them to file within 90 days of closing.

Do Garland property taxes affect what mortgage I qualify for?

Yes. I calculate your debt-to-income ratio on the full PITI payment, so Dallas County's 2.0% rate trims your qualifying loan amount compared with a same-income buyer in Collin County. On a $325,000 Garland home, the $542/month tax escrow affects your DTI about like an extra $81,000 of loan principal. I run every Garland pre-approval with the true local rate for your exact school district. If your ratios are tight — common for first-time buyers — an FHA loan in Garland allows more DTI flexibility, and it stacks with the down payment assistance programs available to Garland buyers.

What other exemptions can lower my Garland tax bill?

Homeowners 65 and older receive an added homestead exemption plus a school-tax ceiling that freezes the ISD portion of the bill going forward. Disabled homeowners qualify for a comparable exemption, and disabled veterans can get a partial-to-total exemption based on VA disability rating. Every Garland owner can also protest the appraised value with DCAD during the spring window — I've seen Garland clients lower their assessment 6–10% at the informal hearing by bringing three or four recent comparable sales from their own street, which permanently reduces the escrow.

How do I keep my Garland mortgage payment manageable despite the 2.0% rate?

For the first-time buyers I work with most in Garland, the biggest lever is program stacking. An FHA loan at 3.5% down keeps your cash to close low, and Texas down payment assistance — TSAHC and TDHCA both serve Dallas County — can cover most of the remaining down payment and closing costs, so more of your monthly budget stays available for the tax and insurance escrow. On top of that, file the homestead exemption in your first 90 days for the year-two escrow reduction, and protest the DCAD appraised value each spring with comparable sales from your street. A seller-funded temporary buydown is also common on Garland contracts where a listing has been active a few weeks. Combined, these steps can offset a real portion of the gap between Dallas County's rate and the lower-tax counties to the north.

A recent Garland example

A few months ago I worked with a renter in Garland who had been paying $1,975 a month and assumed buying was out of reach. We found a $310,000 home in Garland ISD, structured an FHA loan with 3.5% down, and stacked a Texas down payment assistance grant to cover most of the cash to close. The Dallas County tax escrow came to about $517/month, and her full PITI landed near $2,510 — more than her rent, but with a fixed rate and the homestead exemption she filed the first week, her year-two payment adjusted down. She's building equity now instead of watching rent climb.
