Does Overtime Count for a Mortgage? What “No Tax on Overtime” and Tips Mean for Texas Buyers in 2026

Income & DTIBy Bond Peter NjokuOctober 20, 20268 min read

I'm Bond Peter Njoku, a mortgage loan officer in Garland, and a lot of the buyers I work with in Garland, Mesquite and Irving earn real money from overtime and tips — warehouse and logistics shifts, nursing, restaurant and bar work, trades. Since the 2025 tax law created the "no tax on overtime" and "no tax on tips" deductions, I keep hearing the same hope: "Now that my overtime isn't taxed, can I qualify for a bigger house?" The honest answer is: the deduction doesn't raise your qualifying income at all — but the overtime and tips themselves often do, if you document them right.

The pages ranking for this question split in two. Tax sites explain the deduction and never mention mortgages. Mortgage sites explain overtime averaging and haven't been updated for the new deductions. This post connects the two, using Fannie Mae's current guideline (Selling Guide B3-3.3-02, dated March 4, 2026) and a worked example at today's 7.40% rate.

Does "no tax on overtime" increase how much house I can afford?

Not directly. Mortgage qualifying uses your gross income — the pay before taxes — divided against your monthly debts to get your debt-to-income ratio (DTI). The new deductions lower your taxable income on your federal return. They don't change what's on your pay stub or W-2 as gross pay, so your DTI is the same either way.

Where the deduction does help: a bigger refund or lower tax bill means more cash for your down payment, closing costs or reserves. If you're expecting a refund next spring, that money can be part of your plan — it just has to land in your account and be documented like any other savings.

Does overtime count as income for a mortgage?

Yes — and for many hourly workers it's the difference between qualifying and not. Here's how the major loan types handle it:

RuleConventional (Fannie Mae)FHA
HistoryTwo years recommended; at least 12 months can work with positive factorsTwo years generally; a shorter history needs the lender to document why it will continue
How it's calculatedAverage year-to-date and prior-year earnings; at least 12 months of income in the averageAveraged over the history period
Declining trendIncome must have stabilized; then use only the post-decline earningsLender must justify using declining income, often uses the lower figure

Tips not on W-2Two years of tax returns with IRS Form 4137 can document themMust be documented; reported income is far easier to use

Fannie Mae doesn't require a separate verification that overtime will continue unless the lender has a reason to doubt it — like an employer telling the verifier overtime is being cut. If your employer has announced overtime cuts, tell me up front. It's far better to plan around it than to have it surface in the final verification.

How do lenders calculate overtime income for a mortgage?

Here's the exact method I use, step by step, for a buyer whose overtime is steady or rising:

  1. Take this year's overtime from your most recent pay stub (year-to-date).
  2. Take last year's overtime from your W-2 or employer breakdown.
  3. Add them and divide by the total months covered.
  4. Compare year-to-date against last year. If this year is running lower, the underwriter looks harder — and may use only the lower, more recent figure, or none at all if it hasn't stabilized.

Example: $18,000 of overtime in 2025, plus $15,000 from January through September 2026 = $33,000 over 21 months = $1,571 a month of qualifying overtime. Notice it's the full overtime pay that counts toward income — not just the "premium" half that the new tax deduction uses.

Can I use tip income to buy a house in Texas?

Yes, if it's documented. Tips that run through payroll show up on your pay stubs and W-2 and are treated like other variable income — averaged over your history. Cash tips you report on your tax return using IRS Form 4137 can also count under Fannie Mae's guideline, using two years of tax returns. Tips that never show up anywhere can't be used. Starting in 2026, employers report qualified tips separately on W-2s, which actually makes them easier to document.

One caution for servers and bartenders: underwriters compare this year to last year. A slow summer or a restaurant that changed ownership can look like a "declining trend." If your tips dipped for a reason that's over — a remodel closure, an injury — tell me so we can document it. Fannie's guideline allows a lender to exclude a period when a documented, non-recurring event outside your control temporarily kept you from earning.

A Garland warehouse supervisor who almost under-qualified himself

A buyer I worked with this year — a warehouse shift supervisor in Garland — got pre-qualified online on his base pay alone and was told he could afford about $190,000. He'd been working steady overtime for more than two years and assumed it "didn't count" because it varied week to week. When we pulled his W-2s and current stub, his overtime averaged about $1,500 a month and was rising. Counting it properly moved his range into the $270,000s, and he bought a three-bedroom with an FHA loan instead of renting another year.

The thing that made it work: two full years of history, a year-to-date number running ahead of last year, and an employer verification that didn't flag any cuts. If he'd changed jobs six months earlier, we'd have had a much harder conversation — see my notes on qualifying after a new job.

How do I get my overtime or tip income ready for a mortgage?

  • Keep your last two W-2s and your most recent 30 days of pay stubs.
  • If you're paid cash tips, report them on your tax return — unreported tips can't help you qualify.
  • Avoid changing jobs in the year before you buy if overtime or tips are a big part of your pay.
  • If overtime dropped for a temporary reason, write it down now while the details are fresh.
  • Ask for a full pre-approval, not an online pre-qualification that only looks at base pay.
