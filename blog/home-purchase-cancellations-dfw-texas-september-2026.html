1 in 7 DFW Home Sales Fell Through in September 2026: What It Means for Buyers

Market UpdateBy Bond Peter NjokuOctober 13, 20267 min read

Redfin published its September 2026 contract cancellation data last week, and the numbers paint a clear picture of where DFW's housing market stands right now. Nationally, 14.5% of homebuying deals that went under contract in September were canceled — the highest rate since March and April 2020 at the start of the pandemic. Pending home sales fell 4% from the prior month, the biggest monthly drop in more than three years, and are now at their lowest level since spring 2014.

In Dallas and Fort Worth specifically, 15.9% of contracts fell through in September — meaning roughly 1 in 6 DFW homes that went under contract didn't make it to closing. Houston was higher at 17.3%, and San Antonio hit 18.5%. For context, a year ago the national average was closer to 15%, so this represents a meaningful deterioration in deal flow.

What does this mean for you as a DFW buyer? It means you have more leverage than any time in the past four years — but you have to know how to use it. And it means the financing side of a deal has become more important than ever, because a large portion of these cancellations are driven by payment shock from rates at 7.40%.

Why are so many DFW contracts falling through in 2026?

Redfin's analysis points to three main drivers, and as a DFW loan officer I see all three regularly:

1. Payment shock from 7.40% rates. Buyers get pre-approved months ago at 6.5% or lower. They find a home, fall in love with it, and go under contract. Then they come back to lock their rate and discover the market has moved — their $1,800/month payment estimate is now $2,100. Some buyers have the budget flexibility to absorb that. Others don't, and they terminate during the option period. The fix: work with a loan officer who quotes you based on today's actual rate, not an optimistic projection from three months ago.

2. Inspection findings in an inventory-rich market. When buyers had no other options (2021–2022), they waived inspections or accepted homes in rough condition. Today, with inventory up across DFW and sellers competing for attention, buyers are holding sellers to a higher standard. Inspection findings that would have been accepted two years ago are now grounds for termination or renegotiation.

3. More choices = less urgency to settle. A buyer who finds something they don't love completely knows there are 15 other homes in a similar price range in McKinney. The mindset shift from "I'll take what I can get" to "I'll wait for what I actually want" is real, and it shows up in these cancellation numbers.

How does buyer leverage from cancellations translate into real negotiating power?

A 15.9% cancellation rate tells a seller something they feel viscerally but may not put into numbers: roughly 1 in 6 buyers who make an offer on their home will back out before closing. That's a real risk they have to price into their asking price and their willingness to negotiate.

Here's how I help buyers translate this market reality into concrete dollars:

Seller concessions for rate buydowns. With rates at 7.40%, the single most effective thing a seller can offer is a concession that funds a temporary or permanent interest rate buydown. On a $310,000 purchase price, a 3% seller concession ($9,300) can fund a 2-1 buydown that reduces the first-year rate to 5.40% and the second-year rate to 6.40% before settling at the note rate of 7.40% in year three. That initial payment reduction can be $300–$400/month — often the difference between a buyer who's comfortable and one who backs out at the last minute.

I recently helped a Garland couple navigate exactly this scenario. They were under contract at $317,000 on a 4-bedroom in Garland. Their payment at 7.40% on a 30-year FHA came back at $2,209/month PITI — about $180 more than they were budgeting. We requested a $9,510 seller concession (3%), which the seller agreed to given the current market. We used that to fund a 2-1 buydown, bringing their year-one payment down to $1,969/month PITI. The deal closed. Everyone won.

What protects your earnest money if you need to back out?

Texas residential contracts (TREC forms) include two key buyer protections that most buyers don't fully understand until it matters:

The Option Period. Typically 5–10 days from contract execution, the option period allows you to terminate for any reason and receive your earnest money back in full. You pay an option fee to the seller (usually $100–$500) which is non-refundable — but the earnest money (typically $1,000–$3,000 or 1% of purchase price) comes back to you. This period is specifically designed for inspection review, and it's where most legitimate cancellations happen.

The Financing Contingency (Paragraph 4). If you cannot obtain financing approval on the terms specified in the contract, and you applied in good faith, you can terminate and recover your earnest money. This protects buyers against legitimate financing failures — but it does not protect you if you didn't actually apply, or if you're backing out because you changed your mind and want to claim a financing issue.
