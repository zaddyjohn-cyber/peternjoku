DFW Property Tax Protest Guide 2026–2027: Lower Your Mortgage Payment by Appealing Your Assessment

Homeowner FinancesBy Bond Peter NjokuOctober 15, 20267 min read

Most of my clients ask about interest rates, down payments, and credit scores. Almost none of them ask about property tax protests — even though a successful protest can do more for their monthly budget than a 0.25% rate drop. If you're a DFW homeowner and you've never protested your property tax assessment, this is the post I've been meaning to write.

I'm Bond Peter Njoku, a licensed Mortgage Loan Officer (NMLS #2670329) based in Garland. I'm not a property tax consultant, but as someone who works with DFW homeowners on mortgage payments every day, I see directly how property taxes affect PITI — and how a successful protest changes the math. Here's what you need to know heading into the 2027 protest cycle.

Why a mortgage officer is writing about property tax protests

Your monthly mortgage payment isn't just principal and interest. It includes PITI:

  • P — Principal (reduces your balance)
  • I — Interest (cost of borrowing)
  • T — Taxes (collected monthly into your escrow account)
  • I — Insurance (homeowners insurance, also escrowed)

The T portion — taxes — is determined by your county appraisal district's assessed value of your home. If you can lower that assessed value through a successful protest, your annual property tax bill goes down, your monthly escrow contribution decreases, and your total PITI payment drops. A $6,000 reduction in annual property taxes saves you exactly $500/month. On a $350,000 Garland home, that could mean the difference between qualifying for a loan and not qualifying.

Important timing note: the 2026 protest window has closed

The 2026 property tax protest deadline was May 15, 2026 (or 30 days after you received your notice of appraised value, whichever was later). That window is closed. If you didn't protest for 2026, nothing to do there now.

But here's why October is exactly the right time to be reading this: the best time to prepare for the 2027 protest is right now, before the next assessment cycle starts. County appraisal districts set assessed values based on market conditions as of January 1 of each tax year — so the comparable sales from July–December 2026 will directly influence your 2027 assessment. Gathering evidence now means you're ready the moment your 2027 notice of appraised value arrives in late March or April 2027.

How the DFW property tax protest process works

  1. Receive your Notice of Appraised Value (March–April 2027): The county appraisal district mails this to you. It shows the appraised value of your property for the 2027 tax year. Compare it to what you believe your home is worth based on comparable sales.
  2. File your protest (before May 15, 2027, or 30 days after notice, whichever is later): You can file online through your county CAD's portal. Filing is free. You don't need an agent or consultant — thousands of Texas homeowners successfully protest their own assessments every year.
  3. Informal hearing with a CAD appraiser: Before your formal hearing, a CAD appraiser may contact you to settle informally. Many protests are resolved here. If the CAD offers a reduction that seems fair, you can accept it and skip the formal hearing.
  4. Formal hearing before the Appraisal Review Board (ARB): If you don't settle informally, you present your evidence to the ARB — a three-member independent panel. Show your comps, explain your reasoning, answer questions. The ARB cannot raise your value above the original CAD assessment. You can only win or draw.
  5. If needed — district court or binding arbitration: If you're unsatisfied with the ARB's decision, you can escalate to district court or binding arbitration (for properties valued under $5 million). Most residential cases don't get here.

Evidence to gather starting now (Q4 2026 → ready for 2027)

The evidence that wins property tax protests is comparable sales — homes similar to yours that sold for less than your assessed value. The CAD uses sales from the previous 12–18 months to set the next year's values. Here's what to collect starting today:

  • Sold comparable listings: Find 3–5 homes in your immediate neighborhood (ideally within 0.5 miles, similar square footage ±200 sqft, same age range, similar lot size) that sold between July 2025 and January 2026. You can pull these for free from Zillow's sold history, HAR.com, or your county CAD's sales data. Print each comp with its sale date, address, square footage, and price.
  • Recent appraisal report: If you had an appraisal done for a refinance or purchase in 2025 or 2026, keep that report. It shows a licensed appraiser's independent assessment of your home's value.
  • Photos of deferred maintenance or damage: Roof condition, HVAC age, foundation issues, water damage — anything that negatively affects value should be documented with photos and contractor estimates.
  • Closing disclosure: If you purchased the home in 2025 or 2026 for less than the current assessed value, your closing disclosure is evidence that the market bore out a lower value.

A Garland homeowner who cut $200/month from their escrow: how it worked

I worked with a client in Garland who bought a 1,850 sqft home in 2024 for $295,000. By early 2026, the county had assessed it at $328,000 — an increase of $33,000. They filed a protest in April 2026 (before the May 15 deadline) and went to an informal hearing with three comps showing similar homes in the same ZIP code selling for $295,000–$308,000 in late 2025.

The CAD settled informally at $302,000 — a $26,000 reduction from the $328,000 assessment. At Dallas County's effective tax rate of roughly 2.2%, that $26,000 reduction cut their annual property tax bill by approximately $572, or about $48/month. Not life-changing, but meaningful — and the informal hearing took 15 minutes. They wish they'd done it the year before too.

Dallas County 2026: escrow note on delayed tax bills

Dallas County had administrative delays in 2026 related to the Prop A tax bill situation. If you have a mortgage with an escrow account and haven't received a 2026 tax bill yet, your servicer is likely estimating your escrow based on 2025 amounts. Once the actual 2026 bills are processed, expect a potential escrow adjustment notice. If your 2026 tax bill came in higher than your servicer estimated, you'll see a shortage in early 2027 and your monthly payment will adjust upward to replenish the escrow. This is normal — but it's worth reviewing your escrow statement when it arrives.
