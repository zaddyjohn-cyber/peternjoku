VA Appraisal Rules Changed in 2026: What Texas Veterans Buying in DFW Need to Know

VA LoansBy Bond Peter NjokuOctober 22, 20267 min read

I'm Bond Peter Njoku, a mortgage loan officer in Garland, and for years the VA appraisal has been the part of a VA purchase that made listing agents nervous. Sellers heard "VA" and pictured a list of required repairs. In 2026 that list got shorter. On February 27, 2026, VA issued Change 46 to VA Pamphlet 26-7, its Lender's Handbook, removing five minimum property requirements (MPRs) the agency called "superfluous." The change applies to VA appraisals ordered after May 1, 2026.

Most of what you'll find about this online is national lender marketing. This post is for Texas veterans buying in Dallas-Fort Worth: what was removed, what wasn't, and why your Texas option period matters even more now.

What exactly did VA change about appraisals in 2026?

I read the transmittal itself rather than relying on summaries. It lists five changes, all in Chapter 12 (Minimum Property Requirements):

Handbook topicOld rule (before Change 46)New rule (appraisals ordered after May 1, 2026)What it means in DFW
Topic 1 (g) Detached improvementsSheds, detached garages and workshops had to meet MPRsRemovedA rotting shed on a Forney or Terrell lot no longer has to be repaired or torn down just to close
Topic 1 (i) SAH RLC jurisdictionSpecially adapted housing rules referenced regional loan center jurisdictionRemovedAdministrative; little effect on most buyers
Topic 23 (b) Non-vented heatersUnvented gas heaters/fireplaces needed paperwork and an oxygen depletion sensorRemovedOlder Garland, Mesquite and Oak Cliff homes with vent-free gas logs stop being a paperwork problem
Topic 32 (a) Paint, homes built 1978 or laterDefective paint could be a required repairCondensed; treated as cosmeticPeeling exterior paint on a 1990s Rowlett house is no longer a VA condition by itself
Topic 32 (b) Paint, homes built before 1978Defective paint on the home "or related improvements" had to be corrected"Or related improvements" removed; lead-paint rule on the home still appliesPre-1978 homes still need defective paint fixed, but not on every fence and outbuilding
Topic 34 Radon gasStandalone radon section (including new-construction certifications)Removed entirelyVA no longer requires radon steps. Testing is still your choice

Timing is based on the date the appraisal is ordered, not your contract date or closing date. By October 2026 nearly every new VA purchase falls under the new rules. If you're refinancing or reusing an older notice of value, ask me which rule set applies.

What did NOT change in the VA appraisal?

Some buyers hear "VA loosened appraisals" and think the property doesn't matter anymore. It still does. VA still guarantees only homes that are safe, sanitary and structurally sound. In DFW, these are the items I still see turn into conditions:

  • Roof: active leaks, missing shingles, or a roof with no remaining life. Hail is a constant issue across Collin, Dallas and Tarrant counties.
  • Foundation: visible settlement, separating brick, or doors that won't latch, which are common with North Texas clay soil. See my guide to buying a DFW house with foundation problems.
  • Heating, water and electrical: working heat, safe wiring, and a safe water supply. Homes on wells and septic systems in Kaufman and Ellis counties still have extra requirements.
  • Pre-1978 paint: peeling or chipping paint on the house itself still has to be corrected.
  • Value: the appraisal still has to support the price. If it comes in low, the Tidewater process and your options are the same as before. My low appraisal guide walks through them.

Does this make VA offers more competitive in Dallas-Fort Worth?

Somewhat, and that matters in a market where sellers still worry about VA buyers. The two fears I hear most from listing agents are "the appraiser will make us fix everything" and "the buyer can't pay closing costs." Change 46 addresses part of the first one. On the second, VA buyers can receive seller concessions up to 4% plus normal closing costs, and since 2024 veterans have been allowed to pay their own buyer-agent fee under VA's rules.

My advice to agents who represent veterans: say in the offer that the appraisal will be ordered under the post-May 2026 property requirements. It's one line, and it answers the listing agent's question before they ask it.

Why your Texas option period matters more now

The VA appraisal was never a home inspection. Now it checks even less, so more of the job of finding problems falls on you. In Texas, that's what the option period is for. You pay a small option fee for the unrestricted right to terminate within the agreed number of days (my earnest money and option fee guide explains the mechanics), and during that window you hire a TREC-licensed inspector.

Before Change 46, the appraiser might have flagged an unvented heater or a failing detached garage for you. Now they won't. If you care about those items, they have to be in your inspection report and your repair negotiation. Radon is worth a separate note: North Texas generally isn't a high-radon area, but a short-term test costs little if your inspector offers one, and VA no longer prompts it either way.

Veterans with a service-connected disability rating of 10% or more are generally exempt from the funding fee, which takes about $7,300 off this loan and roughly $50 a month off the payment. Closing costs typically run 2–3% of the price, about $6,800 to $10,200 here, and are often covered by seller concessions. My VA closing cost guide for Texas breaks those down.

A Garland example: the gas logs that used to be a problem

Earlier this year I worked with an Army veteran and his wife buying a 1984 brick home in Garland for about $315,000. His credit was in the mid-600s, he had a 30% disability rating (so no funding fee), and the house had original vent-free gas logs in the den plus a detached workshop with a sagging roof.

Under the old handbook, both could have come back as conditions: paperwork and a sensor certification for the heater, and repairs or removal for the workshop. The appraisal was ordered after May 1, so neither was a VA issue. Their inspector still flagged the gas logs, and the buyers negotiated a $1,500 price reduction during the option period and had a plumber inspect them after closing. They closed on time with no repair escrow, and the seller never had to bring a contractor out before closing.

Is the VA Appraisal Modernization Act a law yet?

No. Senators Duckworth, Sheehy and Banks introduced S. 4218, the VA Appraisal Modernization Act, to address the shortage of VA appraisers. It's a bill, not law. I'll update this post if it passes. Until then, appraisal turn times are the issue I watch most. In parts of DFW, VA appraisals can take longer than conventional ones, so I order them as soon as the option period ends.

Frequently Asked Questions

What changed with VA appraisals in 2026?

I'm Bond Peter Njoku (NMLS #2670329). VA's Change 46 to the Lender's Handbook, issued February 27, 2026, removed five minimum property requirements for appraisals ordered after May 1, 2026. Radon gas, non-vented heaters, detached improvements like sheds and detached garages, and the specially adapted housing jurisdiction note were removed, and the paint rule for homes built in 1978 or later was condensed so peeling paint is treated as cosmetic. Pre-1978 homes still need defective paint on the house corrected. Call or text me at 469-545-7180 and I'll tell you which rules apply to your appraisal.

Does VA still require a home to pass inspection in Texas?

I'm Bond Peter Njoku (NMLS #2670329), and VA has never required a home inspection, but it still requires the home to be safe, sanitary and structurally sound. The appraiser still checks the roof, foundation, heat, water and electrical systems, and anything serious can become a required repair. Because the appraisal now checks fewer items, I tell every Texas veteran to hire a TREC-licensed inspector during the option period. Call or text me at 469-545-7180 before you sign so the option period is long enough for inspection and negotiation.

Do I still need a radon test for a VA loan in DFW?

I'm Bond Peter Njoku (NMLS #2670329). For VA appraisals ordered after May 1, 2026, VA no longer has a radon requirement, including the old certifications for new construction. North Texas is generally not a high-radon region, but testing is inexpensive and still your call. Ask your inspector during the option period if you want peace of mind. Call or text me at 469-545-7180 with any questions about your VA purchase.

Will a shed or detached garage hold up my VA loan closing?

I'm Bond Peter Njoku (NMLS #2670329), and for appraisals ordered after May 1, 2026, non-living detached structures no longer have to meet VA's minimum property requirements, so a worn shed or detached garage generally won't be a required repair. The appraiser can still note a genuine safety hazard, and it can still affect value. This matters a lot for veterans buying on larger lots in Forney, Terrell or Royse City.
