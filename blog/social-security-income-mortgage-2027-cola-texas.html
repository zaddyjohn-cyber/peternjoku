Buying a Home on Social Security in Texas: How Lenders Count Your Benefit and When the 2027 COLA Counts

Income & QualifyingBy Bond Peter NjokuOctober 14, 20266 min read

The Social Security Administration announces the 2027 cost-of-living adjustment (COLA) on October 14, 2026, the same morning the September CPI report comes out. Forecasts have been between 3.2% and 3.6%, compared with 2.8% for 2026. If you're a retiree planning to buy a home in Rockwall, McKinney, Garland or anywhere else in DFW, that raise affects how much house you qualify for, but only from the date your new benefit is documented. I'm Bond Peter Njoku (NMLS #2670329). Below I explain how lenders count Social Security income, how the 25% gross-up actually works, and whether to close before or after the COLA takes effect.

Can I get a mortgage on Social Security income in Texas?

Yes. Social Security retirement, disability and survivor benefits all count as qualifying income on conventional, FHA, VA and USDA loans. Two points borrowers often don't know:

  • There's no age limit. Federal fair-lending law (the Equal Credit Opportunity Act) bars lenders from refusing you, or shortening your term, because of your age. A 74-year-old can take a 30-year fixed loan.
  • There's no minimum history. Fannie Mae's Social Security section (B3-3.4-15, updated October 7, 2026) requires no minimum receipt history. A benefit that starts on or before your first mortgage payment can be documented with the award letter.

Does Social Security have to continue for 3 years to count?

It depends on whose work record the benefit comes from:

Benefit typeContinuance required (Fannie Mae)Typical documents
Retirement or long-term disability on your own recordNone, unless the lender has reason to think it will stopSSA award letter, or SSA-1099 / tax returns, or proof of current receipt
Retirement on another person's record (e.g. spousal)3 years from the application dateAward letter + proof of current receipt + continuance
Survivor benefits, benefits for a minor child3 years from the application date (age can be used to prove it)Award letter + proof of receipt
SSIDocumented per benefit typeAward letter + proof of receipt

Fannie Mae's October 7, 2026 update (SEL-2026-09) changed the three-year continuance clock to run from the application date. I covered the other changes in that update in my post on Fannie Mae's October 2026 income rules. If you get survivor benefits for a child who turns 18 within three years, that income usually can't be counted.

How does the Social Security "gross-up" really work?

Many websites say lenders add 25% to your whole Social Security check. That isn't how Fannie Mae's rule works. Lenders may gross up the nontaxable part of your income, because a nontaxable dollar goes further than a taxable one. For Social Security, Fannie Mae lets the lender treat 15% of the benefit as nontaxable with no documentation, then gross that 15% up by 25%. The net effect is about 3.75% added to your benefit:

Monthly benefit15% treated as nontaxable25% gross-upQualifying income
$1,500$225+$56$1,556
$2,400$360+$90$2,490
$3,000$450+$113$3,113
$3,900 (couple)$585+$146$4,046

If your tax returns show that more than 15% of your benefit is nontaxable, which is common for retirees with little other income, the lender can gross up the documented nontaxable amount instead. When the whole benefit is nontaxable, a $3,000 check can count as $3,750. FHA, VA and USDA each have their own gross-up rules, and FHA's percentage is smaller, so I compare loan programs before deciding which one to use. Lenders also generally work from the gross benefit on your award letter, before Medicare Part B is deducted.

Should I wait for the 2027 COLA before buying a house?

The COLA applies to benefits paid starting January 2027. SSA usually posts the new benefit notice in your my Social Security account in early December. Your lender can only count the higher amount once it's documented, so timing matters:

Closing timingBenefit amount usedWhat to do
Before your 2027 notice is issued (Oct–early Dec 2026)Current 2026 benefitQualify on today's figure. Don't count on the raise.
After the notice is issued, before January payment2027 benefit may be usable with the new noticeDownload the notice from my Social Security and send it to me right away
January 2027 or later2027 benefit, confirmed by the notice and bank depositProvide the new notice + a statement showing the deposit

How much does the raise add? On a couple's $3,900 combined benefit, a 3.5% COLA adds about $137 a month, or about $142 after the gross-up. At a 45% debt-to-income ratio, that supports about $64 a month more in house payment, roughly $9,000 more loan at 7.40%. It helps, but it rarely decides whether you can buy. If you've found the right house now, the COLA usually isn't a good reason to wait. A rate move of a quarter point on the same loan costs or saves more than that. I compare the two in should I wait for rates to drop.

How much house can a retiree qualify for on Social Security in DFW?

Here is a composite of a Rockwall couple I worked with this fall. She's 68 and he's 66. Their Social Security is $2,400 and $1,500 a month, and he has a $1,800-a-month pension. They sold their Garland home and brought $130,000 to the purchase of a $380,000 home near Lake Ray Hubbard.

  • Qualifying income: $3,900 Social Security grossed up to $4,046, plus $1,800 pension = $5,846/mo
  • Loan: $250,000 conventional at 7.40%: principal and interest about $1,731
  • Property tax: Rockwall County ~1.8%, about $570/mo (before over-65 exemptions, which will lower it)
  • Homeowners insurance: about $280/mo
  • Total PITI: about $2,581/mo, a 44.1% debt-to-income ratio with no other debts

That's an approval under Fannie Mae's automated underwriting, but with little room to spare. What made it comfortable was something many retirees don't know about: they also had $310,000 in IRAs. Fannie Mae lets lenders turn retirement assets into qualifying income without you withdrawing anything. I explain that in how to qualify for a mortgage using your 401(k) or IRA. And once they file for the Texas homestead exemption and over-65 exemption, their school taxes are frozen at the first year's level, which lowers their escrow too. More on Rockwall in my Rockwall mortgage guide.

What documents do I need for a mortgage on Social Security income?

  1. Your most recent SSA award letter or annual benefit notice (download it from ssa.gov/myaccount)
  2. Your SSA-1099 for last year, or federal tax returns if you want a larger gross-up
  3. A recent bank statement showing the deposit
  4. For survivor or spousal benefits: proof the benefit continues 3+ years (age of beneficiary, etc.)
  5. Statements for any pension, annuity, IRA or 401(k) you want counted

Then the steps are the same as for any buyer. Start with a pre-approval so you know your number before you start looking.
