FOMC October 28, 2026: Will the Fed Hold Rates — And Does It Matter for DFW Buyers at 7.40%?

Rate UpdateBy Bond Peter NjokuOctober 13, 20268 min read

The Federal Open Market Committee meets October 27–28, and DFW buyers and homeowners with floating rates are watching closely. But here's the honest take before the meeting: a Fed hold on October 28 is very likely — and it may not move mortgage rates meaningfully in either direction. Here's why, and what DFW buyers should actually be doing right now.

What is the current Fed funds rate and what will happen on October 28?

The Fed's current target rate range is 3.75–4.00%, following a unanimous 25-basis-point hike at the September 16 meeting — the first increase since July 2023. That hike came after persistent inflation kept the Fed in a cautious stance through 2026's first half.

For the October 27–28 meeting, market pricing tells the clearest story. As of October 12–13, CME FedWatch and prediction market tools show approximately:

  • 81–83% probability: Hold (rates remain at 3.75–4.00%)
  • 16–18% probability: Hike (25bp increase to 4.00–4.25%)
  • ~1% probability: Cut

Hike odds dropped sharply from ~73% just two weeks ago after the September jobs report showed only 29,000 jobs added — well below the 80,000+ economists expected. New York Fed President John Williams said publicly that policymakers "have time to gather more information" and "there is no need for urgency." That's Fed-speak for: we're probably holding in October.

Importantly, this is not a Summary of Economic Projections (dot plot) meeting. The March, June, September, and December meetings include updated projections; October does not. So there will be no new dot plot to read through for signals on 2027 rate expectations — only Powell's press conference at 2:30 PM ET on October 28.

Why a Fed hold doesn't mean mortgage rates drop

This is the most important thing I explain to DFW buyers who are waiting for the Fed to "do something" before locking. The confusion stems from a common misunderstanding about how mortgage rates work.

The Fed controls the overnight federal funds rate — the rate banks charge each other for very short-term loans. The 30-year fixed mortgage rate, by contrast, tracks the 10-year U.S. Treasury yield. These are related but independent. You can have the Fed funds rate at 3.75% while the 10-year Treasury sits at 5.28% — which is exactly where we are right now.

The 10-year Treasury at 5.28% (its highest level since 2002) is being driven by factors that have nothing to do with what the Fed decides on October 28:

  • Persistent inflation expectations — investors demand higher yields to compensate for expected price increases
  • U.S. federal deficit — the government is issuing more debt, which competes for capital and pressures yields higher
  • Weak Treasury auction demand — recent auctions have underperformed, signaling lower appetite for U.S. debt at current yields
  • Global bond selloff — yields rising internationally force U.S. yields to stay competitive to attract capital
  • AI and hyperscaler investment spending — private sector demand for capital competes with Treasury demand

None of those factors disappear with a Fed hold. The 10-year yield — and therefore mortgage rates — can stay elevated even as the Fed keeps its thumb off the rate hike button.

Should DFW buyers lock their rate before or after October 28?

For buyers closing within 30–45 days of October 28, here's my honest assessment:

The case for locking before October 28: A Fed hold is already priced into the market — it likely produces little downward movement in mortgage rates. A surprise hike (still 17–18% odds) could push rates up 25–50 basis points quickly. The asymmetry is unfavorable for floating: you're risking a meaningful increase to capture a small potential benefit. With rates at 7.40% — already a 7-year high — the downside scenario is worse than the upside.

The case for floating past October 28: If the October CPI (around November 12) comes in softer than expected, Treasury yields could pull back and mortgage rates could drop toward 7.00–7.15% by mid-November. If your closing is in December and you can tolerate the uncertainty, floating through November is a calculated bet. But you're making a bet.

A McKinney buyer I'm working with is under contract at $425,000 with a November 15 closing. After reviewing the rate landscape, we locked at 7.375% last week. The math: if rates drop 0.25% after October 28, they lose about $37/month — $444/year — on a loan they might refinance in 2–3 years anyway. If rates spike 0.25% and they missed the lock, they'd be paying that same $37/month extra indefinitely. The lock made sense for their timeline and risk tolerance.

What if the Fed surprises with a hike on October 28?

A 25bp hike to 4.00–4.25% would likely push the 10-year Treasury briefly higher and mortgage rates toward 7.60–7.75% within a few days. That's a meaningful move on a $400,000 DFW purchase — approximately $62–$83/month more in P&I. Buyers floating through the meeting without a lock absorb that immediately. Buyers who locked before October 28 are protected.

The jobs data gave the Fed room to hold. But as I covered in my post on the 10-year Treasury and why rates are rising in October 2026, the current rate environment has multiple independent upward pressures that don't disappear with a single data point. The Fed's tone on October 28 will matter as much as the decision itself.
