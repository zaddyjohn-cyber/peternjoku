Buying a Lake House in Texas: Second Home Mortgage Rules, Down Payment, and Payment Math (2026)

Second HomesBy Bond Peter NjokuOctober 24, 20266 min read

I'm Bond Peter Njoku, a mortgage loan officer in Garland. Many Dallas-Fort Worth families dream of a weekend place on Cedar Creek Lake, Lake Texoma, Lake Granbury, Possum Kingdom, or Lake Tawakoni. A lake house can be financed as a second home with as little as 10% down on a conventional loan. That's far less than the 15–25% typical for an investment property. But Texas adds wrinkles that national guides never mention: no homestead exemption on the second home, an 80% cap on tapping your primary home's equity, and flood zones on many lakefront lots.

What counts as a second home for a mortgage?

On a conventional loan, a second home must be a one-unit property you occupy for part of the year, that you have exclusive control over, and that is suitable for year-round use. It can't be a timeshare, and it can't be under a rental management agreement that gives someone else control of occupancy. If that's the plan, it's an investment property, with different pricing and down payment.

A national guide that ranks well for this topic says second homes can be rented "up to 180 days per year." That isn't a Fannie Mae rule. Occasional rental is allowed, but the income can't be used to qualify, and if the property operates like a full-time rental, it's an investment property. I underwrite to the actual guideline, not the rule of thumb.

Why do second-home rates cost more?

Fannie Mae and Freddie Mac charge loan-level price adjustments on second homes. Under the grid introduced in 2022 (Fannie Mae Lender Letter LL-2022-01), the second-home adjustment runs from 1.125% of the loan amount at 60% LTV or less to about 4.125% at the highest LTVs. Lenders usually build that into the rate, which is why second-home quotes often come in roughly 0.25–0.75% above a primary-home rate. Your Loan Estimate will show the actual pricing. Putting 25% or more down usually gets better pricing than 10%.

What Texas rules affect a lake house loan?

No homestead exemption. Texas allows one homestead. Your lake house pays the full tax rate, without the $140,000 school homestead exemption your primary home gets, and without the 10% annual cap on appraised-value increases. That's why I used a full ~2.0% rate above. See my homestead exemption guide.

Using your primary home's equity has a cap. Many buyers fund the down payment with a HELOC or home equity loan on their DFW home. Because your primary residence is a Texas homestead, those loans are limited to 80% combined loan-to-value under Section 50(a)(6), and the payment on that HELOC counts in your DTI. See Texas HELOC rules.

A later cash-out on the lake house isn't a 50(a)(6) loan. Because the lake house isn't your homestead, a future cash-out refinance on it follows ordinary second-home or investment guidelines, not the Texas home-equity rules.

Flood zones. Many lakefront lots on Texas reservoirs are partly in FEMA flood zones. If any building is in a Special Flood Hazard Area, flood insurance is required, and it can add hundreds of dollars a month. Check the flood zone before your option period ends. See my Texas flood insurance guide.

"Too close to home." A house on Lake Ray Hubbard 20 minutes from your Garland home can still be a second home, but underwriters will ask why you need it. Be ready to explain how you use it, especially if it's in a rental market.

How did a Garland couple qualify for a Cedar Creek Lake house?

This summer I helped a Garland couple buy a $450,000 house on Cedar Creek Lake, about an hour and a half southeast of Dallas. Their combined income was about $16,500 a month. Their Garland home payment was $1,950, they had $600 in other monthly debts, and their credit scores were in the mid-700s.

With the lake house at roughly $3,950 a month, their total DTI came to about 39% ($1,950 + $3,950 + $600 ÷ $16,500), comfortably under the 45% limit. They used savings for the 10% down, and we documented about four months of reserves on both homes. The house was partly in a flood zone, but the home itself sat above it on the elevation certificate, so flood insurance wasn't required. We confirmed that before the option period ended.

How do I start?

  1. Get pre-approved as a second home buyer so both payments are in your DTI from the start.
  2. Decide how you'll fund the down payment: savings, a HELOC on your primary home, or a cash-out refinance.
  3. Check the flood zone and insurance quotes before your option period ends.
  4. Compare 10%, 20%, and 25% down. The pricing differences can be large on second homes.

Frequently Asked Questions

How much do I need to put down on a lake house in Texas?

I'm Bond Peter Njoku (NMLS #2670329), a Garland loan officer. If the lake house qualifies as a second home, a conventional loan can work with as little as 10% down. On a $450,000 home, that's $45,000 plus 2 to 3% in closing costs. Putting down 20 to 25% removes PMI and usually improves pricing. FHA, VA, and USDA loans generally can't be used for second homes. Can I use rental income to qualify for a second home mortgage?

I'm Bond Peter Njoku (NMLS #2670329). No. On a second home, potential rental income can't be used to qualify, so you need to afford both your primary home and the lake house on your current income. Occasional rental is allowed, but a property managed as a full-time rental, especially under a management agreement that controls occupancy, is an investment property. Call or text me at 469-545-7180 and I'll tell you which category your plans fit.

Does a Texas lake house get a homestead exemption?

I'm Bond Peter Njoku (NMLS #2670329). No. Texas allows only one homestead, so your lake house pays property taxes without the $140,000 school homestead exemption and without the 10% annual cap on appraisal increases. That makes the tax bill noticeably higher than on a primary home of the same value. I budget a full county tax rate in second-home payment estimates. Call or text me at 469-545-7180 for a payment estimate on a specific property.

Can I use a HELOC on my DFW home to buy a lake house?

I'm Bond Peter Njoku (NMLS #2670329). Yes, and many of my clients do. Because your primary home is a Texas homestead, a HELOC or home equity loan is limited to 80% combined loan-to-value under Section 50(a)(6), and the HELOC payment counts in your debt-to-income ratio for the lake house loan. I compare it against savings and a cash-out refinance before you decide. Call or text me at 469-545-7180 to run the numbers.
