Hines Brown Ranch in West Fort Worth: What 3,800 New Homes Mean for Buyers and Financing

New ConstructionBy Bond Peter NjokuOctober 22, 20266 min read

I'm Bond Peter Njoku, a mortgage loan officer based in Garland who works with buyers across Tarrant County, and the biggest new-home announcement in Fort Worth this fall is Brown Ranch. Houston-based developer Hines has acquired the 1,476-acre ranch on Fort Worth's western edge and plans about 3,800 single-family homes over roughly 12 years, according to CRE Daily's October 6, 2026 report. The Brown family's broker said homes should sell for about $500,000 on average.

Nearly all of the coverage so far is written for investors and developers. Here's the buyer's side: what we know, what we don't, and how a home in a community like this will actually be financed.

Still not announced: the MUD's name and tax rate, school district assignments, HOA dues, the first-phase opening date and actual base prices. CRE Daily also reported lot sizes of 2,000 to 4,500 square feet. That's small for single-family lots and may refer to home sizes, so confirm with the builders before relying on it. Any specific Brown Ranch price or rate you hear right now is a guess.

How does a MUD tax change my mortgage payment?

A MUD sells bonds to pay for water, sewer, drainage and sometimes roads, then repays them through a property tax on homes inside the district. Your lender includes that tax in escrow just like county and school taxes, so it raises your monthly payment and counts against your debt-to-income ratio. I cover how these districts work in my Texas MUD district guide.

Should I use the builder's lender at Brown Ranch?

Several of the named builders have affiliated or preferred lenders and will offer incentives such as closing cost credits or rate buydowns for using them. Those offers can be good. But the incentive is often tied to the builder's lender, and the builder's lender may price the rate higher to offset it. My builder lender comparison shows how to compare offers on the Loan Estimate.

One builder deserves a note. HistoryMaker Homes, on the Brown Ranch list, was acquired by Century Communities on October 8, 2026. If you're considering a HistoryMaker home anywhere in DFW, read my post on what HistoryMaker buyers should ask after the sale.

New construction also brings a timing issue: if the home won't be finished for six to nine months, ask the builder whether they'll pay for an extended rate lock, and read my rate lock extension guide before agreeing to one.

Should I wait for Brown Ranch or buy now?

Brown Ranch is a long project. Land development and road work come first, so new homes there are likely still a while off, and the community won't be complete for about 12 years. If you need to buy in the next year, existing west-side master plans such as Walsh are where you'll find inventory and builder incentives today. If you're flexible, it's worth watching the first-phase releases, since early sections often come with the strongest incentives.

A west Fort Worth example: the payment quote that left out the MUD

This year I helped a nurse and an electrician compare two new homes west of Fort Worth, both near $480,000. One builder's sales office quoted a payment about $300 a month lower than the other. When I ran both through a Loan Estimate, the cheaper quote had left out the MUD tax and used a 2-1 temporary buydown rate as if it were permanent. With the MUD included at the full note rate, the gap shrank to under $40 a month. They picked the home they actually preferred, used the builder's closing cost credit with my financing, and closed with a payment they'd budgeted for from the start.
