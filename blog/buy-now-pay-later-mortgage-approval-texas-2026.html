Does Buy Now, Pay Later Affect Mortgage Approval? How Texas Underwriters Count Affirm, Klarna and Afterpay in 2026

Credit & DTIBy Bond Peter NjokuOctober 19, 20266 min read

I'm Bond Peter Njoku, a mortgage loan officer in Garland, and buy now, pay later is the debt I most often find in a buyer's file that the buyer forgot to mention. Not because anyone's hiding it — because Affirm, Klarna and Afterpay don't feel like "debt." They feel like a checkout button. Then an underwriter opens two months of bank statements, sees six recurring withdrawals, and asks for every payment schedule.

BNPL rarely sinks a mortgage on its own. But it can push a tight debt-to-income ratio over the line, and it can delay a closing if it shows up late. Here's how underwriters actually find and count it in 2026, the FHA rule most articles leave out, and a worked example on a Mesquite FHA purchase at today's 7.40% rate (Freddie Mac, week of October 15, 2026).

Where does buy now, pay later show up in a mortgage file?

The credit report — sometimes. Reporting is uneven. Affirm reports repayment data to some bureaus; other providers report little or nothing. FICO released BNPL-aware scores (FICO Score 10 BNPL and 10T BNPL) in 2025, but mortgage lenders still run classic FICO versions that don't use BNPL data. FHA is adding VantageScore 4.0 and FICO 10T as acceptable models only for case numbers assigned on or after January 1, 2027, under HUD Mortgagee Letter 2026-11.

The bank statements — almost always. Every lender reviews recent statements to verify your down payment and reserves. Recurring BNPL autopays stand out, and the underwriter will ask for the plan details: payment amount, payments remaining, payoff balance.

HUD flagged exactly this gap in 2025, when it issued a request for information on how BNPL affects FHA borrowers' DTI. I haven't seen a final rule come out of it, so today's rules are the ordinary installment-debt rules below.

That FHA 5% cap is the detail I almost never see in BNPL articles, and it matters because first-time buyers using FHA are the heaviest BNPL users I meet.

How much can BNPL change my mortgage approval? A Mesquite FHA example

Here's a composite of a buyer I worked with this year in Mesquite. Gross income $90,000 a year, or $7,500 a month. Target: a $280,000 home with an FHA loan.

  • Down payment at 3.5%: $9,800. Base loan $270,200, plus 1.75% upfront MIP financed = $274,929.
  • Principal and interest at 7.40%: $1,904
  • Property tax at Dallas County's ~2.0%: $467
  • Homeowners insurance: about $150
  • FHA annual MIP at 0.55%: about $124
  • Total PITI: about $2,645 — a 35.3% front-end ratio

Her other debts: a car payment of $420 and a student loan of $180. Then the BNPL:

Back-end DTI without BNPL: ($2,645 + $420 + $180) ÷ $7,500 = 43.3%.
With the 14-month furniture plan: ($3,245 + $190) ÷ $7,500 = 45.8%.
If all four plans counted (for example, if she'd added one more pay-in-4 and pushed short-term debts over $375): 49.0%.

Her file still approved at 45.8%, but it was closer than it needed to be. She paid the furniture plan's remaining $2,660 balance from savings that were above her required reserves, which brought her back to 43.3% and gave the underwriter one less thing to question.

What should I do with my BNPL plans before applying for a mortgage?

  1. List every plan — provider, monthly payment, payments left, payoff balance. Your provider's app shows all of it.
  2. Pay off long plans first. Anything with more than ten payments left counts on every program.
  3. Let short plans finish. A pay-in-4 with one payment left will be gone before closing.
  4. Don't pay off debt with your down payment. Reserves and cash to close come first; I'll tell you which payoff moves your DTI the most per dollar.
  5. Stop opening new plans 60 days before you apply and until after closing.
  6. Keep the plan statements handy so the underwriter's request is a two-minute email, not a three-day delay.

Can a new BNPL plan during escrow delay my Texas closing?

Yes. Lenders re-verify before closing, and a new financed purchase — the washer and dryer, the patio set, the TV for the new living room — can show up on a final bank statement or a soft credit refresh. In Texas, your Third Party Financing Addendum protects your earnest money only until the financing deadline in the contract. If a new debt forces a re-underwrite after that date and the approval changes, that protection may be gone. Buy the furniture after you have the keys.

Frequently Asked Questions

Does buy now, pay later show up on a mortgage application?

Often not on the credit report, almost always on the bank statements. I'm Bond Peter Njoku (NMLS #2670329), and some BNPL providers report to only some credit bureaus while others don't report at all, so a tri-merge credit report may miss your plans. But underwriters review your recent bank statements, and recurring Affirm, Klarna or Afterpay autopays are easy to spot there. When they see them, they ask for the payment schedule and decide whether to count it in your debt-to-income ratio. Call or text me at 469-545-7180 and I'll review your statements before an underwriter does.

Can BNPL payments be left out of my debt-to-income ratio?

Sometimes. I'm Bond Peter Njoku (NMLS #2670329), and on conventional loans an installment debt with ten or fewer payments remaining may be excluded from DTI, though the lender can still count it if the payment is large enough to affect your ability to pay. On FHA, debts with ten or fewer months left are excluded only if their combined payments are no more than 5% of your gross monthly income. Any plan with more than ten payments left generally counts in full. Call or text me at 469-545-7180 and I'll show you which of your plans count.

Should I pay off buy now, pay later before applying for a mortgage?

If it moves your DTI or it's a long-term plan, usually yes. I'm Bond Peter Njoku (NMLS #2670329), and I'd rather you pay off a 12- or 18-month Affirm plan before we apply than have it push your debt-to-income ratio over a program limit. Small pay-in-4 plans with one or two payments left typically don't matter once they're documented. Don't drain your down payment or reserves to do it — call or text me at 469-545-7180 first and we'll pick which balances to clear.

Can I use buy now, pay later after I'm pre-approved for a mortgage?

Please don't. I'm Bond Peter Njoku (NMLS #2670329), and lenders re-check bank statements and credit before closing, so a new BNPL plan for furniture or appliances between pre-approval and closing can trigger a re-review of your file and, if it's large enough, a higher DTI that changes your approval. Wait until after you've closed and have the keys. Call or text me at 469-545-7180 before any new purchase you plan to finance while under contract.
