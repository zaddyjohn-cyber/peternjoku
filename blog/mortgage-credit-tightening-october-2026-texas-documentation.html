Mortgage Credit Is Tightening in Texas: What Lenders Now Want in Your File (October 2026)

Mortgage ProcessBy Bond Peter NjokuOctober 16, 20268 min read

I'm Bond Peter Njoku, a mortgage loan officer in Garland, and on October 8, 2026 the Mortgage Bankers Association published a number that most borrowers will never see but will absolutely feel: its Mortgage Credit Availability Index fell to 107.1 in September, down 0.2% from 107.3 in August. That index measures how willing lenders are to lend — a higher number means looser credit, and it's benchmarked so that 100 equals March 2012. When it drops, somebody's loan is getting harder to close.

The headline is not the interesting part. The split underneath it is. The Conventional index fell 0.4%. The Government index — FHA, VA and USDA — was completely unchanged. Joel Kan, the MBA's vice president and deputy chief economist, attributed the decline specifically to lenders tightening documentation requirements on conventional loans and reducing the loans they offer for cash-out refinances and investor home purchases. Jumbo credit availability declined for a second straight month.

So if you are a first-time buyer in Mesquite or Garland planning to use FHA, nothing about your approval got harder in September. If you're a self-employed borrower going conventional, or a homeowner wanting cash out, or buying a rental — your file just got more paperwork. Here is exactly what that looks like, and what to have ready.

What documents should I have ready for a Texas mortgage in late 2026?

When Kan says lenders "tightened documentation requirements," this is what it means on the ground. I now ask for the complete set before I submit, rather than letting underwriting ask twice:

  • 30 days of paystubs, consecutive and current.
  • Two years of W-2s. If you're self-employed: two years of personal and business tax returns, all schedules, plus a year-to-date profit and loss statement.
  • Two months of bank statements — every single page. This is the number one reason files get kicked back. If your statement says "Page 4 of 7," underwriting wants all seven, including the blank one.
  • A written letter of explanation for any non-payroll deposit. If $4,000 landed in your account and it wasn't your employer, expect to source it and paper it.
  • Gift documentation. A signed gift letter plus the donor's bank statement showing the money existed before it moved. A letter alone is no longer enough at most investors.
  • Reserve documentation. Retirement and brokerage statements, even on programs that don't require reserves — they are the strongest compensating factor available when a file is marginal.
  • Leases and a rent roll for any property you already own and collect rent on.
  • Letters of explanation for credit events — recent late payments, collections, or a dispute on your report.
  • Photo ID and, if applicable, your non-borrowing spouse's information, because Texas is a community property state and the file needs it.

One more thing that catches people: nearly every lender re-pulls your credit shortly before closing. If you finance furniture or buy a car during your contract period, that re-pull finds it, your debt-to-income recalculates, and in a tightening environment you may no longer fit. Do not open credit between application and funding.

Why does this hit Texas cash-out refinances harder?

Because Texas already has the strictest home equity rules in the country, and fewer lender programs sit on top of those rules rather than replacing them.

Under Section 50(a)(6) of the Texas Constitution, a cash-out refinance on your homestead is capped at 80% of the home's value — not the 85% or 90% available in most states. You must receive a required notice and wait 12 days before closing. Certain fees are capped. And the loan permanently changes the character of your mortgage.

So when the MBA reports that lenders "reduced offerings of loans that allow for cash-out refinances," a Texas homeowner is choosing from a smaller menu of already-restricted products. That's worth knowing before you start, because for a lot of my clients a home equity line turns out to be the better instrument — especially at a 7.40% first-mortgage rate, where refinancing your whole balance to access equity means repricing a loan you may already like.

How much does the rate environment compound this?

Freddie Mac's Primary Mortgage Market Survey put the 30-year fixed at 7.40% for the week of October 15, 2026 — unchanged from the prior week, the highest since November 2023, and up from 6.27% a year earlier. That matters here because tighter documentation and a higher rate squeeze the same number: your debt-to-income ratio.

Do I still qualify if my conventional file gets tighter?

Usually yes — just through a different door. The three moves I make most often right now:

  1. Switch the program, not the house. Government credit availability didn't tighten. An FHA loan at 580+ with 3.5% down, which stacks with Texas down payment assistance, often survives a file that conventional underwriting pushed back on.
  2. Document income a different way. If you're self-employed and the conventional tax-return path isn't producing enough qualifying income, a bank statement loan or a 1099-based program can work — and this is the one segment the MBA says is actually growing.
  3. Strengthen rather than re-apply. Reserves, a co-borrower, paying down one revolving balance, or a modestly larger down payment move a marginal file more reliably than shopping for a lender with looser rules.

A real example from this fall. I worked with a borrower in Mesquite — a self-employed HVAC contractor, credit in the low 700s, about $94,000 of qualifying income on two years of returns, buying at $312,000 with 5% down. His conventional file came back with a second document request: a year-to-date profit and loss, a business bank statement analysis, and sourcing on a $6,800 deposit that turned out to be a customer's equipment deposit. We papered all three, but the underwriter's recalculated income left his DTI at 49% — technically inside guidelines, but tight enough that one surprise would have killed it. We ran FHA in parallel: same house, 3.5% down, DTI at 44%, and he closed in 29 days. The conventional file wasn't wrong. It was just the harder door in September 2026.
