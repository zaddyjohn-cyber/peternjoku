Down Payment Assistance in Plano, TX (2026 Guide)

First-Time BuyersBy Bond Peter NjokuJuly 29, 20268 min read

I'm Bond Peter Njoku, a licensed Mortgage Loan Officer (NMLS #2670329), and Plano buyers ask me the same question almost every week: "Isn't down payment assistance only for lower-priced homes?" It isn't. Plano sits in Collin County, where the income ceiling on these programs is actually higher than in Dallas or Tarrant County, and there's no cap tying the grant to a specific home price beyond standard loan limits.

Why Plano's Income Limits Work in Your Favor

Because Plano is in Collin County, the 2026 income limit for TSAHC and TDHCA assistance sits around $119,700 for a smaller household — notably higher than the roughly $97,200 limit in Dallas and Tarrant counties. That gap is significant for Plano's dual-income professional households, many of whom would get turned away from assistance programs in nearby Dallas County suburbs but qualify here without issue.

Plano's median home price often runs $450,000–$650,000, well above the DFW average, so a 5% grant translates into $22,500–$32,500 — real money on a Plano purchase. I typically pair this assistance with FHA financing for buyers under 680 credit, and with conventional financing for move-up buyers who want to avoid FHA's mortgage insurance premium on a higher loan amount.

A Realistic Plano Scenario

On a $500,000 Plano home, a 5% Home Sweet Texas grant is $25,000. Paired with FHA's 3.5% down requirement of $17,500, that leaves $7,500 in additional funds to apply toward closing costs or a rate buydown. For buyers who prefer conventional financing to skip FHA mortgage insurance, I run a side-by-side comparison with a 3% or 5% conventional down payment structure so you can see which path actually saves more over the life of the loan.
