HOA Dues and Your Mortgage in Texas: Resale Certificates, DTI, and the Fees Buyers Miss (2026)

Buying a HomeBy Bond Peter NjokuOctober 24, 20265 min read

I'm Bond Peter Njoku, a mortgage loan officer in Garland. In much of Dallas-Fort Worth, especially the newer suburbs in Collin, Denton, Rockwall, and Kaufman counties, you'll be hard pressed to find a home without a homeowners association. Buyers usually think about HOA dues as a lifestyle cost. Lenders see them differently: every dollar of HOA dues counts in your debt-to-income ratio, the same as your mortgage payment. At today's 7.40% rate (Freddie Mac PMMS, mid-October 2026), $80 a month in dues reduces your buying power by about $11,500.

The Texas articles about HOA resale certificates are written by management companies and title companies, and they explain the certificate well. None of them explain what the dues do to your mortgage approval. This post covers both.

Do HOA dues count in my debt-to-income ratio?

Yes. Fannie Mae, Freddie Mac, FHA, VA, and USDA all include HOA dues in your monthly housing expense. Lenders call it PITIA: principal, interest, taxes, insurance, and association dues. That number drives both your front-end ratio (housing only) and your back-end ratio (housing plus other debts).

Here's what dues cost in buying power at 7.40% on a 30-year loan, where every $1,000 borrowed costs about $6.92 a month in principal and interest:

Monthly HOA duesAnnualLoan amount you give upWhat that feels like
$40$480~$5,800Older subdivision with a basic pool or entry
$80$960~$11,550Typical suburban HOA with amenities
$125$1,500~$18,050Larger master-planned community
$250$3,000~$36,100Townhome or condo with exterior maintenance

Those are illustrations, not quotes for specific neighborhoods. Dues vary a lot across DFW. If you're close to your maximum DTI, a $125-a-month HOA can be the difference between approval and a counteroffer. See my Texas DTI guide and how Texas property taxes affect DTI.

What is a Texas HOA resale certificate?

Under Texas Property Code §207.003, the association must provide a resale certificate disclosing the key facts about the HOA and the specific property. The seller usually orders it. Here's what the law requires:

ItemTexas rule
Delivery deadline10 business days after a proper written request
Fee cap: original certificate$375
Updated certificate$75 cap; can be requested within 180 days; due within 7 business days
FreshnessCan't be prepared more than 60 days before delivery
Who paysSet by the purchase contract

The certificate must show, among other things: the amount and frequency of regular assessments, approved special assessments, any unpaid amounts on the property, reserves for capital expenses, the operating budget and balance sheet, unsatisfied judgments and pending lawsuits against the association, the common-area insurance certificate, known violations on the property, transfer fees, and whether the HOA can foreclose for unpaid assessments.

What in the resale certificate matters to my lender?

  • The regular assessment amount: this is the number that goes into your DTI. If it's higher than the listing said, your approval needs to be re-run.
  • Special assessments: an approved $3,000 roof or pool assessment can become a cost to you, and a large one can affect underwriting.
  • Unpaid dues on the property: these have to be paid off at closing, usually by the seller.
  • Pending lawsuits: these matter most for condos, where lenders review the project. See Texas condo mortgage rules.
  • Known violations: an unapproved fence or shed can tie into a survey issue.

Can I back out after I read the HOA documents?

Usually, yes. The TREC addendum for property subject to mandatory HOA membership lets you choose how the subdivision information is delivered. Under its standard terms, you can terminate within a short window after you receive the information (three days on the current form) and get your earnest money back. If the documents are never delivered, you can terminate before closing. Read the addendum your agent uses, because the choices you check decide your deadline. Ask for the documents early, especially if your option period is short.

What HOA fees will I pay at closing?

Expect some combination of these on your Closing Disclosure:

  • Transfer fee: the HOA or management company charges this to change ownership records.
  • Working capital or reserve contribution: common in newer DFW master-planned communities, often equal to a few months of dues or a flat amount.
  • Prorated dues: you reimburse the seller for any period they prepaid.
  • Resale certificate fee: up to $375, depending on the contract.

These count as cash to close, so I include them in your estimate up front. Also remember that a PID (public improvement district) assessment or a MUD tax isn't HOA dues. Those show up on your property tax bill, and they still count against your DTI. My MUD district guide explains them.

How did HOA dues change a Little Elm buyer's choice?

Earlier this year I worked with a buyer comparing two $420,000 homes in the Little Elm and Frisco area. One sat in an older subdivision with $55 a month in dues. The other was in a newer master-planned community with resort-style amenities: $125 a month in dues, plus a working capital contribution at closing.

The $70-a-month difference cost about $10,100 in loan amount at 7.40%. Since the buyer's DTI was already near 45% with a car payment, the newer home only worked with a slightly lower price. They negotiated a $9,000 price reduction, plus a seller credit for the transfer and capital fees. It worked because I re-ran the approval with the actual dues from the resale certificate before the option period ended, not from the listing estimate.

If you're buying in Frisco, McKinney, Prosper, or anywhere with an HOA, get a pre-approval that includes the real dues for the homes you're considering.
