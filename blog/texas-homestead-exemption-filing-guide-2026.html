When to File Your Texas Homestead Exemption After Closing — DFW 2026 Guide

Property TaxesBy Bond Peter NjokuSeptember 18, 20268 min read

I'm Bond Peter Njoku (NMLS #2670329), and this is one of the most valuable pieces of paperwork DFW buyers overlook right after closing. For 2026, the Texas homestead exemption removes $140,000 from your home's taxable value for school district taxes — up from $100,000 before — and if you just closed on a home, you can claim it for this year, prorated from your closing date, without waiting for next April.

When do I need to file my homestead exemption after buying a DFW home?

Right away — don't wait for the standard filing season. Since a 2022 law change, new Texas homeowners can claim the homestead exemption prorated from their closing date for the remainder of the purchase year, as long as the previous owner didn't already carry a homestead exemption on the property that same year. If you closed in September 2026, you can file now and get a partial-year exemption applied to your 2026 tax bill, rather than waiting until 2027. Everyone else — homeowners who already own and haven't yet filed — faces the standard annual deadline of April 30.

How much does the homestead exemption actually save in DFW?

For 2026, the general homestead exemption removes $140,000 from your home's taxable value for school district taxes. Homeowners 65 or older, or disabled, get an additional $60,000 (a combined $200,000), plus a tax ceiling that freezes their school district taxes at that year's level going forward. Counties and cities may offer up to an additional 20% optional exemption on top of that. Here's what the school-district portion alone is worth across DFW county tax rates:

How do I file, and which DFW appraisal district do I use?

Filing is free and takes about 10-15 minutes online through your county appraisal district. You'll need your closing documents and a copy of your Texas driver's license showing the property address.

What if I already missed the deadline on a past purchase?

It's recoverable. Texas allows late homestead exemption filings for up to two years after the delinquency date, and if approved, you'll receive a refund for the property taxes you overpaid during that window. If you closed on a home in the last two years and never filed, it's worth checking today rather than assuming the savings are gone for good.

Named example: a Frisco buyer who filed the same week they closed

I worked with a buyer who closed on a $460,000 home in Frisco in early September 2026. Because she filed her homestead exemption the same week as closing rather than waiting, she locked in the $140,000 exemption prorated for the rest of 2026 — an immediate reduction to her school-district taxable value that will show up on this year's bill instead of next year's, saving her roughly $2,100 in her first partial tax year alone at Collin County's effective rate.
