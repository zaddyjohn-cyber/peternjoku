FICO 10T and FHA Loans in Texas: What Changes on January 1, 2027

Credit ScoreBy Bond Peter NjokuSeptember 22, 20269 min read

I'm Bond Peter Njoku (NMLS #2670329), and HUD has confirmed a real, dated change coming to FHA lending: starting January 1, 2027, FHA-approved lenders will be able to use the FICO 10T credit score model instead of only the decades-old Classic FICO score. This isn't a rumor or a distant proposal — it's a confirmed FHFA/HUD milestone in the credit-score modernization effort already underway with VantageScore 4.0. If you're a first-time buyer in Garland, Mesquite, or anywhere in DFW planning to use an FHA loan, here's exactly what's changing, when, and what to do about it now, in 2026, before the switch happens.

What is FICO 10T, and how is it different from the score you have now?

Classic FICO — the model FHA lenders use today — looks at a snapshot of your credit file at the moment your report is pulled. FICO 10T instead uses trended data: up to 24 months of your account balances and payment history, so it can see whether you're steadily paying down debt or letting balances climb, not just where your balance sits today. FICO 10T can also incorporate rental payment history when it's present on your credit file — something Classic FICO generally ignores entirely.

When can FHA lenders actually use it?

Until January 1, 2027, every FHA loan I originate still runs on Classic FICO. The 580 minimum mid-score for 3.5% down, and the 500–579 tier requiring 10% down, aren't changing — what's changing is how that score gets calculated, and for some buyers, that calculation could work in their favor.

Why trended and rental data actually matters for DFW first-time buyers

This site's most common buyer profile is a first-time buyer in Garland, Mesquite, or Dallas with a credit score in the 580–680 range, often coming straight out of renting. Classic FICO doesn't see a single one of those on-time rent payments. FICO 10T can, if your landlord — or a rent-reporting service you enroll in yourself — actually reports that payment history to the credit bureaus. For a renter who's paid on time for two or three years but has a thin credit file otherwise, that's real, previously invisible credit history finally counting toward qualification.

Named scenario: preparing a Garland renter for 2027

I'm currently working with a client in Garland who's renting, has a steady job, and pays her $1,450 monthly rent on time every month — but her credit file only shows two credit cards and no mortgage history, which keeps her Classic FICO score in the low 600s. She's targeting a purchase in mid-2027, after FICO 10T becomes usable for FHA loans. Rather than wait, we enrolled her in a rent-reporting service this month so two full years of on-time rent payments will already be on her file by the time FICO 10T counts them. In the meantime, we're also working down two credit card balances, since trended data rewards a consistent pattern of paying debt down, not just a low balance on one particular day.

What should I do right now, in 2026, to prepare?

  • Get your rent reported. If your landlord doesn't report payments to the bureaus, a rent-reporting service can build that history before it matters.
  • Pay down revolving balances steadily rather than paying them off in one lump sum right before applying — a consistent downward trend is what trended data is built to reward.
  • Don't open new credit accounts in the months before you apply, whether you're using Classic FICO today or FICO 10T later.
  • Talk to me about your specific timeline. If you're buying before January 2027, none of this changes your underwriting model — but the habits above help your Classic FICO score too.

How do I get started on an FHA loan in Texas?

Whether you're buying this year under Classic FICO or waiting for the FICO 10T rollout, the qualification groundwork is the same: know your real credit picture, get pre-approved, and build a plan around your actual numbers rather than a national headline.
