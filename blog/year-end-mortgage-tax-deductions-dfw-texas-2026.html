Year-End Mortgage Tax Deductions for Texas Homeowners 2026

TaxesBy Bond Peter NjokuNovember 6, 20268 min read

November is the right time for Texas homeowners to take stock of their 2026 mortgage tax picture — before December 31 passes and certain timing strategies become unavailable. This post explains the five key deductions available to Texas mortgage holders in 2026, including two changes from prior years that many homeowners do not know about yet.

I'm Bond Peter Njoku, a Garland-based mortgage loan officer. I am not a tax professional — I cannot give you tax advice specific to your situation. But I can explain how these deductions work at a general level so you can have a more informed conversation with your CPA or tax preparer before year-end.

What changed for Texas homeowners' mortgage taxes in 2026?

Two meaningful changes apply to 2026 returns:

1. PMI is deductible again. The One Big Beautiful Bill Act (OBBBA), signed in 2026, restored the deductibility of mortgage insurance premiums (PMI and FHA MIP) as qualified residence interest. This deduction had expired after 2021 and was not available for the 2022–2025 tax years. For 2026 and future years (until the OBBBA provisions change), homeowners with PMI or FHA MIP can deduct those premiums on Schedule A, subject to income phase-outs.

2. The SALT cap increased. The 2026 SALT (State and Local Tax) deduction cap is $40,400 for married filing jointly and $20,200 for single filers — increased from the prior $10,000 cap under the TCJA. This change is especially significant for Texas homeowners, as explained below.

The five mortgage-related deductions for Texas homeowners in 2026

1. Mortgage Interest Deduction

The mortgage interest deduction allows you to deduct interest paid on mortgage debt up to $750,000 ($375,000 for married filing separately) on your primary residence and one second home. At current rates, this is the largest mortgage-related deduction for most DFW homeowners.

An important caveat: the deduction only saves you money if your total itemized deductions exceed the 2026 standard deduction. For a married couple, that is $32,200. On a $332,500 loan at 7.18%, your first-year interest alone is ~$23,730. Add $7,000 in property taxes (2% on $350K home) and you are at $30,730 — close to but below the $32,200 MFJ standard deduction. If you have additional deductible items (PMI, points paid at closing, charitable contributions), you likely clear the threshold.

2. Property Tax Deduction (SALT)

Texas homeowners may deduct their state and local property taxes up to the SALT cap. For 2026, that cap is $40,400 married filing jointly — a fourfold increase from the $10,000 cap that applied from 2018 through 2025 under the TCJA.

Here is the Texas-specific advantage: because Texas has no state income tax, Texas homeowners can apply their entire $40,400 SALT cap to property taxes. A California homeowner with $15,000 in state income tax uses all of their SALT cap on that before deducting a dollar of property tax. A Texas homeowner can deduct up to $40,400 in property taxes — and virtually all DFW homeowners are under that limit ($40,400 ÷ 2% = $2,020,000 assessed value before you hit the cap).

3. PMI / FHA MIP Deduction (Restored 2026)

Mortgage insurance premiums — PMI on conventional loans and MIP on FHA loans — are deductible again in 2026 as qualified residence interest, subject to these income phase-outs:

  • No phase-out if your AGI is $100,000 or less ($50,000 MFS)
  • Phased out ratably between $100,000 and $110,000 AGI
  • Fully phased out above $110,000 AGI ($55,000 MFS)

For a DFW buyer with a $280,000 FHA loan, the annual MIP at 0.55% is approximately $1,540 per year. At a 22% tax bracket and full eligibility, that is $339/year in tax savings — meaningful when it was zero for the past four years.

4. Discount Points Deduction (Purchase Year)

If you bought a home in 2026 and paid discount points to buy down your mortgage rate, those points are fully deductible in the year you paid them on a primary residence purchase. One point on a $332,500 loan is $3,325 — fully deductible on your 2026 return if you paid it at closing. This is unique to purchase-year points; refinance points must be deducted ratably over the loan term.

5. Year-End Timing: Prepay January 2027 Property Tax Now

Texas property taxes are due January 31 of the year following the tax year (the 2026 bill is due January 31, 2027). Under the cash basis accounting rule for individuals, you can deduct property taxes in the year you pay them — not the year they were assessed. If you pay your January 2027 Texas property tax installment before December 31, 2026, you can deduct it on your 2026 federal return.

This strategy is worth evaluating if you are on the borderline between itemizing and taking the standard deduction. Adding $7,000 in property tax payments to your 2026 deductions could tip you over the $32,200 MFJ threshold. The tradeoff: you give up the deduction on your 2027 return, where you would have deducted it anyway — so the value is primarily in timing (deduction in 2026 vs. 2027) and in clearing the standard deduction threshold if you are close.

Caveat: if you pay through escrow, your servicer controls the payment timing. You would need to contact your servicer to confirm whether they will make an advance payment to Dallas, Collin, or other Texas county taxing authorities before December 31. Many will not.

Unless they have substantial other itemized deductions (charity, additional state taxes, unreimbursed job expenses), they would take the standard deduction for 2026 and the mortgage deductions start saving them money starting in 2027, when they will have a full year of interest (~$23,730) plus property taxes (~$7,000) totaling ~$30,730 — again close to but needing additional items to clear the standard deduction threshold.

The mortgage interest deduction is valuable, but it is not automatic — it requires total itemized deductions to exceed the standard deduction, which the increased standard deduction in recent years has made harder to achieve for typical DFW buyers.

What is the Texas homeowner advantage with the SALT cap in 2026?

The 2026 SALT cap is $40,400 for married filing jointly ($20,200 single).
