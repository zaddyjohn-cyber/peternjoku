Good Neighbor Next Door Program Texas 2026: 50% Off Homes for Teachers, Firefighters, Police & EMTs

Special ProgramsBy Bond Peter NjokuOctober 7, 20269 min read

I'm Bond Peter Njoku, mortgage loan officer (NMLS #2670329) with Mortgage Funding Solutions, and one of the most underused homebuying programs I know is the HUD Good Neighbor Next Door program. If you're a K-12 teacher, law enforcement officer, firefighter, or EMT in Texas, you can buy a HUD-listed home at 50% off the appraised value — and combine that discount with FHA financing that requires just 3.5% down on the already-reduced price.

That's not a typo: 50% off. On a $200,000 home, that's a $100,000 purchase price. With FHA, your minimum down payment is $3,500. Your monthly principal and interest payment at current rates would be around $611.

The program is real, it works, and most eligible public servants in Texas have never heard of it.

Who qualifies for the Good Neighbor Next Door program in Texas?

GNND is restricted to four specific professions. There is no flexibility on this list:

Eligible ProfessionRequirementsMust Work In the Area?
K-12 TeacherFull-time employment at a public or private pre-K–12th grade schoolYes — must teach at a school in the area served by the home's school district
Law Enforcement OfficerFull-time employment by a federal, state, local, or Indian tribal government agency as a law enforcement officerYes — must serve the community where the home is located
FirefighterFull-time employment by a fire department or fire district serving the communityYes — must serve the community where the home is located
EMT/Emergency Medical TechnicianFull-time employment with an emergency medical services responder unit serving the communityYes — must serve the area where the home is located

Important: Nurses are NOT eligible for the Good Neighbor Next Door program. This is one of the most common misconceptions I encounter — nurses, doctors, and other healthcare workers frequently ask about GNND and are disappointed to learn they don't qualify. There IS a separate program called Nurse Next Door (operated by a private company, not HUD) that offers grants and down payment assistance specifically for nurses. If you're a nurse in DFW, I'm happy to walk you through what's available. Call or text me at 469-545-7180.

What are the other requirements for GNND in Texas?

Beyond your profession, you must also meet three additional criteria:

  1. First-time homebuyer status: Neither you nor your spouse can have owned residential property within the past 12 months. (Veterans who do not currently own a home are exempt from this requirement.)
  2. Owner-occupancy: You must agree to live in the home as your primary residence for 36 consecutive months after purchase. You cannot rent it out during this period.
  3. Good standing with federal loans: You must not be in default on any federal loan (including previous FHA mortgages or federal student loans).

How does the 50% discount work — what is a silent second mortgage?

When you buy through GNND, HUD prices the home at 50% of its appraised value. You take out a first mortgage for your portion (the 50% price, less your down payment). HUD simultaneously creates a "silent second mortgage" for the other 50% — the discount amount.

This silent second mortgage has these characteristics:

  • No monthly payments required
  • No interest accrues
  • Recorded as a lien on your home's title
  • Forgiven in full after 36 months of continuous owner-occupancy
  • If you sell, refinance in a way that cashes out equity, or move out before 36 months, you must repay a pro-rated portion

I helped a DFW teacher last year navigate this process. She's a 6th grade English teacher in Garland, single income, 620 credit score. The home listed at a HUD appraised value of $175,000 — she purchased it at $87,500. Her FHA down payment was $3,062. Her current monthly payment (principal, interest, taxes, insurance) is about $1,400/month on a home she'd never have been able to afford at market rate. After 36 months, the silent second is forgiven entirely.

How do I find Good Neighbor Next Door listings in Texas?

GNND properties are HUD-owned REO (real estate owned) homes — homes HUD acquired after an FHA-insured foreclosure. Here's how to find Texas listings:

  1. Go to hudhomestore.com
  2. In the search filters, select State = Texas
  3. Look for the GNND (Good Neighbor Next Door) designation on individual listings
  4. New GNND listings are posted on Monday mornings each week during a 7-day exclusive listing window for eligible buyers
  5. After 7 days, unsold homes become available to all buyers at the 50% discount (so act during the exclusive period)

GNND listings in Texas vary. Some weeks, there are 5–10 Texas listings. Other weeks, none. I recommend setting up a weekly Monday morning check. The program is available statewide, and listings have appeared in Dallas, Garland, Fort Worth, Arlington, and other DFW-area cities. The key is being pre-approved and ready to move when a listing comes up.

Can I stack GNND with Down Payment Assistance programs in Texas?

In most cases, no — GNND and DPA programs (TSAHC, TDHCA, Dallas DHAP) cannot be layered together because GNND already provides the most powerful down payment reduction available. The silent second mortgage counts as a lien that affects how other assistance programs calculate their own eligibility.

However, GNND can be combined with FHA (most common), VA (if eligible veterans prefer GNND's larger discount), or conventional loans. The program works best as a standalone financing strategy — the 50% discount makes DPA programs largely unnecessary.
