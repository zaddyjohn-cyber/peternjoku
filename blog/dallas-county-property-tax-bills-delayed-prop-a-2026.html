Dallas County Property Tax Bills Are Delayed Until After the Nov. 3 Prop A Vote. What It Means for Your Mortgage

Local NewsBy Bond Peter NjokuOctober 14, 20268 min read

If you own a home in Garland, Mesquite, Irving, Richardson or Dallas and you're waiting for your 2026 property tax statement, it isn't coming in October this year. Dallas County is holding its 2026 bills until after voters decide Proposition A on November 3, 2026, a tax rate election that adds about 2.4 cents per $100 of value to fund homelessness services. Early voting runs October 19–30. I'm Bond Peter Njoku (NMLS #2670329), a Garland-based loan officer. Below I go through what the delay does to your escrow account, to a home purchase closing in November or December, and to the deadline for homeowners who pay their own taxes.

One correction first. Some coverage (including my own September post on the Dallas County rate increase) presented $0.248650 as the new county rate. That is the rate only if Prop A passes. If it fails, the county rate falls back to the voter-approval rate of $0.224650. Either way it's higher than 2025's $0.215500, so your county taxes go up whatever happens on November 3.

Why are Dallas County property tax bills late in 2026?

On August 11, 2026, Dallas County Commissioners voted 3–2 to adopt a rate of $0.248650 per $100 and send the portion above the voter-approval rate to the ballot. Texas law requires voter approval for a rate above the voter-approval rate. Until the election is decided, the county doesn't know which rate to bill, so Tax Assessor-Collector John Ames is holding statements until after Election Day.

Three details matter for homeowners:

  • Bills were calculated on October 1 at the tentative (higher) rate. If you look up your account online before November 3, you may see the Prop A rate.
  • If Prop A fails and you've already paid at the higher rate, the county says it will refund the overpayment automatically.
  • The due date doesn't move. 2026 taxes are still due by January 31, 2027. Penalty and interest start February 1 (6% penalty plus 1% interest in the first month, rising after that). Because statements go out later, you have less time between getting the bill and paying it.

The Dallas County Tax Office also collects for many cities and school districts in the county, so the whole combined statement is delayed, not only the county line.

How much does Prop A change my Dallas County tax bill?

The county rate is a small part of your total bill. School district and city rates are much larger. Here is the county portion only at three different rates, so you can see the dollar difference the election makes:

*Dallas County's own figure for last year's median homestead bill is $558.97 because last year's median taxable value was lower. The county calculates $666.52 for this year's median homestead if Prop A passes. The other figures are the rate times taxable value, county portion only.

For most homeowners the election itself moves escrow by about $4–$8 a month. The bigger change is that county taxes rise about 4–15% this year either way. Add school, city and community college rates, plus any rise in your appraised value, and your escrow analysis can change by much more than the Prop A line.

I have an escrow account. Do I need to do anything?

Usually not. Your servicer gets the statement and pays it from escrow before January 31. Two things are worth checking:

  1. Don't pay it twice. Dallas County mails a courtesy copy to the homeowner even when a mortgage company pays the bill. Every winter some escrowed homeowners pay the courtesy copy themselves. The money comes back eventually, but it's an avoidable mess. If your monthly payment includes taxes, leave the bill to your servicer.
  2. Expect your escrow analysis to show a shortage. Your servicer runs its annual escrow analysis after paying the bill. If 2026 taxes come in higher than what was collected, the servicer spreads the shortage over the next 12 months and raises the ongoing payment to match. I explain how that works in my escrow shortage guide. Most servicers let you pay the shortage in one lump sum if you'd rather keep the monthly payment lower.

If you protested your appraisal this year and won, check that the servicer is paying the corrected bill, not the October 1 tentative figure.

I pay my own property taxes. What changes?

If you own your home free and clear, or your loan has no escrow, you'll get the bill directly. Plan for these three things:

  • Budget at the higher rate. If Prop A fails you get money back. Budgeting low and paying late costs you the penalty.
  • Watch the calendar. With statements arriving in November instead of October, it's easy to put the bill aside during the holidays and miss January 31.
  • Think about year-end timing. Property taxes are deductible in the year you pay them. If you itemize, paying before December 31 puts the deduction on your 2026 return. Paying in January puts it on 2027. I go through the numbers in my 2026 SALT deduction guide for Texas homeowners.

What if I'm buying a home in Dallas County in November or December?

Closings this fall work differently, because the 2026 bill may not exist yet at closing. Here's what happens:

Closing itemNormal yearFall 2026 in Dallas County
Seller's tax proration creditBased on the current-year billTitle uses the tentative October 1 figure or last year's taxes, with an adjustment once the real bill is issued
Initial escrow depositBased on the known billEstimated. Lenders often estimate from the higher tentative rate to avoid a shortage
Who pays the 2026 billBuyer (through escrow or directly) by Jan 31Same. The bill may simply arrive after you move in
Post-closing adjustmentRarely neededMore likely. The TREC contract has buyer and seller re-settle prorations if actual taxes differ from the estimate

For a fall closing, the seller credits you their share of 2026 taxes (January 1 to the closing date), and you pay the whole 2026 bill when it comes due. The standard Texas resale contract (TREC) says that if the current-year taxes differ from what was prorated at closing, buyer and seller re-settle once the real statements are available. With Dallas County bills delayed, expect that to happen more often this year. My property tax proration guide walks through the math.

Here is a composite scenario based on a closing I'm working on: a Garland buyer under contract at $315,000, closing December 5, FHA, 3.5% down. The loan amount before the upfront MIP is $303,975. At 7.40% (Freddie Mac PMMS, week of October 8) the principal and interest is about $2,105 a month. Garland's combined rate is close to 2%, about $525 a month on the full price. I ran the escrow at the higher tentative rate, and the seller's proration covers January 1 through December 5, roughly eleven months of the year's taxes. If Prop A fails, the buyer's escrow is slightly over-funded and the surplus comes back at the first annual analysis. If it passes, the estimate was already correct. The closing date doesn't depend on the election.

Does the tax increase change how much house I qualify for?

Slightly. Lenders count the full PITI payment, taxes included, in your debt-to-income ratio. An extra $5 a month in county taxes reduces purchase price by only about $700 at today's rates. In Dallas County the bigger factor is the combined rate of about 2%, compared with about 1.5% in Collin County. I compare counties in how Texas property taxes affect your DTI. If you're comparing a Garland house with a Plano or Wylie house, compare the full PITI for each, not the list prices.
