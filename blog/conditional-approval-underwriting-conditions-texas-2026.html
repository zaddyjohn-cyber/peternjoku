Conditionally Approved for a Mortgage in Texas? What Happens Next (and How the TPFA Deadline Fits In)

UnderwritingBy Bond Peter NjokuOctober 24, 20266 min read

I'm Bond Peter Njoku, a mortgage loan officer in Garland. "Conditionally approved" is good news, but it's not the finish line. It means an underwriter has reviewed your file and will approve the loan if you provide a short list of items. Most of my DFW buyers move from conditional approval to clear to close in about one to two weeks, and you control most of that time.

The national articles on this cover the basics. What they leave out is how conditional approval lines up with the deadlines in a Texas contract, and the Texas-specific items, like surveys, HOA certificates, and cash-out acknowledgments, that show up on our conditions lists. Here's how it works here.

What does conditionally approved mean?

The underwriter has run your credit, income, assets, and the property through the guidelines (usually with an automated approval from Fannie Mae, Freddie Mac, FHA, VA, or USDA) and found the loan approvable. Before final sign-off, they need proof of a few things. Each item on that list is a "condition."

Conditions usually come in two groups:

  • Prior-to-doc (PTD): items needed before the lender can draw closing documents and issue your Closing Disclosure. Most conditions fall here.
  • Prior-to-funding (PTF): items checked at or right before closing, like a final verbal employment check, signed tax forms, or the final title commitment.

How does conditional approval fit with my Texas financing deadline?

This is the part national articles skip. Most DFW purchase contracts include the TREC Third Party Financing Addendum. The current version (TREC 40-11, dated 11-04-2024) has two separate protections:

Buyer ApprovalProperty Approval
CoversYour credit, income, and assetsAppraisal, insurability, lender-required repairs
DeadlineThe number of days after the effective date written in the addendumOn or before the 3rd day before the closing date
To terminateWritten notice to the seller, plus the lender's written reasons (40-11)Written notice, plus the lender's written statement (40-11)
Earnest moneyRefunded if you terminate in timeRefunded if you terminate in time
If you miss the deadlineBuyer Approval is treated as obtainedProperty Approval is treated as obtained

Here's the catch. Conditional approval is not the same as Buyer Approval being safe. If your Buyer Approval window is 21 days and you still have an open income or asset condition on day 20, you have a decision to make. You can extend the deadline with the seller in writing, or you can let it pass and carry the risk that a credit or income problem costs you your earnest money. I give my buyers a plain answer before that deadline: either "your conditions are credit-safe," or "we need an extension." Older contracts on form 40-10 use slightly different wording, so check which version yours uses. My TPFA buyer guide goes deeper.

Can my loan still be denied after conditional approval?

Yes, though it's uncommon when nothing changes. These are the things that cause late denials:

  • New debt: a car loan, furniture financing, or a new credit card balance that pushes DTI over the limit. See car loans before buying.
  • Job change or loss of income before the final verbal employment check
  • Unsourced deposits you can't document. That money can't be used, which can leave you short at closing.
  • Low appraisal that changes the loan-to-value
  • Rate lock expiration at today's 7.40% market (Freddie Mac PMMS, mid-October 2026), where a re-lock at a higher rate can change your DTI

What did a Mesquite buyer's conditions look like?

This summer I worked with a buyer purchasing a $285,000 home in Mesquite with an FHA loan. Their credit score was in the 640s, and they had a W-2 job plus weekend catering income. The conditional approval came back with five conditions: an updated pay stub, two months of bank statements, an explanation for a $3,200 cash deposit, the insurance binder, and the appraisal.

The cash deposit was the hard one. Cash from side work can't be sourced the way a payroll deposit can, so we left that $3,200 out of their verified assets. They still had enough for the 3.5% down payment ($9,975) plus closing costs after a seller credit. We cleared every condition in six business days, well inside their 21-day Buyer Approval window, and they closed on schedule.

How do I clear conditions faster?

  1. Answer the same day. Every day a condition sits is a day added to your timeline.
  2. Send complete documents. Upload every page of a bank statement, including the "intentionally blank" ones. Missing pages are the top cause of repeat conditions.
  3. Don't move money around. Every transfer between accounts creates another paper trail to explain.
  4. Freeze your credit habits. No new cards, financing, or co-signing until after funding.
  5. Get your insurance quote in week one. In Texas, wind/hail deductibles can surprise you. See DFW insurance costs.

Frequently Asked Questions

How long does it take to go from conditional approval to clear to close in Texas?

I'm Bond Peter Njoku (NMLS #2670329), a Garland loan officer. For most of my DFW buyers it takes about one to two weeks, and the biggest factor is how fast the borrower sends complete documents. Conditions handled by others, like the appraisal, survey, or HOA certificate, can add a few days. Once the underwriter signs off, you get the Closing Disclosure at least three business days before closing. Call or text me at 469-545-7180 and I'll map out your timeline.

Is conditional approval the same as Buyer Approval in the Texas financing addendum?

I'm Bond Peter Njoku (NMLS #2670329), and no, they're different. Conditional approval is the lender's status, and Buyer Approval is a deadline in the TREC Third Party Financing Addendum. If you don't terminate by that deadline with the required notice, the contract treats Buyer Approval as obtained, even if you still have open conditions. I tell my buyers before the deadline whether their remaining conditions put their earnest money at risk. Call or text me at 469-545-7180 to check your dates.

Can I be denied after conditional approval?

I'm Bond Peter Njoku (NMLS #2670329). Yes. The most common reasons are new debt, a job change, deposits that can't be sourced, a low appraisal, or a re-lock at a higher rate that changes your debt-to-income ratio. If nothing changes in your finances and you clear the conditions, denials at this stage are rare. Don't open credit, change jobs, or move money without asking me first. Call or text me at 469-545-7180 with any questions.

I'm Bond Peter Njoku (NMLS #2670329). In Texas, there are also survey or T-47 acceptance, the HOA resale certificate, and on cash-out refinances, the 50(a)(6) fair market value acknowledgment.
