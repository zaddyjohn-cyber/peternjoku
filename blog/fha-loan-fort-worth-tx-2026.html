FHA Basics Every Fort Worth Buyer Should Know

FHA loans are backed by the Federal Housing Administration and built for buyers who don't have 20% saved or who have had credit challenges in the past. The minimum credit score is 580 for 3.5% down, and if your score falls between 500 and 579, you can still qualify with 10% down. That's a meaningfully lower bar than conventional loans, which usually want 620 or higher — and it's why FHA remains the top choice for so many Tarrant County first-time buyers I work with.

Fort Worth's housing stock ranges widely in price, from starter homes in the $220,000s to newer builds well above $400,000, and FHA's loan limits comfortably cover the bulk of that inventory. I run the numbers for each client so you know exactly where you land before you start touring homes.

Stacking FHA With the Fort Worth HAP Program

Fort Worth has one of the strongest local down payment assistance programs in the metro: the Fort Worth Homebuyer Assistance Program (HAP), which offers up to $25,000 in forgivable assistance over five years for qualifying first-time buyers. I regularly stack this with FHA financing so my clients cover most or all of their 3.5% down payment without touching their own savings. On a $280,000 home, that 3.5% down comes out to about $9,800 — HAP assistance can absorb that entirely, plus a chunk of closing costs.

I also work with TSAHC Home Sweet Texas and TDHCA My First Texas Home for Fort Worth buyers who don't qualify for the city-specific HAP program, since income limits and requirements differ slightly between them. Learn more on my down payment assistance page, and I'll help you figure out which combination gets you the most money toward your purchase.

The FHA Process From Application to Closing

Here's what happens after you reach out to me about an FHA loan in Fort Worth:

  • Pre-approval: I pull credit, verify income and assets, and hand you a real pre-approval letter — usually within 24 hours.
  • House hunting: Your agent submits offers backed by my pre-approval. FHA offers compete just as well as conventional ones in most price ranges across Fort Worth.
  • Appraisal and underwriting: FHA requires an FHA-approved appraiser to confirm minimum property standards. Older Fort Worth homes sometimes need minor repairs flagged during this step, which I help you navigate.
  • Closing: Most FHA files I close in Tarrant County take 30-35 days from contract to keys.

FHA vs. Conventional for Fort Worth Buyers

If your credit score is above 680 and you have at least 5% saved, conventional financing might edge out FHA on long-term cost since it drops mortgage insurance once you hit 20% equity. But for buyers under 680 or with less saved, FHA's lower rate requirements and DPA stacking usually win. I run both scenarios for every Fort Worth client before recommending either one, and I'll tell you honestly which saves you more over the life of the loan.
