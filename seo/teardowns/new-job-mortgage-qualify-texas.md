## Facts cross-checked (use these)
- Standard guideline across FHA/Conventional/VA/USDA: 2-year employment history preferred, but this
  is about continuity of income/career, not necessarily 2 years at the SAME employer.
- A new job in the SAME line of work (or a raise/promotion) is treated favorably — often needs just
  a signed offer letter + first pay stub, sometimes no gap-explanation needed at all.
- A career change (different field) usually requires a letter of explanation and sometimes a longer
  paper trail; underwriters want to see the new income is stable and likely to continue.
- Conventional loans allow "income from a new job start date" (future/non-employment income docs)
  in some cases — using an executed offer letter when the borrower hasn't started yet, provided the
  start date is within a defined window (commonly ~60-90 days) and income is guaranteed/non-
  contingent. Flag as lender-specific — don't overclaim a universal window without lender
  confirmation language.
- Probationary periods: some employers have a 90-day probation; lenders may ask for extra
  documentation (employer verification, offer letter) during this window but a probation period
  alone doesn't automatically disqualify a borrower.
- Self-employment transition (W-2 to 1099, or new business): different rules entirely — typically
  needs 2 years of self-employment history; link out to the site's own self-employed post rather
  than duplicating that content here.
- Compensating factors that offset a short job history: large down payment, high credit score, low
  DTI, strong reserves.
