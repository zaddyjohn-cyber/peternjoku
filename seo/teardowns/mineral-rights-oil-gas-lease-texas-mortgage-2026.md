## What nobody in this SERP covers — the entire lender and title-policy layer

Verified separately this run:

- **T-19.2 and T-19.3, Minerals and Surface Damage endorsements.** Premium per the TDI Basic Manual:
  **$50.00 on an Owner's Policy, $0.00 on a Loan Policy**, issued **per tract** (several tracts can mean
  several endorsements). **T-19.2** applies to residential of **one acre or less** and to commercial, and
  covers *improvements* excluding lawns, shrubbery and trees; **T-19.3** covers everything else and is
  limited to *permanent buildings* — so T-19.2 is the broader improvement coverage of the two. Both cover
  **damage from surface use for mineral development**, not loss of the minerals themselves.
- **Freddie Mac:** no right of **surface or subsurface entry within 200 feet of a residence**, and a title
  policy endorsement is required to protect the lender.
- **Fannie Mae, Selling Guide B7-2-05 (Title Exceptions and Impediments):** outstanding oil, water or
  mineral rights are acceptable where they are **customarily waived by other lenders** and do not
  **materially alter the contour of the property or impair its value or usefulness**.
- A standard Texas policy **excludes coverage of mineral ownership** and gives no affirmative statement
  on it; a **recorded oil and gas lease is listed as a Schedule B exception**, which is itself a clue that
  a severance happened earlier in the chain.
- **Separate, unrelated Texas 200-foot rule:** "minerals" does not include coal, lignite or iron ore on or
  within 200 feet of the surface where producing them would destroy or deplete the surface. Must not be
  merged with Freddie's 200-foot setback.

## ⚠️ DATA FLAGS

- **The 200-foot rule is FREDDIE's, not Fannie's.** Do not attribute it to Fannie. Do not merge it with
  the Texas coal/lignite/iron-ore 200-foot doctrine. (Research pass negative list.)
- Fannie's **multifamily** guide uses 250-foot subsurface and 600-foot equipment distances. **Multifamily
  only** — not applicable to a single-family loan and not used in the post.
- **T-19.2 / T-19.3 scope wording** comes from the TDI manual plus a title-company commentary; the
  endorsement forms themselves were not read line by line. The post describes coverage in general terms
  and tells the reader to get the actual endorsement language from the title company.
- Virtual Underwriter's TX 12.16 section bodies (12.16.1–12.16.7) were **gated** — only the section
  titles were retrievable. Nothing in the post relies on them.
- Lexology and the TLTA deck were not read (403 / PDF not fetched). The "no evidence of drilling plus a
  waiver of surface rights means little lender concern" framing that surfaced in search is attributed
  generally in the post, not quoted as TLTA's position.
- **No severance-frequency data exists in retrievable form.** The widely repeated claim that "most Texas
  property is presumed severed" came from one practitioner summary. The post says mineral severance is
  *common* in Texas and does not put a percentage on it.
- The Texas REALTORS FAQ has an apparent wording error ("allows buyers to make any reservations" where it
  means sellers). Not repeated.
