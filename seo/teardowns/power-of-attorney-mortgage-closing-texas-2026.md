## Verified rule text to build the post on (7 TAC §153.15, "Location of Closing: Section 50(a)(6)(N)")

- A Texas home equity loan may close **only at an office of the lender, an attorney at law, or a title
  company.**
- **Any power of attorney** allowing an attorney-in-fact to execute the closing documents **must be
  signed by the owner, or the owner's spouse, at the permanent physical address of an office of the
  lender, an attorney at law, or a title company.** This is the trap: the POA's *signing location* is
  regulated, not just the closing's.
- Acceptable proof of where it was signed: (A) a written statement in the POA acknowledging the date and
  place of execution; (B) an affidavit of a witness present at the signing; or (C) a notary certificate
  of acknowledgment giving the date and place (Ch. 121, Civil Practice and Remedies Code).
- **2020 amendment:** the closing may occur in any area at that permanent physical address, indoor office
  or parking lot included.
- Practical consequence, from compliance sources: unless the lender has verification that the POA was
  executed in the proper location, it should not be used to close the home equity loan. Many Texas title
  companies will not accept a POA on a home equity loan at all.

## ⚠️ DATA FLAGS

- **"Fannie Mae will not purchase a Texas home equity loan closed via POA"** — surfaced from an aged
  compliance summary, **not verified against the Selling Guide.** Do NOT assert it. Say instead that many
  lenders and title companies refuse a POA on a 50(a)(6) and the borrower must get written confirmation
  first. (Research pass flag 9.)
- **Estates Code §751.151 / "within 30 days"** — secondary sources only; primary text not read. Attribute
  the recording requirement to Property Code §12.001 and frame the 30-day figure as what practitioners
  cite. (Flag 10.)
- Grey Square's "six months" POA age limit and "7–14 business day" VA review are the author's practice
  experience, not rules. Present them as what to plan for, not as requirements.
- The 10-business-day lender accept/reject rule that surfaced in search is from a ~6-year-old source and
  was **not** used.
