## THE CORE GAP (bigger than any single competitor weakness)
Every real competitor result (Rocket, Amerisave, SuperMoney) frames "USDA loan + new construction" as the **construction-to-permanent loan product** — buying raw land and building from scratch with a single close. That product exists but is a small slice of what DFW buyers searching this term actually need. The much more common real-world case in fast-growing USDA-eligible corridors like Forney and Terrell is buying an **already-built or nearly-complete new-construction/production home from a builder** using a standard USDA purchase loan — no ground-up construction loan required. None of the top results distinguish these two paths, none name a DFW growth corridor, and none address builder plan-approval or new-build inspection questions in that context.

## GAP SHEET — what our post must cover
1. **Lead by splitting the two paths clearly**: (a) buying a completed/near-complete new-construction home from a builder with a standard USDA purchase loan — the common DFW case — vs (b) USDA's true construction-to-permanent loan for building from raw land. Competitors conflate these.
2. **Name the eligible new-construction corridors**: Forney (75126), Terrell (75160), Royse City, parts of Kaufman/Rockwall/Ellis counties — tie to the actual building boom there. No competitor names a single DFW city.
3. **Builder/plan approval requirements** for the standard-purchase path: new-construction appraisal, builder must provide a new home warranty, plans/specs reviewed, final inspection/certificate of occupancy required before closing — distinct from the ground-up construction-loan builder vetting (2 yrs experience, $500K liability insurance) that competitors focus on.
4. **USDA vs FHA/conventional new-build inspection comparison table** — none of the competitors table this out.
5. **Full math example on the 1% upfront guarantee fee (financeable) + 0.35% annual fee** — competitors mention "guarantee fees" vaguely; none show the dollar math.
