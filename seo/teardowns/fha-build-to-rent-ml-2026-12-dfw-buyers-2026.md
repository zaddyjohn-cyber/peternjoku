## GAP SHEET — our post must cover

1. **What ML 2026-12 actually is.** HUD Mortgagee Letter 2026-12, "'Build-To-Rent' Developments
   Underwriting Eligibility," effective **October 14, 2026**. It makes qualifying BTR
   communities — detached single-family homes, duplexes and triplexes, site-built or
   manufactured — eligible for FHA **multifamily** mortgage insurance.
2. The two programs: **Section 221(d)(4)** = new construction/substantial rehab;
   **Section 223(f)** = acquisition or refinance, and only where the certificate of occupancy
   is dated **less than three years** before application.
3. Property conditions: **5 or more units on one contiguous site**, professional management,
   **no interspersed privately owned lots**, and **no sales or partial releases of individual
   homes during the loan term**. Minimum **7% vacancy** assumption for market-rate deals unless
   comparable market conditions justify higher. Exempt from most FHA Act design requirements
   but still must meet public/common-use accessibility and usable-doors requirements.
4. **THE CORRECTION, stated plainly and early:** this is a commercial multifamily program for
   developers and owners. It is **not** a consumer mortgage. An individual cannot use ML 2026-12
   to buy a rental house. The consumer FHA loan is still the 203(b) program — 3.5% down,
   580 FICO, **$563,500** DFW limit, owner-occupied.
5. COMPARISON TABLE: FHA 221(d)(4)/223(f) BTR (developer) vs FHA 203(b) (your loan) vs FHA
   203(k) vs conventional investor loan — across borrower type, occupancy, units, down payment,
   purpose, and whether an individual can use it.
6. **Why a DFW buyer should care anyway** — the real consequences:
   - DFW is a national BTR leader. Point2Homes projected ~**8,470** single-family rentals under
     development in DFW (2025 report; Fort Worth largest share, McKinney second).
   - Northmarq: DFW BTR absorption **+30% YoY** in 2025, vacancy compressed 70 bps to **6.3%**,
     average BTR rent about **$2,130/month**.
   - Cheaper FHA-insured construction debt for BTR sponsors means more competition for the same
     outer-suburb land that entry-level for-sale builders want (Forney, Royse City, Princeton,
     Terrell corridors).
   - A BTR section inside or beside your subdivision affects appraisal comps and resale pool.
7. **Rent vs buy math**, worked: $2,130/mo BTR rent vs owning a $300K Forney/Royse City home at
   7.40% — P&I $2,078, +Kaufman County 1.9% tax ($475/mo) + insurance (~$175/mo) ≈ $2,728 PITI
   before DPA; then show the FHA 3.5%-down version and the USDA $0-down version, which is the
   real answer in Forney/Royse City/Terrell.
8. Pending legislation, flagged honestly as not law: Senate passed the **21st Century Road to
   Housing Act** in March 2026 with language requiring many BTR projects to convert homes to
   individual ownership after seven years; NAHB estimated ~40,000 units/yr affected. **Stalled
   in Congress** — do not state it as current law.
9. Named composite scenario — Royse City renter in a BTR home at $2,130 who bought with USDA.

  lists 2026-12 as "'Build-To-Rent' Developments Underwriting Eligibility"; effective date
  October 14, 2026 corroborated by Bravo Capital (Yahoo Finance), WRE News and Roach Lorenz.

  as quoted letter text.

  from Point2Homes and Northmarq. Label the year. Do not present as October 2026 figures.

  NOT law.** Must be labeled as such.
- DFW median rent ~$1,783/mo (down ~5.8% YoY, mid-2026) comes from a single blog — use the
  Northmarq BTR-specific $2,130 instead, which is the relevant comparison and better sourced.

  for Dallas, Collin, Denton, Tarrant, Rockwall, Kaufman, Ellis). Per CLAUDE.md, do not write
