1. **No underwriting mechanics.** Says only "lenders cap the loan at appraised value." Never
   explains the LTV recalculation — that the loan amount is recomputed as (LTV × appraised value),
   that the *down payment requirement does not shrink*, and that the gap is cash on top of the
   original down payment. This is the single biggest gap.
2. **No loan-program comparison table.** FHA/VA/conventional handling is scattered in prose and
   inconsistent. No side-by-side.
3. **VA Tidewater mentioned but not explained** — and described inconsistently (sometimes "agents",

## ⚠️ DATA FLAG — resolved 2026-10-08

**SKILL.md Tier 10 item 1 said "FHA locks the value for 90 days." That is wrong.** Verified against
HUD **Mortgagee Letter 2022-11** (hud.gov) and FHA INFO 2022-71: the FHA **initial appraisal
validity period is 180 days** from the appraisal's effective date, for case numbers assigned on or
after June 1, 2022 (raised from the prior 120 days). An appraisal **update** extends validity to
**one year** from the initial report's effective date (raised from 240 days).

The competitor's "120 days conventional and FHA" is also stale for FHA — it predates ML 2022-11.

The genuinely important FHA mechanic, which no competitor states: the appraised value attaches to
the **FHA case number**, not the lender. Switching lenders on the same case number keeps the
appraisal; a **new case number generally requires a new appraisal**. SKILL.md corrected in this run.

Conventional (Fannie/Freddie) appraisal validity remains **120 days**, extendable to 12 months with
an appraisal update. VA appraisals (NOVs) are valid **6 months**. Stated as such in the post.

9. **The underwriting math, explicitly.** $350K offer / $335K appraisal in Garland at 95% LTV

   **additive to**, not a substitute for, the down payment. No competitor does this.
