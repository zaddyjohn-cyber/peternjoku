1. **No Texas statute anywhere.** iQRATE gestures at "the Texas Property Code" for entry notice without a

   sale) or **§91.001** (month-to-month termination).
2. **Nothing on the TREC contract.** No discussion of the possession paragraph, of disclosing an existing
   lease, or of how a sitting tenant interacts with the option period and the walk-through.
3. **Neither page tells the owner-occupant buyer what to actually do.** Gustan Cho says the tenant "must
   either leave or have their lease end soon" and stops. That is the whole problem, unaddressed.
4. **No percentage of rent** given by either page for qualifying income, and **Form 216 is never
   mentioned.**
5. No table on either page. No FHA/VA occupancy certification detail. No month-to-month notice length.
6. iQRATE is a Nevada company writing about Dallas from Las Vegas; its local credibility is thin.

## Verified Texas law to build the post on

- **Prop. Code §92.105(a):** the **new owner is liable for return of the security deposit from the date
  title is acquired**, regardless of whether notice went to the tenant under subsection (b).
- **Prop. Code §92.105(b):** the former owner stays liable for a deposit received while they owned the
  property until the new owner delivers the tenant a signed statement (sources differ on exact wording —
  one version says until the new owner has received the deposit or assumed liability for it). A seller can
  be released by transferring the deposit and giving the tenant written notice naming the new owner and
  address.
- **Prop. Code §91.001:** a month-to-month tenancy may be terminated by either party giving notice; where
  rent is paid monthly, that is one month's notice. A sale does **not** by itself end the tenancy.
- Texas common law: a buyer **takes title subject to an existing lease**, so a fixed-term tenant can
  generally stay through the term.

## GAP SHEET — what our post must cover

1. Everything iQRATE has: lease verification, estoppel certificate contents, appraisal access, deposit
   and prepaid-rent proration at closing, rent vs. market rent, DSCR math, reserves, down payment tiers,
   inherited tenants, obligations to the tenant.
2. Everything Gustan Cho has: the **60-day** occupancy expectation, lease survives the sale,
   month-to-month vs fixed term, cash for keys, red flags, inspection with a tenant in place.
3. **The Texas statutes above, by section** — especially §92.105's date-of-title rule, which is the single
   most expensive thing a buyer in this situation can get wrong.
4. **A table splitting the two buyer types** — "I want to live here" vs "I want to keep it rented" —
   across: loan type available · down payment · occupancy requirement · can lease rent count as income ·
   what the lease does to your timeline · reserves.
5. The owner-occupant playbook nobody writes: the lease has to end or the tenant has to leave *before*
   your occupancy deadline, so the fix happens in the **contract**, not in underwriting — seller delivers
   the property vacant at closing, or the lease expires inside the window, or you don't buy it as a
   primary residence.
6. Rental-income mechanics: **typically 75%** of gross rent (a 25% vacancy factor), Form 1007 for
   single-family, Form 1025 for 2–4 units, Form 216 where an operating statement is required, and the
   documents the underwriter wants — executed lease, estoppel, proof of deposit history.
7. Appraisal access with an uncooperative tenant and what it does to the closing date.
8. Payment math at **7.24%** on a DFW price point, and the DTI effect of negative cash flow.
9. Named composite scenario: a Mesquite buyer under contract on a tenant-occupied $280,000 house with
   seven months left on the lease.
10. 4 FAQs with NMLS + phone: can I buy with an FHA loan if a tenant is living there · how soon do I have
    to move in · who is responsible for the security deposit after closing · can I use the rent to qualify.

## ⚠️ DATA FLAGS

- **60-day occupancy** — cited by secondary sources to Fannie B2-1-3-04; primary not read this run. Frame
  as the standard primary-residence expectation that lives in the note and occupancy affidavit. (Research
  pass flag 12.)
- **75% of gross rent** — standard practice, taken from secondary lender sources this run, not the Selling
  Guide. Say "typically 75%" and that the exact treatment is the underwriter's. (Flag 11.)
- **§92.105(b) wording differs between aggregators.** The post states the (a) rule firmly (consistent
  across sources) and describes (b) as the seller-release mechanic without quoting one version as
  statute.
- §91.001's excerpt available this run did **not** include the notice-length subsections. "One month's
  notice where rent is monthly" comes from secondary commentary — stated as such, and the reader is told
  to have a Texas attorney confirm the notice their lease requires.
- iQRATE's "$50 gift card" and the Austin proration example are that page's own illustrations; our post
  uses its own DFW numbers.
