## Competitor weaknesses to exploit

1. **No comparison table anywhere in the top 10.** A dollar table alone differentiates.
2. **No current-rate context.** Not one page connects the advice to an actual rate environment.
   Freddie Mac PMMS 7.40% on 2026-10-08 is the 7th consecutive weekly increase — this is precisely
   when locks expire into worse pricing. Zero competitors mention it.
3. **Fee ranges conflict wildly (0.125% vs 1%)** and no page explains *why* — that extension pricing
   is priced off the cost of extending the hedge, so it scales with how far rates moved, not with a
   flat lender fee schedule.
4. **No one explains worst-case re-lock pricing** (re-lock at the worse of the original lock or
   current market — the standard secondary-marketing rule). This is the single most important thing
   a borrower needs to know and it is absent from the entire SERP.
5. No Texas specificity, no loan-type differences (FHA/VA/USDA timelines run longer, so lock risk is
   higher), no named scenario.

## GAP SHEET — what our post must cover

Must match competitors:
1. What a rate lock is and what an extension actually does (adds days, keeps rate/points)
2. Typical extension cost range per period, with the conflict explained (0.125%–0.375% common;
   up to ~1% on long or badly-timed extensions)
3. Extensions in 7/15/30-day increments; lenders commonly cap the number
4. Free extension when the lender caused the delay — and how to ask for it
5. Float-down options and their cost
6. How to avoid needing an extension (longer initial lock + cushion)

## ⚠️ DATA FLAGS
- Rate figure: use **7.40%** (Freddie Mac PMMS released 2026-10-08; prior week 7.28%; year-ago
  6.30%; 15-yr 6.73%). This supersedes SKILL.md's "use 7.0% for 2026" default — SKILL.md says use
