## Verified facts to build on

**TREC Form 61-0 — "Seller's Disclosure About Groundwater and Surface Water Rights":**
- Listed by TREC with an effective date of **07/01/2026**. Form copies circulating are dated **05-04-2026**;
  commentary says TREC adopted the rules **2026-05-04**.
- A brand-new **standalone** form, separate from the Seller's Disclosure Notice.
- Triggered by new **Paragraph 7(I)** of the One to Four Family Residential Contract (**7(k)** on Farm & Ranch).
- Committee materials show "Water Notice:" was later dropped from the title.
- **Critical nuance from TREC's own FAQ:** the 61-0 "is not required to be provided by another law."
  Unlike the Seller's Disclosure Notice under **Property Code § 5.008**, its force comes *only* from the
  TREC contract. A broker guide puts it plainly: "we cannot require sellers to complete it."
- Independence Title cheat sheet: **5 trigger questions**; if all five are "no," the seller is not required
  to complete it. One trigger is awareness of a **water well** on the property. Surface water rights mean a
  permit, certified filing or certificate of adjudication (TCEQ-administered).
- TREC created it following the **Sunset Advisory Commission's** special-purpose review of TREC's
  disclosure requirements.

**Expanded Seller's Disclosure Notice (same 2026 cycle):**
- Adds items on **insurance / windstorm coverage**, **private road maintenance obligations**,
  **above-ground storage tanks over 500 gallons**, and **conservation easements**.
- "Generators" added to Paragraph 2B (Improvements) in the contract.
- Republic Title and HAR: mandatory use **2026-07-01** for the revised forms.
- Statutory baseline: § 5.008 requires the notice for most 1–4 unit residential sellers before or at
  contract. Texas REALTORS® TXR-1406 is commonly used; TREC's own is 55-0 (older sources say OP-H).
- Flood questions were added **2019-09-01**: floodplain status with FEMA definitions, prior flood-related
  insurance claims, FEMA aid received, plus water damage *not* from a flood event. Repairs do **not**
  extinguish the duty — known prior flooding is a material fact regardless of remediation.

## ⚠️ DATA FLAGS

1. **Effective-date conflict.** Republic Title + HAR say mandatory 2026-07-01; neuhausre says TREC 55-0
   took effect 2026-05-28. **July 1 is better supported** — state it, and flag the conflict rather than
   asserting one date silently.
2. **trec.texas.gov REFUSED CONNECTION to WebFetch on this run** (`ECONNREFUSED 204.64.197.3:443`), twice.
   The form's effective date and FAQ language come from the search index and title-company guidance, not a
   direct read. Tell readers to confirm on trec.texas.gov. **Retry the fetch on a future run and tighten.**
3. **Form numbering** is inconsistent across sources (OP-H vs 55-0). Use 55-0 and note TXR-1406 is the
   version most DFW agents hand over.

6. **What underwriting actually does with each disclosed item.** Build the table the SERP lacks:
   - prior flood claim / FEMA aid → flood zone determination, **NFIP policy as a condition of funding**,
     elevation certificate
   - above-ground storage tank >500 gal → appraiser safety/soundness call, possible removal before closing
   - private road, no maintenance agreement → **legal-access and maintenance-agreement requirements** on
     agency loans; can stop a file
   - water well disclosed → routes straight into the FHA well-distance rules (link the companion post
     written the same day)
   - conservation easement → title exception and an appraisal problem, not just a disclosure line
   - insurance/windstorm item → **insurability as a funding condition** (link the TDI roof-age post)
7. **The timing point:** the disclosure arrives before or at contract, which is *before* the appraisal is
   ordered — so a buyer who reads it properly can price the risk during the option period instead of
   discovering it at underwriting.
