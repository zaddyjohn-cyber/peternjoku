## ⚠️ DATA FLAGS

**Market data (WebSearch 2026-10-09) — attribute each to its own source, do NOT merge:**
- **ICE Mortgage Monitor (June 2026 edition):** demand for home equity loans and HELOCs hit an
  **18-year high in Q1 2026**
- **New York Fed:** HELOC balances reached **$459 billion in Q2 2026**, about **$142B above their
  early-2022 low**, the **17th consecutive quarter of growth**
- **CNBC (2026-10-03):** homeowners originated **nearly 20% more second mortgages/HELOCs in Q2
  than in Q1**; also reports rates above 7.5% have made HELOCs expensive enough to suppress
  renovation plans, and that some borrowing is for staying afloat rather than improvements
- Refiguide estimate: roughly two-thirds of outstanding mortgages carry rates well below market

**Flags:**
- The "18-year high" (ICE, originations demand) and "17 straight quarters" (NY Fed, balances)
  measure different things. Keep them in separate sentences with separate attributions.
- CNBC's framing is the **contrarian** read — equity borrowing is up partly from distress, not
  only opportunity. Include it; the honest version is more credible and matches house voice.
- CNBC says "above 7.5%" for rates; Freddie Mac PMMS for 2026-10-08 is **7.40%** for the 30-year
  fixed. These are not the same instrument — HELOC pricing is prime-based and quoted separately
  from the 30-year fixed. Do not use 7.5% as the 30-year figure anywhere.
- **Fee cap:** the SERP contains both 2% and 3%. Correct current figure is **2%**, effective for
  loans made on or after 2018-01-01 under Prop 2 (Nov 2017), with appraisal/survey/title charges
  excluded from the cap. Verify phrasing stays "lender fees capped at 2% of the loan principal,
  excluding appraisal, survey, title examination and title insurance premiums."
- No USDA/DPA/conforming figures appear in this post, so the SKILL.md limit tables are not at risk.
