**SERP read: the weakest SERP of the five, by a wide margin.** Three of the top five results are
Australian or New Zealand pages built on **mortgage stress testing** — lenders there assess at
~3 percentage points above the offered rate, or a ~9% floor. **US lenders do not do this.** Fannie
Mae/Freddie Mac, FHA, VA and USDA all qualify at the actual note rate (ARMs being the narrow
exception). So the pages ranking for this query are giving American readers a mechanism that does
not exist in their market. The one genuinely US result answers a *different* question (approval
withdrawn before closing, not pre-approval amount reduced). There is effectively no correct
US-market page on this keyword.

## Competitor weaknesses to exploit

1. **Foreign qualifying mechanics presented as universal.** Correcting this — US lenders qualify at
   the note rate, not a stress-test rate — is the single highest-value correction on the page.
2. **No page quantifies the actual 2026 US rate move.** 30-yr fixed went from **6.30% a year ago to
   7.40% on 2026-10-08** (Freddie Mac PMMS). That is a precise, citable, year-over-year buying-power
   haircut nobody has calculated.
3. **No page accounts for Texas property taxes in the DTI**, which is exactly what makes the
   shrinkage worse here than in low-tax states — Texas escrow is a much larger share of PITI.
4. **No page explains pre-approval expiration and re-issue** (typically 60–90 days; credit report
   validity 120 days for most agency programs) or that re-issuing re-prices at today's rate.
5. No page distinguishes the three separate reasons an amount drops: rate moved, DTI changed
   (new debt/income), or credit score tier changed (LLPA/pricing).
6. No named scenario, no table, no Texas county math.

## ⚠️ DATA FLAGS
- **Rate series (Freddie Mac PMMS):** 30-yr fixed **7.40%** week of 2026-10-08; **7.28%** prior
  week; **6.30%** a year earlier; 15-yr **6.73%** (prior week 6.60%). Seventh consecutive weekly
  increase; highest since November 2023. 10-yr Treasury averaged **5.28%**, up 9 bps week over week.
- MND read **7.59%** and MBA **7.49%** for the same week — higher than PMMS. Use PMMS as the
  benchmark; mention daily pricing ran higher but never average the three.
- **Do not import the Australian/NZ stress-test numbers** (3-point buffer, 9% floor) as if they
  applied. They are the error this post exists to correct.
- The AU example ($474,400 at 3% vs $418,900 at 4%) is AUD and a different product. Build our own
  table from the US P&I formula at the PMMS rates instead.
- **Qualifying-ratio guidelines are guidelines, not hard cutoffs** — AUS findings and compensating
  factors move them. Phrase as typical ranges, and keep the compliance box language intact.
- DPA/USDA income limits and the $832,750 conforming / $563,500 FHA figures (CLAUDE.md) are not
  used in this post's math, but if the conforming limit is referenced, it must be **$832,750**,
  never the stale $806,500.
