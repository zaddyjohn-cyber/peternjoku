Authorities cited in the order: 42 U.S.C. 1490a(a)(1)(B), 38 U.S.C. 3762(c)(1), EO 14394 of 2026-03-13.
**The only deadline anywhere in the order is the 180 days in Sec. 3(b)** (≈2027-04-20).

## Data points available

- HPA 1998: MI cancels **automatically at 78%**; borrower *request* below **75%** (2–5 years seasoning)
  or below **80%** (more than 5 years).
- ~**40%** of borrowers have a mark-to-market LTV below 80% (industry estimate, draft version).
- Fannie: ~**8,000** AOL-backed loans, ~**22,000** through title acceptance, **zero** title defects
  reported; ~**$25M** saved over ~18 months; **$500–$1,500** per loan.
- Freddie lender title assessment: **$600–$650** average per transaction, range **$400–$1,700**,
  **>$3B** in volume.
- Refinances are ~**7%** of title premiums (KBW). ALTA: refi fraud/forgery claims average ~**$207,000**.
- Broker Action Coalition: ~**$2,400** saved on an illustrative $500,000 refinance.

## Competitor weaknesses to exploit

1. **Neither ranking page separates "shall" from "shall consider."** HousingWire says the order "calls
   for" appraisal waivers and LLPA cuts. It does not — Sec. 4(b) says *consider*.
2. **NAR gets the MI threshold backwards** (says you must *request* release at 78%; the HPA makes 78%
   automatic) and **adds a purchase-only limit to Sec. 5 that the order does not contain.**
3. **Zero Texas content on any page.** Nobody notes that the refi relief is aimed at *limited cash-out*
   refinances — which in Texas means a **50(f)(2)** rate-and-term, not a **50(a)(6)** cash-out.
4. **Nobody mentions that a Texas 50(a)(6) can never get an appraisal waiver** regardless of what FHFA
   decides, because the Selling Guide requires an appraisal attached to the written acknowledgment of
   fair market value.
5. No table. No FAQ. No payment math. No "here is what to do this week."
6. Nobody notes that Sec. 4's pilot is capped at **80% LTV** — the same ceiling Texas already imposes on
   home equity by constitution, so the two limits coincide for Texas homeowners.

## GAP SHEET — what our post must cover

1. What EO 14438 is, when it was signed (2026-10-22), and that it is an executive order directing
   agencies, not a law that changes anyone's loan today.
2. **The verb table above** — mandated vs. "shall consider," section by section. This is the post's spine.
3. Title insurance on refinances: what the title acceptance pilot is, the 80% LTV and geographic limits,
   the Fannie/Freddie volume and savings figures, and the ALTA fraud-claim counterargument.
4. **Texas title premium is promulgated by TDI** — so a waiver saves a Texas homeowner a published,
   knowable number, not a negotiated one. Link to the title insurance cost post.
5. PMI: the HPA 78% / 75% / 80% framework **as it stands today**, then what the pilot would add, then the
   catch — the pilot covers mortgages made *after* it is established, so it does nothing for a loan
   closed in 2026.
6. The Texas 50(a)(6) vs 50(f)(2) distinction, and the appraisal-waiver carve-out.
7. USDA/VA **direct** loans only — not guaranteed loans. Say plainly that most Texas VA and USDA
   borrowers use the *guaranteed* programs and are untouched by Sec. 5.
8. Payment math: what a rate change actually does vs. what a fee change does, at 7.24% on a $350K loan.
9. Named composite scenario — a Garland homeowner asking whether to wait for the EO to lower their refi
   cost. Answer: the break-even is driven by the rate, not the $800 of title.
10. 4 FAQs with NMLS + phone: does this lower my rate · do I get PMI cancelled automatically now · will
    my refi skip the appraisal · does any of this apply to a Texas cash-out.
11. Comparison table: what changes today / what is only under consideration / what Texas law overrides.
