## Competitor weaknesses to exploit

1. **Every top result has the direction of the trend wrong as of this week.** None cites the
   2026-10-08 TDI data.
2. **No page explains the escrow *mechanics* of a premium decrease** — that a lower premium does
   NOT lower your payment automatically; it shows up at the next annual escrow analysis, and a
   surplus over $50 must be refunded under RESPA while a smaller surplus can be retained.
3. **No page connects falling insurance to DTI/qualifying.** A lower escrow line lowers PITI, which
   lowers back-end DTI, which raises the price a buyer qualifies for — directly useful at 7.40%.
4. No page tells readers TDI now publishes a **rate-filing search tool and county premium data
   (2019–2025)** so they can check whether their own carrier filed a decrease. TDI launched these
   2026-06-22. Actionable and unique.
5. No named scenario, no dollar math, no DFW county specificity.

## ⚠️ DATA FLAGS

**Verified 2026-10-09 via WebFetch of carriermanagement.com/news/2026/10/08/292585.htm
(published 2026-10-08), reporting a TDI release:**
- Homeowners: filed rate changes averaged a **4.3% DECREASE over the past 90 days**
- About **700,000 homeowners policyholders** affected by decreases filed since **July 1, 2026**
- **ASI Lloyds** (Progressive company): **6% decrease**, 71,000 policyholders
- **Texas Farm Bureau Mutual**: **5.2% decrease**, 133,000 policyholders
- Auto: ~4.4 million policyholders affected by decreases filed since July 1, 2026
  (Auto Club County Mutual −3%, 235,000; Loya −10.3%, 39,000)
- Quoted: **Insurance Commissioner Amanda Crawford** ("Lower rate filings are an encouraging sign
  for Texas consumers," urged shopping around) and **Gov. Greg Abbott**
- ~160 companies sell auto and homeowners insurance in Texas

**⚠️ COLLISION — two different "4.3%" figures, opposite directions. Do not conflate:**
- **−4.3%** = average *homeowners* filing over the 90 days ending Oct 2026 (TDI, this release)
- **+4.3%** = average *statewide filed increase for calendar 2025* (per KLTV May 2026, down from
  +18.7% in 2024 and +21.1% in 2023)
Our post must state the −4.3% figure with its 90-day window attached every time it appears, and may
use the +4.3%/2025, +18.7%/2024, +21.1%/2023 series as the historical trend that is now reversing.
Writing "insurance is down 4.3%" unqualified would be wrong — it is the *filed* average change,
not a change in premiums already being paid.

**Other flags:**
- These are **filings**, which are reviewed by TDI and apply at new business and renewal — not
  automatic cuts to existing policies. Say so explicitly.
- harbertgroup's "+1.0% for the 90 days ending March 2026" is not necessarily wrong — it is a
  different, earlier window. Do not present it as contradicting TDI; the trend simply moved.
- The IIAT FAIR Plan item is the residual market (effective 2026-09-01 for new business and
  renewals) and affects few borrowers. Mention only as a footnote, if at all.
- SKILL.md/CLAUDE.md contain no insurance figures, so nothing in the source docs needs correcting.
