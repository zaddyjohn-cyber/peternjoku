## TRERC (the authority on the notice law)

- H1: "Don't Piddle Around with PID Notices." Kerri Lewis, former TREC general counsel. **Nov 16, 2021.**
- H3s: How Does a PID Differ from a MUD? · New PID Notice Requirements · New TREC Addendum and More ·
  Where to Find Required PID Information · Consequences for Failing to Give the PID Notice · Exemptions ·
  Limits of Liability · Takeaway
- **HB 1543 took effect 2021-09-01.** Prompted by complaints about builder nondisclosure of PID
  assessments from **North Texas homeowners**.
- What HB 1543 changed: PID notice made similar to the MUD notice · **notice must be signed by the
  buyer** · penalties and remedies expanded · coverage extended beyond residential to condominiums,
  commercial, new construction and unimproved property · the §5.014(c) condominium-interest exemption
  removed · the closing copy must be **recorded** · PIDs must file the notice with the city or county on
  their **next annual service plan update**, after which the city or county has **seven days** to file it
  with the county clerk.
- **Remedies if the notice is not given before the contract:** terminate and recover earnest money
  **any time prior to closing**; or, after closing, sue for **all purchase costs plus interest and
  attorney's fees** with reconveyance to the seller; or sue for **damages not to exceed $5,000 plus
  reasonable attorney's fees.** **Notice given only at closing bars the damages suit.**
- Statutes: LGC **Ch. 372 or Ch. 382** (PID formation); Water Code **Ch. 49** (MUD); §5.014(c) exemptions.
- The notice form **does not contain a blank for the assessment amount or any payment schedule** — the
  seller, city or county may add it voluntarily.
- **A PID assessment can be paid up front**, saving interest and administrative expense.
- No tables. No FAQ. No form number. No dollar figures. No lender content.

## GAP SHEET — what our post must cover

1. What a PID is, how it is created (LGC Ch. 372/382, the 50%-of-value-and-50%-of-owners petition, public
   hearing, **no bond election** — unlike a MUD), and what it funds.
2. **The MUD vs PID table**, corrected: Water Code **Ch. 49** carries the MUD *notice* sections (§49.452,
   §49.4521) while **Ch. 54** carries creation and bond authority — LRG and TRERC each cite only one and
   look like they disagree. Also: tax vs special assessment, collection, **lien priority**, early payoff,
   term.
3. **The notice law in full** — §5.014 (the notice and its contents), §5.0141 (before contract execution),
   §5.0143 (recorded at closing), §5.014(c) exemptions, HB 1543 effective **2021-09-01**, the buyer's
   signature requirement, the form (**TREC 53-0 / TXR 1955**, both numbers given), and the fact that the
   form **does not state the amount**.
4. **Both late-notice remedies, correctly distinguished** — §5.0141's seven-day termination window when
   the notice arrives *after* the contract is signed, versus terminate-any-time-before-closing when the
   notice was **never** given; plus the post-closing suits and the $5,000 damages cap, and that notice
   delivered only at closing kills the damages claim.
5. **The lender layer — the whole reason to write this post:**
   - how a PID installment reaches the monthly payment (escrowed with taxes, or billed separately and
     still counted);
   - that it goes into **PITI and therefore into DTI** either way, so it reduces the loan amount you
     qualify for — with a worked dollar figure;
   - where it shows up on the **Loan Estimate and Closing Disclosure** (Section F/G prepaids and initial
     escrow, and the projected payments table);
   - **the year-one escrow shortage trap no competitor mentions:** if the escrow was set from a prior-year
     tax bill that predates the PID levy — common on new construction, where the land was unimproved —
     the first escrow analysis produces a shortage and the payment jumps. Tie to the existing escrow
     shortage post.
6. **A DTI table** showing the same buyer's maximum loan amount with and without a PID installment at

7. Worked PITI math at **7.24%** on a DFW new-construction price point, with the PID line broken out.
8. Prepaying the assessment: allowed, saves interest and admin cost, and what it does (and doesn't do) to
   the monthly payment — and the refinance/sale angle on an unpaid assessment.
9. **Name actual DFW growth corridors** where buyers meet PIDs on new construction (the Prosper, Celina,

## ⚠️ DATA FLAGS

- **HB 1543 is 2021, not 2019.** 87th Legislature, signed 2021-06-04, effective **2021-09-01**; it added
  §§5.0141–5.0145 and amended §5.014. An earlier note in SKILL.md and one search framing said 2019 —
  wrong, corrected. (Research pass flag 5.)
- **Form numbering conflict:** LRG says TREC 53-0; the Texas Realtors legal memo dated 2021-09-01 says
  TXR-1955. Give **both** and do not pick one. (Flag 6.)
- **Late-notice remedy conflict** between LRG (7 days) and TRERC (any time before closing) — these are
  **different fact patterns**, not a contradiction. Explain both. (Flag 7.)
- **MUD chapter conflict** Ch. 54 vs Ch. 49 — both correct about different things. Say so. (Flag 8.)
- **Lien priority** ("first and prior lien except government ad valorem taxes") is **LRG's claim only**,
  not confirmed by TRERC and not checked against LGC Ch. 372. Attribute it, don't assert it.
- **§5.0142 appears on no fetched page** and its content is unknown. Do not cite it.
- **No real DFW PID rates or bond terms are retrievable.** LRG's $1,800/year PID line is its own
  illustration and the $0.80/$100 is a MUD rate. Our post's PID figures must be **labeled as
  illustrative** the same way, and the reader told to get the actual installment from the service and
  assessment plan.
- **TRERC's tax-deductibility claim** for PID payments was not verified and is outside our lane — not used.
- TRERC is five years old and says the service-plan filing process was still unsettled when written.
- The claim that Texas has **no** statutory PID notice requirement (surfaced in search, likely from the
  page that failed DNS) is **contradicted by both fetched pages and by the bill text.** Not used.
- Statute section text was **not** read from statutes.capitol.texas.gov this run; sections come from the
  enrolled bill summary, TRERC and LRG. The post cites them as sections to look up, and tells readers to
  have the title company confirm.
