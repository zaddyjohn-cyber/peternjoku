## Competitor weaknesses to exploit

1. **All still say "appraisal waiver."** Fannie renamed it **value acceptance** (SEL-2025-07);
   Freddie's equivalent is **ACE** (Automated Collateral Evaluation). No competitor page uses
   the current terms, and none mentions the hybrid tier (value acceptance + property data /
   ACE+ PDR). Owning the current vocabulary is a direct relevance win.
2. **Conflicting, stale LTV limits.** SoFi says 80% LTV purchase; others say 90%. Neither
   mentions the 97% inspection-based tier. We can state the tiers cleanly.
3. **No 2026 data.** Nobody cites how often waivers actually happen now.
4. **The Texas 50(a)(6) rule is buried or missing.** Only the refi-specific blog touches it.
   This is the strongest Texas differentiator available.
5. No comparison table anywhere. No FAQ schema. No named scenario.
6. None explains that a waiver removes the buyer's valuation safety net — and what to do instead
   (keep the option period / inspection, which in Texas is a separate paid right).

## GAP SHEET — our post must cover

1. Terminology, current: **value acceptance** (Fannie, renamed from "appraisal waiver" in
   Selling Guide announcement **SEL-2025-07**); **ACE** (Freddie's Automated Collateral
   Evaluation). Freddie's parallel reconsideration-of-value update was **Bulletin 2025-12**.
2. Who decides: the **AUS** (Desktop Underwriter / Loan Product Advisor) offers it; the borrower
   cannot request it, and the **lender may still require an appraisal anyway**.
3. The three valuation tiers, as a TABLE:
   - Full appraisal (interior + exterior, URAR)
   - Value acceptance + property data (Fannie) / ACE+ PDR (Freddie) — third-party property data
     collector, no appraiser opinion of value
   - Value acceptance / ACE — no property visit at all
   Columns: what happens, who visits the property, typical cost to buyer, max LTV, turn time.
4. Eligibility, stated as tiers not a single number: purchase loans on principal residences have
   been expanded to higher CLTV bands (Fannie expanded certain purchases into the **80–90%
   CLTV** band in 2025; the **inspection-based / property-data** route reaches up to **97%
   LTV**). Cash-out refinances on a principal residence are eligible only up to **70% LTV/CLTV**.
   Tell readers the bands move and to confirm with the lender — do not state one hard number.
5. **2026 uptake data** (nobody else has this): combined GSE waiver share reached **28% in
   March 2026**, up from 26% in February, versus a near-**50%** peak in the 2021 refi boom.
   Waivers are concentrated in refinances — about **52%** of Freddie no-cash-out refis and
   **48%** of comparable Fannie transactions used them in March 2026. Property data collections
   are roughly **2–3%** of GSE transactions. (AEI Housing Center data via Appraisal Institute.)
6. **THE TEXAS SECTION — the differentiator.** On a **Texas Section 50(a)(6)** cash-out /
   home-equity refinance, **value acceptance is not available and a new appraisal is required**,
   regardless of what DU returns (Fannie Selling Guide **B5-4.1-01 / B5-4.1-03**: fair market
   value must be based on an appraisal, and the appraisal must be attached to the written
   acknowledgment of fair market value). A Texas **rate-and-term** refinance is different and
   can get value acceptance. This single distinction is what the SERP is missing.
7. Not available on **FHA, VA or USDA** — those have their own valuation rules (FHA appraisal
   validity is **180 days** per ML 2022-11, and the value attaches to the FHA case number).
   Correct the common assumption that "no appraisal" is a government-loan option.
8. Ineligible property/transaction types: $1M+ values, manufactured homes, 2–4 units, new
   construction, unique properties, gift-of-equity and some non-arm's-length deals.
9. What the buyer **gives up** and what to do instead: a waiver saves roughly $550–$800 and
   7–10 days in DFW, but removes the independent valuation check. In Texas the protection is
   the **option period** (a separately paid termination right) plus the inspection — keep both.
10. Payment/cost math: appraisal cost range in DFW, timeline savings, and what overpaying by
    $10,000 on a $350K home at 7.40% actually costs over the loan.
11. Named composite scenario — Plano buyer offered value acceptance, kept the option period,
    and a Rockwall 50(a)(6) borrower who had to get a full appraisal.
12. 4 FAQs with NMLS + phone.

## ⚠️ DATA FLAGS

- **Texas 50(a)(6) appraisal requirement: verified** against Fannie Mae Selling Guide B5-4.1-01
  summaries plus three independent lender product matrices (Plaza, Lakeview, Windsor, Computershare).
  Safe to publish. Cite B5-4.1-01 by name.
- **LTV bands conflict across sources** (80% vs 90% purchase; 97% inspection-based). The 2025
  expansion into 80–90% CLTV and the 97% property-data tier are both reported, but no primary
  2026 Selling Guide text was retrieved. The post presents these as bands that move and tells
  readers to confirm — it must NOT state a single hard maximum as fact.
- Cash-out principal-residence cap of **70% LTV/CLTV** came from Fannie's own value-acceptance
  page excerpt — the most reliable figure in the set. Safe.
- 2026 uptake percentages (28%, 52%, 48%, 2–3%) are **AEI Housing Center** estimates as cited by
  the Appraisal Institute, not GSE-published figures. Attribute them, don't present as official.
- SEL-2025-07 and Bulletin 2025-12 are **2025** documents. Do not imply a 2026 rule change —
  there is no new 2026 appraisal-waiver rule, and the post should say so explicitly rather than
  manufacture news.
- FHA 180-day appraisal validity (ML 2022-11) is already corrected in SKILL.md (the old "90
  days" was wrong). Use 180 days.
