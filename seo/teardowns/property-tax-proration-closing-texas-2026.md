## GAP SHEET (must cover)
1. Who pays what: seller covers Jan 1 through the day before closing; buyer covers closing day
   through Dec 31. Seller pays via a credit to the buyer at closing.
2. Why Texas works this way — taxes are billed in arrears for the calendar year, lien attaches Jan 1.
3. The math, shown explicitly: annual estimate / 365 = daily rate, x seller days = credit. Use a
   DFW example in Peter's own territory (Garland or Rockwall), not Plano.
4. Table 1 — which figure the title company uses by closing window (match competitor, then add that
   an October-or-later closing in 2026 uses the actual bill, which is why Q4 closings are the most
   accurate of the year).
5. Table 2 — proration credit and escrow reserves by DFW county on a $350,000 home: Collin 1.5%,
   Rockwall 1.8%, Denton 1.8%, Kaufman 1.9%, Dallas 2.0%, Tarrant 2.2%. Show annual tax, daily rate,
   and a sample credit for an October 31 closing (303 seller days).
6. The distinction nobody explains from the lender side: proration credit (a credit TO the buyer)
   vs Initial Escrow Payment at Closing (a charge to the buyer). Net them out.
7. Texas tax calendar 2026-2027: bills mail starting Oct 1, 2026, large metro counties Oct 1-15,
   payment due Jan 31, 2027 regardless of arrival date.
8. New-construction trap: land-only assessment year one, improved value year two, and what to tell
   the LO to escrow at. Cite the magnitude with our own DFW numbers.
9. Homestead exemption interaction: filing changes next year's bill, not the proration, and how the
   $140,000 school-district exemption flows into escrow the following year.
10. Loan-program differences: FHA and USDA require escrow; conventional escrow waiver typically
    needs 20% equity; VA in practice escrows.
11. Named composite scenario — Rockwall or Forney buyer closing in late October 2026.
