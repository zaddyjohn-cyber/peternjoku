## Verified facts to build on

**Appraisal:**
- A pool **in good condition** typically raises value; one **needing resurfacing** would likely appraise
  lower. Appraisers typically **overlook above-ground pools** — in-ground is what gets valued.
- Contributory value is routinely far below build cost (standard appraisal practice — present as general
  principle, not a sourced percentage).

**FHA / loan rules:**
- FHA property rules rest on **safety, security and soundness** under HUD Handbook **4000.1**. "Safety"
  = nothing creating an immediate risk of injury or illness.
- **There is no comprehensive FHA list of required repairs** — the appraiser exercises judgment
  (Triple Play presentation on 4000.1).
- A **drained / empty pool** is generally left to appraiser judgment on whether it must be filled and
  tested; in practice an empty pool typically has to be filled before closing. **Blog-level sourcing.**
- **FHA 203(k) can fund repairing or removing an existing pool, but NOT building a new one.**
- Conventional guidelines do not explicitly address pools.

**Insurance / Texas law:**
- TDI: homeowners policies may give limited or no pool liability coverage; umbrella is the usual fix.
- Premiums rise with a pool; code-compliant fencing typically required by the carrier.
- **Texas Health & Safety Code Chapter 757** sets pool enclosure requirements for certain properties;
  **municipal building codes set separate requirements for private residential pools.**
- Companion fact from the same day's research: **TDI's 2026-09-29 clarification** bars declining or
  non-renewing for **age** of home or roof — but **condition** is still fair game, which is exactly how a
  neglected pool enters the picture.

Rate benchmark: PMMS **7.32%**, week of 2026-10-22. $350K at 20% down = $280K loan = **$1,923** P&I.
