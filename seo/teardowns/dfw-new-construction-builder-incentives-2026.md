## Differentiation strategy — the gap this competitor doesn't fill
He never explains how a builder's captive/preferred lender actually underwrites the buydown, how the
buydown cost gets disclosed (seller/builder-paid rate buydowns show on the Closing Disclosure as a
seller concession, subject to loan-type concession limits), or whether using the builder's in-house
lender vs. an independent broker changes your rate/terms/leverage. That's squarely a mortgage
officer's territory and a real gap.

## Verified facts
- Typical DFW builder incentive package: $8,000-$25,000 in value; some (Walsh, Tavolo Park-type
  master-planned communities) offer $25,000-$40,000+ "flex cash"
- 2-1 temporary buydown: rate is 2 points below note rate in year 1, 1 point below in year 2, full
  note rate from year 3 on
- Permanent buydown via discount points: roughly 1% of loan amount per 0.25% permanent rate
  reduction
- September (end of Q3) incentive packages run ~20-40% more generous than mid-quarter, per
  HousingWire's DFW builder-incentive coverage — this gives the post a legitimate "why now" hook
- Seller/builder-paid concessions are capped by loan type: FHA allows up to 6% of purchase price,
  conventional up to 3-9% depending on LTV, VA up to 4% (link/cite these caps accurately, don't
  invent unconfirmed numbers beyond the FHA 6%/conventional tiers already used elsewhere on-site)
