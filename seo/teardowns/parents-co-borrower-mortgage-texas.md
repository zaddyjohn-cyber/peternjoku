## Facts cross-checked (use these)
- This arrangement is called a "non-occupant co-borrower" (sometimes "non-occupying co-borrower").
- FHA: allows non-occupant co-borrowers; FHA defines "family member" broadly (parent, step-parent,
  foster parent, grandparent, sibling, etc.) — this matters because family-member co-borrowers get
  more favorable treatment than a non-relative.
- Conventional (Fannie Mae): allows non-occupant co-borrowers too, but requires the primary
  occupant-borrower's own DTI to still meet certain thresholds in some scenarios (guideline nuance
  — frame as "ask me to check the specific program" rather than overclaiming one number).
- USDA/VA: non-occupant co-borrowers are NOT typically allowed on USDA or VA loans — those programs
  are structured around occupant-borrowers (VA has its own co-borrower/spousal rules, USDA requires
  all borrowers to occupy). This is an important local correction to make since a generic page might
  not clarify it — flag clearly.
- Non-occupant co-borrower's income/debts ARE included in DTI, so it doesn't help if the co-borrower
  already carries a lot of debt.
- TSAHC/DPA-stacked loans: co-borrower/cosigner rules can differ per the DPA program (see
  kb.tsahc.org) — a buyer stacking DPA with parents as non-occupant co-borrowers should confirm
  program-specific rules before assuming FHA's rule applies to the DPA layer too.
