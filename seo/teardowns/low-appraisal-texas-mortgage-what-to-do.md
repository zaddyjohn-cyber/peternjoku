## Facts cross-checked (use these)
- Five standard options when appraisal < contract price: (1) buyer covers the gap in cash,
  (2) seller reduces the price to match appraised value, (3) split the difference, (4) buyer
  restructures financing (bigger down payment reduces the loan-to-appraised-value ratio need, or
  switch loan programs), (5) walk away using an appraisal/financing contingency.
- Texas-specific contract mechanic: the TREC (Third Party Financing Addendum) typically gives the
  buyer a way to terminate and recover earnest money if the property doesn't appraise at or above
  the sales price, PROVIDED that addendum/contingency was included in the contract — this is a real
  Texas-specific detail competitor pages gloss over generically.
- Reconsideration of Value (ROV): buyer's agent/lender can submit additional comps or point out
  factual errors to challenge a low appraisal; this doesn't guarantee a new number but is a
  legitimate formal process, distinct from just "complaining."
- Loan-program nuance: FHA appraisals are tied to the specific property for 4-6 months (FHA "case
  number" follows the property to a new buyer at a lower price if the deal falls through) — this is
  a detail unique to FHA that VA/conventional don't share the same way, worth flagging as a reason
  FHA low-appraisal situations sometimes play out differently in negotiations.
- Switching down payment structure example (80/10/10 or FHA 3.5%) can reduce out-of-pocket cash need
  when the appraisal comes in low, without asking the seller for anything.
