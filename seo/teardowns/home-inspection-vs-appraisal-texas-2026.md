## Verified facts to build on

- **Inspection:** buyer hires the inspector and **owns the report**; buyer arranges and pays directly.
  Typical **$300–$500**, add-ons (radon, mold, termite, sewer line) **$100–$300 each**. Texas source:
  generally **2–4 hours** depending on property size.
- **Appraisal:** the **lender orders** it; the buyer pays, collected at order or rolled into closing costs;
  **the buyer does not choose the appraiser**. Texas guide: **$314–$424** in 2026 (Angi, via neuhausre —
  attribute).
- The appraisal estimates **market value for the lender**; the inspection assesses **physical condition
  for the buyer**. The appraisal gives the buyer **no repair-request lever** the way an inspection does.
- A low appraisal leaves a gap someone must cover.
- Rate benchmark: PMMS **7.32%** 30-yr, week of 2026-10-22.

## GAP SHEET — what our post must cover

Cover (competitors have these):
1. Side-by-side definition: who orders, who pays, who receives the report, what each measures.
2. Cost ranges and duration for both, with sources.
3. That the appraisal is for the lender, not the buyer.
4. What a low appraisal does to the deal.
5. Whether you can skip either one.

Add (nobody in this SERP has these):
6. **The core gap — what the underwriter actually receives.** The appraisal goes in the loan file; **the
   inspection report does not.** Buyers routinely assume the lender "knows" about the inspection findings.
   It does not, with one big exception (below). No ranking page says this.
7. **The exception that matters — FHA and VA appraisers double as a minimum-property-standards check.**
   Peeling paint on a pre-1978 home, exposed wiring, a non-functioning HVAC, an active roof leak: the
   *appraiser* flags these, and then they become repair conditions that must be cleared before closing.
   This is where the two documents overlap and where Texas buyers get surprised. Conventional appraisals
   mostly do not work this way.
8. **Where each one sits in the Texas contract** — the inspection lives in the **option period** (that is
   the only window where it is real leverage, and the fee is what buys it); the appraisal lives under the
   **Third Party Financing Addendum's Property Approval**, which survives to the third day before closing.
   Two different clocks, two different remedies. The entire national SERP is blind to this.
9. **Loan-program comparison table** — FHA / VA / USDA / conventional on appraisal validity period, MPR
   repair authority, and who can pay for repairs.
10. **Timeline in days** — option period inspection day 1–7, appraisal ordered after Buyer Approval,
    typical return window, re-inspection fee if repairs are required.
11. **Named composite scenario** — Mesquite buyer, $285K, FHA, inspection finds items the seller fixes
    during the option period, appraiser separately calls out a safety item; $1,958 P&I at 7.32%.
12. 4 FAQs, first person, NMLS + phone.
