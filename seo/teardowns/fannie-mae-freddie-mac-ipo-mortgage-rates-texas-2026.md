## Verified facts for our post (WebSearch 2026-09-29)
- The GSEs remain in conservatorship, entered September 2008; government holds 79.9% of each.
- An IPO has been repeatedly teased, most recently within the past month, but has NOT happened and
  the timeline is unsettled.
- Reported complication: Bill Pulte, the FHFA housing chief running the IPO effort, was given the
  additional role of Director of National Intelligence, which has led observers to doubt the effort
  will land on schedule (CNN, via MPA coverage).
- Bill Ackman has publicly warned against a rushed IPO.
- Mark Zandi (Chief Economist, Moody's Analytics) estimates privatization could cost a typical new
  mortgage borrower **$1,800 to $2,800 per year, roughly $150 to $230 per month**.
- Context rate: Freddie Mac PMMS 30-year fixed = **7.03%** and 15-year = **6.42%**, week ending
  Sept 23, released Sept 24, 2026 (verified directly at freddiemac.com/pmms).

## GAP SHEET (must cover)
1. Plain-language explanation of what conservatorship is and what an IPO would change — 79.9%
   government stake, since September 2008, GSEs are 57.4% of outstanding residential mortgages.
2. The honest status line: nothing has changed for a borrower today. No IPO has occurred. This is
   the contrarian, trust-building lead, same voice that works on the refinance post.
3. Why the market cares: the implicit government guarantee is what keeps conforming rates low; a
   private guarantor has to price that risk, which flows into g-fees and then into rates.
4. **Table 1 — the Zandi estimate on real DFW loan sizes.** Apply $150-$230/month to actual DFW
   price points: $250K Terrell, $300K Garland, $350K Rockwall, $450K Prosper, $600K Flower Mound.
   Show current payment at 7.03% vs the same loan with the estimated add-on.
5. **Table 2 — what would and would not change**: conforming loans (affected), FHA (not a GSE —
   unaffected), VA (unaffected), USDA (unaffected), jumbo (already private). This is the single
   most useful thing for a DFW buyer and no competitor has it.
6. Timeline and uncertainty section: IPO teased but not scheduled; Pulte's dual role; Ackman's
   warning; valuation talk near $500B. Label all of it clearly as unresolved.
7. What a buyer under contract right now should do — lock decisions are driven by the 10-year
   Treasury and today's 7.03% PMMS, not by IPO headlines. Do not delay a purchase over this.
