Why Prosper is different

Prosper is not a typical Collin County market

Most of my Prosper buyers are moving up from Frisco, Plano or Allen, and they arrive expecting the financing to work the same way. It often doesn't. Prosper's price points sit higher, a real share of the inventory crosses the conforming loan limit into jumbo territory, and the MUD and PID assessments attached to several master-planned communities can move a monthly payment by hundreds of dollars — a difference that never shows up in the listing price.

Almost everything here is new construction too, which changes the timeline. Builder contracts, extended rate locks and completion dates six to nine months out all need planning at contract signing, not at closing. Read this before you sign

MUD, PID and what Prosper property taxes really cost

This is the single most common surprise for buyers moving to Prosper, and it is worth understanding before you tour a model home.

Prosper property tax rates generally run 2.0%–2.7% of assessed value annually. That spread looks small written down. It is not. Many of Prosper's newer master-planned communities sit inside a Municipal Utility District (MUD) or Public Improvement District (PID) — mechanisms that fund the roads, water infrastructure and amenities that made the community attractive in the first place. The assessment sits on top of the base tax rate.

On a $700,000 home, the difference between 2.0% and 2.7% is roughly $4,900 per year, or about $408 per month added through escrow. That is a real change to what you can afford, and it does not appear anywhere in the listing price or the builder's brochure.

None of this makes a MUD or PID community a bad buy — the amenities are usually why people choose Prosper. But you should know the number before you commit, not at closing. I check it for every Prosper file I work on and build it into your payment estimate from the start. Prosper also spans both Collin and Denton counties, so file your Texas Homestead Exemption with the correct appraisal district after closing.

New construction

Should you use the builder's lender?

Almost every Prosper builder offers an incentive for using their preferred lender — usually a closing cost credit, sometimes a rate buydown. Buyers often assume that settles it. It doesn't, and it's worth doing the arithmetic.

Sometimes the builder's offer genuinely is better, and when it is I'll say so plainly. But a $10,000 closing credit paired with a rate a quarter-point higher can cost you considerably more across the years you actually hold the loan. The only way to know is to compare the same scenario twice.

Three other things worth planning for on a Prosper new build:

  • Long build timelines change your rate lock. A home completing in six to nine months may need an extended lock, which carries a cost. We should discuss lock strategy at contract signing, not two weeks before closing.
  • Your approval has to survive the build. New debt, a job change or a fresh credit pull between contract and closing can undo an approval that was solid on day one. Don't finance a car mid-build.
  • Outside lenders are accepted. Prosper builders routinely work with outside pre-approval letters. You are not obligated to use theirs to buy the home.
