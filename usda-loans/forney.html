Eligibility

Is your Forney address actually USDA-eligible?

Here is the honest position, and it is more nuanced than most pages will tell you: much of the Forney area qualifies, but not all of it, and the eligible footprint has been shrinking.

USDA eligibility is drawn from population and urban-classification data. Forney has been one of the fastest-growing small cities in Texas for over a decade, and as the developed area expands, previously eligible pockets get reclassified. The result is that eligibility here is genuinely address-specific — a home two streets from an eligible property may not qualify. Outlying addresses toward the county line are the most reliably eligible; areas closest to the Mesquite side are the most likely to have dropped out.

This is why I check the exact address before we build a strategy around USDA, and why you should be wary of any lender who tells you “Forney is eligible” without looking up your specific property.

To check an address yourself: go to eligibility.sc.egov.usda.gov, choose “Single Family Housing Guaranteed,” enter the full street address, and the map returns eligible or ineligible immediately. If it comes back ineligible, do not give up on zero down — depending on your situation a VA loan, or an FHA loan paired with TSAHC down payment assistance, can get you close to the same outcome.

Timeline

What a Forney USDA closing actually looks like

USDA files take 30–45 days, longer than a conventional or FHA purchase, and the reason is structural rather than a sign that anything is wrong: after I underwrite and approve your loan, the file goes to USDA's own state office for a final review before it can close. That step is not optional and cannot be rushed.

The practical consequence is that your contract dates need to reflect it. A 30-day close written into a Forney contract on a USDA file is optimistic; 45 days is realistic and gives you room. I have seen buyers put earnest money at risk because a lender unfamiliar with the program agreed to a timeline the program cannot support.

  1. Address and income check — before anything else, usually while we are on the first call
  2. Documents — 2 years W-2s, 30 days paystubs, 2 months bank statements, ID
  3. Pre-approval letter — typically 24–48 business hours, usable with any Forney Realtor
  4. Under contract — appraisal ordered, underwriting begins
  5. USDA state review — the step that sets the timeline, typically several business days
  6. Clear to close — final figures confirmed and closing scheduled

One warning that applies throughout: do not open new credit or finance a car between pre-approval and closing. It is the most common way a solid USDA file falls apart.
